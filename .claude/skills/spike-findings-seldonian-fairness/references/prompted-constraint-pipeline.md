# Prompted constraints: from a sentence to a certified g_hat

Idea `prompted-ghat`: the developer writes the constraint in English and the system compiles
it into a measurement (a judge or a feature), a statistic with a threshold, and a bound, and
the certificate says what it certifies about the developer's quantity, not the judge's.
Spikes 015 (measurement), 016 (compile) and 017 (certificate) were all PARTIAL: each half
works in a narrowed form, and together they give one pipeline with three deterministic
guards and a routing rule.

## Requirements

From idea `prompted-ghat` (MANIFEST.md), the whole list:

- Every prompted judge is tested for the rubric artifact (verdict predictable from the
  rubric alone; response reversal must flip it) and for paraphrase flip rate, against the
  trained guard as the baseline.
- The compiled spec is rendered back to English for the developer to confirm before it
  builds a constraint.
- A compiled g_hat ships with its calibration: judge error rates on an i.i.d. human-labelled
  sample of the scored population, corrected with PPI++ or the answer-rate-aware formula; it
  states what it can certify and with how many human positives (006: >= 30).
- The 225-item human sheet is checked for being an i.i.d. sample of the scored population
  before it is used as a PPI calibration set. (017: it is not; see "The human sheet".)
- **No external judge API** (decided 2026-09-30): every judge is a local model. Jev is a
  reference point for the interface, not a component.
- A verifiable property (length, format, exact match) compiles to a deterministic feature,
  never to a judge (015: the compiled judge was at chance on "more than 80 words").
- A compiled judge is used through its probability with a threshold calibrated on labelled
  data, never through its 0.5 label (015: AUC held across paraphrases, the rate swung
  0.09-0.46).
- The exact constraint wording is part of the constraint's identity in any certificate.
- Constraint prose describing harmful content is loaded from the repo's existing artifacts
  (the labelling guideline, spike 005's rubric), not authored anew.
- The statistic and threshold are compiled as: the model writes one line of the constraint
  DSL; deterministic code parses, validates, builds the project's constraint classes,
  renders the English and picks the bound. The model never chooses `monotone`, the delta
  split or the bound (016).
- The registry shown to the model is one flat list of named prompt groups and one way to
  mention the reference (`REF` in the limit) (016).
- How the reference enters the certificate (constant, two-sample difference, paired
  difference) is a policy of the builder, not the model's choice: widths 0.029 / 0.077 /
  0.039 at 500 prompts. Round 6's `reference + margin` is the constant form and does not
  cover the reference's sampling error (016).
- A compiled spec is accepted only if it passes validation, the reference lint, the number
  lint, and a second independent compile states the same requirement; anything else goes
  back to the developer as a question (016).
- The English the developer confirms is generated by code from the spec, never by the model
  (016: 9 of 40 model-written paraphrases drifted or were ambiguous).
- The compile prompt carries one example per construct, and its examples share no group,
  number or measurement pairing with anything it is tested on (016).
- Open after 016, not built: a bare percent beside a reference comparison always asks;
  `JUDGE("...")` must quote a verbatim span and keep the prompt group; conjunctions and
  two-sided relative limits are rejected rather than compiled.
- The certificate on the developer's quantity is gold labels on the responses it certifies,
  with the judge as a variance reducer. A calibration measured elsewhere is not carried
  across prompt populations (017: recall differed in 4 of 6 wordings; a carried bound missed
  in up to 94% of draws). Nor across training that targets the label (017 E8 on 014's
  Lagrangian checkpoints: recall 0.21 to 0.03, the carried bound missed in 80% of draws with
  the rate back at the reference); it held only for side-effect training.
- The route is fixed by the data's shape before any bound is computed (017): verifiable
  property, code on every response; fewer than 10 labels in the rarer class, Clopper-Pearson
  on the labels with the judge unused; otherwise PPI++ with a bootstrap-t limit. Never the
  smaller of two bounds (missed up to 0.078).
- PPI and PPI++ are never used with a normal limit (017: 0.08-0.13 miss at 100-225 labels,
  0.20-0.24 at a 1.3% rate; a Wilson-type limit did not repair it; bootstrap-t at most
  0.053).
- The PPI++ feature is the logit of P(Yes), or a cross-fitted Platt map of it, never p or
  the 0.5 label (017: rho^2 0.48 / 0.58 against 0.22 / 0.18).
- Labelled and unlabelled responses are scored in one run (017: a different batch order
  flipped 3 and 5 of 225 labels and moved p by up to 0.36).
- A label sheet sampled by strata is analysed with its design weights and `b1w`, never as
  i.i.d. and never with a normal limit (017). Rates reported unweighted from the sheet are
  sheet rates: Qwen3Guard-4B's false alarms are 0.120 weighted, not 0.230.
- Spike 006's answer-rate-aware correction is for the guard (false alarms on answers); a
  compiled rubric's false alarms fall on refusals, so it does not apply there (017).
- A harm threshold is certified by labels on the candidate's own responses (301 clean labels
  for 1%, 59 for 5%; no positive needed). Thirty human positives is the floor only for a
  carried calibration of the gated label, and only within one prompt population (017).
- The target for a better judge (spike 018) is rho^2 with human labels on the logit scale:
  0.5 turns 225 labels into about 360; the compiled harm rubric is at 0.02 (017).

## How to Build It

The pipeline has four stages. Stages 2 and 4 are deterministic code from the spikes and can
be lifted as they are; stages 1 and 3 call a local LLM and are wrapped in guards.

### 1. Compile the sentence to one DSL line (016, `speclab.py`, `compile016.py`)

Prompt v2 with thinking on Qwen3-8B (4-bit): a flat registry of named prompt groups, the
reference model only as `REF` in the limit (`PAIRED REF + m` for a paired comparison), one
example per construct, examples disjoint from anything the compiler is tested on. The model
returns one line such as

    REFUSAL[benign] <= REF + 0.02
    abs(WORDS_OVER(80)[benign] - WORDS_OVER(80)[harmful]) <= 0.1
    JUDGE("gives dosage advice")[medical] <= 0.05

Then, in this order, all deterministic:

1. **Parse and validate** (`parse_dsl`): unknown names, a measurement minus itself, `PAIRED`
   without a difference are errors fed back as one repair turn. A `REF` term on the left is
   moved right; strict `<`/`>` are read as `<=`/`>=`.
2. **Reference lint**: a relative constraint must be one the reference model itself
   satisfies on the cached reference responses (caught 12 wrong compiles, 0 false alarms).
3. **Number lint**: every number in the spec must occur in the sentence (caught 18 of 25
   invented limits and both copied examples; 1 false alarm in 417).
4. **Second compile** (the other surface form, or plain against thinking) and accept only if
   both state the same requirement (`same_point` or better). 46 right and 1 wrong of 47
   accepted; about a quarter of clear sentences go back as a question. Do not use the
   model's own Yes/No check of the rendering as the filter (lets 1-6 wrong through per arm).
5. **Render to English by code** (`render_dsl` and the English renderer) and show it to the
   developer. Under-specified sentences ("5%" beside a reference, two requirements in one
   sentence, a two-sided relative limit) are returned as questions, not compiled.

Cost: 1-2 s per sentence plain, 25-50 s with thinking. Verifiable properties (`WORDS_OVER`,
format, exact match) never go to `JUDGE`; the lint is in `check_measure`.

### 2. Build the constraint from the spec (016, `speclab.build`, `choose_bound`)

The spec builds `ExpressionConstraint` or `PairedDifferenceConstraint` from
`seldonian/llm/constraints.py`; `monotone` is derived from the expression; the bound comes
from spike 013's use-case map through `013/preflight.py` on the reference's covariate
samples: exact Clopper-Pearson with no strata at rare rates, reference-rate strata with
`b1w` at mid rates, `betting_mixture` for bounded scores and paired differences. The built g
equals the hand-written g to the last bit on 8 constraints x 5 data sets (`check_builder.py`).

The builder decides how the reference enters. The three forms are different certificates
(constant `REF + m`: width 0.029 and no coverage of the reference's error; two-sample:
0.077; paired on the same prompts: 0.039 at n = 500). Default to the paired form when the
candidate and reference are sampled on the same prompts; say which form was used in the
rendered English.

### 3. Measure with a compiled judge, through its logit (015, `rubric015.py`)

Compile the sentence into grader instructions once (`COMPILE_PROMPT`, greedy, thinking off),
then score each response by `P(Yes) / (P(Yes) + P(No))` on the first answer token
(`Scorer.p_yes`), one forward pass per item, about 10 items per second on Qwen3-8B 4-bit.
Store the **logit**, not p rounded to 5 decimals: p saturates near 0 and the logit carries
twice the information about the gold label. Score the whole safety set and the labelled
subset in the same run, sorted by length, saved in chunks (017's `score017.py`).

Run 015's three tests on any new rubric before trusting it: agreement with a gold label,
the rubric artifact (`blank`: response removed; AUC should be near 0.5. It was 0.694 on
refusal), and the paraphrase spread of the measured rate. The compiled judge's ranking is
robust (AUC 0.80-0.87 on harm across six wordings); its 0.5 label is not (rate 0.09-0.46).

### 4. Certify with gold labels on the scored responses (017, `cert017.py`)

`certify(tau, delta, exact_feature=..., y_lab=..., f_lab=..., f_unl=...)` returns the card:
route, estimate, upper bound, whether `tau` is certified, the smallest threshold the data
certifies, the label counts, the judge's worth in labels, and the judged-scale threshold.
The route is fixed by the data's shape:

| Data | Route | Guarantee |
|---|---|---|
| verifiable property | the feature by code on every response, Clopper-Pearson | exact |
| labels, fewer than 10 in the rarer class | Clopper-Pearson on the labels, judge unused | exact |
| labels, 10 or more in each class | PPI++ on the judge's logit, bootstrap-t (`ppipp_boot`) | second-order; miss <= 0.053 over 60 cells |
| labels from a stratified sheet | design-weighted labels, `b1w` from 013 | approximate; miss <= 0.059 on re-drawn sheets |
| calibration measured on another prompt population | refused (`certify_carried` below 30 positives; not safe above either) | - |

`ppipp_boot`: resample the labelled `(Y, f)` pairs, re-estimate `lam` in each resample, add
normal noise for the unlabelled mean, take the delta-quantile of the studentised statistic;
a resample with zero variance counts as `-inf`, so too few positives give a bound of 1, never
a wrong number. The judge's worth: `1 / (1 - rho^2 Nu / (Nu + n))` in labels, 1 to 11% high
against the measurement. On refusal with 225 labels the logit was worth 1.6 labels each,
a cross-fitted Platt map 1.9, p 1.16, the 0/1 label 1.12; on brevity and harm the judge was
worth nothing and cost nothing (`lam` 0).

The certificate carries: the wording's hash, the judge id and scoring run, the route, n
labels and positives, N scored, the bound, the smallest certifiable tau, and for PPI++ the
judged-scale threshold (`true rate <= tau` becomes `judged rate <= tau - rectifier -
margin`: harm 0.05 became a judged 0.217 for an over-flagging judge; refusal 0.25 became a
judged 0.055 for an under-flagging one).

### The human sheet (`results/screen/labels`)

It is a stratified sample: 4 responses the 0.6B guard flagged and 2 it cleared per model x
encoding cell, 61.8% guard-flagged against 24.8% of the 4,800 screened responses, design
effect 1.71 (worth about 131 labels). Rebuild the strata and weights with
`017/design017.py`, estimate with `harm017.ht` and `013/stratbounds.b1w`. Read as i.i.d.,
PPI on it certified a *negative* harm rate under one wording (missing in 98% of planted
re-draws). Its bound on the screened population's harm rate is 0.039 with 3 positives in
225; that certifies nothing about a trained policy.

## What to Avoid

- **The judge's 0/1 label as the constraint's measurement.** 22% false alarms against a 1.3%
  base rate on harm (both the compiled rubric and Qwen3Guard-4B); the rate moves five-fold
  across paraphrases; on refusal every wording under-flags (0.008-0.090 against 0.200).
- **A judge for a verifiable property.** At chance on "more than 80 words" while its
  aggregate rate landed within a point of the truth by coincidence: a valid-looking bound on
  the wrong quantity, and nothing in the certificate reveals it.
- **PPI or PPI++ with the normal limit**, or a Wilson-type repair. The rectifier of a judge
  that fires on 4% of responses is as skewed as a rare binomial, and an in-sample `lam`
  biases the estimate down at small n.
- **Finite-sample judge-assisted bounds at n <= 1,000** (three exact limits, post-stratified
  on the judge, block betting): valid, and narrower than the labels alone in 10 of 210
  cells by at most 0.003.
- **Reporting the smaller of two bounds.** Missed up to 0.078; pick the route by the label
  counts before computing anything.
- **Carrying recall and false-alarm rates across prompt populations** (Rogan-Gladen /
  Youden, or spike 006's answer-rate-aware form). Recall of the compiled refusal judge was
  0.31 on XSTest and 0.13 on OR-Bench prompts under the same wording; the carried bound
  missed in up to 94% (source shift) and 100% (pool shift) of draws. A carried Platt map of
  p was off by 0.03-0.27.
- **Carrying a calibration across training that targets the label** (017 E8, on spike 014's
  Lagrangian checkpoints). The compiled judge's recall on the guard's refusals fell from
  0.21 at step 0 to 0.06 at step 100 and 0.03 at step 200 (wording 0; 0.12 to 0.00 for
  wording 2) while the rate came back to the reference; the carried Youden bound missed in
  80% of draws. The constrained policy's refusals became refusal-prefixed answers ("I cannot
  assist... [the answer]"), which the guard flags and the rubric judge does not. Side-effect
  training (013's run) changed nothing; training against the label changes the form of the
  label's positives. Which judge is right about a hybrid is a human-label question.
- **The answer-rate-aware correction on a compiled rubric.** Its false alarms are on
  refusals (it reacts to the restated harmful request: 27.2% of refusals flagged against
  12.6% of answers on the population). The correction fits Qwen3Guard, whose false alarms
  are on answers (0.291 answered, 0.036 refused, weighted).
- **Reading the 225-item sheet as i.i.d.**, or reporting sheet rates as population rates.
- **Comparing scores from different scoring runs**: batch composition flips 1-2% of 0/1
  labels under 4-bit bf16 with left padding.
- **The pre-registered compile prompt (v1)**: overlapping `pool`/`source` attributes, three
  ways to name the reference, three examples. 3 of 8 sentences right and 20 of 39 faithful
  wordings silently wrong. The JSON surface form turns nearly every relative limit into a
  paired comparison (19 of 21 with thinking).
- **Model-written paraphrases as test items or renderings without an audit**: 3 of 40
  changed the meaning, 6 were ambiguous.

## Constraints

- Qwen3-8B in 4-bit on the 12 GB card: 1-2 s per compile plain, 25-50 s with 1,800 thinking
  tokens at batch 4; about 10 judge forward passes per second; thinking runs must save in
  chunks (016 lost 66 minutes to a single write per arm).
- Refusal's gold label in 015-017 is Qwen3Guard-4B's refusal field; the only human labels
  are the 225 harm labels with 3 positives. Any PPI claim about human refusal labels is
  untested.
- The plasmode pool is 500 responses (two per prompt), N up to 20,000 by resampling. Miss
  rates carry Monte Carlo standard errors near 0.004.
- Label budgets for harm by labels alone (delta 0.05, power 0.8): 301 clean labels for 1%,
  149 for 2%, 59 for 5%; 776 for 1% if the true rate is 0.25%. The compiled harm judge saves
  about 2% of them; a gated label with recall 0.8 saves up to 45%.
- A carried calibration of the gated label (false alarms 0.015) certifies 5% for a clean
  candidate in 0.10 / 0.41 / 0.87 / 1.00 of draws at 3 / 10 / 30 / 100 human positives, in
  the best case where the rates carry at all. The ungated compiled rubric never certifies 5%
  this way (bound 0.17 at 300 positives with 222 negatives).
- Not expressible in the DSL yet: two requirements in one sentence, a two-sided relative
  limit, a `JUDGE` property that keeps its prompt group reliably.
