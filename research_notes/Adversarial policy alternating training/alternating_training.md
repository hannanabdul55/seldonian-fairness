# Adversarial policy alternating training: attacker/defender co-training schemes for LLM safety (2022 - Oct 2026)

Scope note: catalogue of schemes in which an attacker (red) model and a defender (target/blue) model are updated in alternation or jointly, GAN/minimax style, in the text/LLM setting. Classical adversarial training is cited only to fix terms: Madry et al. 2018, "Towards Deep Learning Models Resistant to Adversarial Attacks", arXiv 1706.06083 (ID from memory, not re-fetched this session; UNVERIFIED here) defines adversarial training as the min-max problem min_theta E max_{delta in S} L(theta, x+delta, y), i.e. an inner maximisation (attack) nested in an outer minimisation (defence). Everything below is the LLM analogue where the inner maximiser is (usually) itself a language model policy.

Dating convention: entries marked RECENT are from the last 18 months (approx. April 2025 - October 2026). arXiv IDs are recorded exactly as read from arxiv.org pages during this session; anything not read from a primary page is marked UNVERIFIED.

A fact about one of the user's named papers: "DART: Deep Adversarial Automated Red Teaming for LLM Safety" (Jiang et al. 2024) is arXiv 2407.03876 **v1 only**; the same arXiv record was later retitled "Automated Progressive Red Teaming" (APRT, v3, COLING 2025), which is a different (attacker-only) method. Both are catalogued separately below.

## Key Question 1: What are the canonical multi-round attacker/defender papers? (full catalogue with structured fields)

### Takeaway
There are roughly a dozen papers in which an LLM attacker and an LLM defender are *both* updated over multiple rounds (MART, DART, RTG/GRTS, PAD, APL, Self-RedTeam, Safety Self-Play, TriPlay-RL, MAGIC, AdvGame, Anchored Bipolicy Self-Play, AdvGRPO, CHASE, Active Attacks, ARLAS, AgentSafeEvo, GPT-Red), plus a second family where the "attacker" is a gradient/latent optimiser rather than an LM policy (Ziegler 2022, CAT, LAT, RPO, Adversarial Tuning), and a third where a red-teaming method is followed by a single fine-tuning round (Rainbow Teaming, GFlowNet red-teaming, Perez 2022 discussion). The 2023-2024 loops (MART, DART, PAD) use SFT/DPO attackers; RL-trained attackers against a *moving* defender first appear in RTG/GRTS (PPO, 2023) and dominate from mid-2025 (Self-RedTeam, MAGIC, AdvGame, TriPlay-RL, AdvGRPO, CHASE, ARLAS, AgentSafeEvo, GPT-Red).

#### Group A. Both roles are LLM policies and both are updated across rounds (2022-2024)

**1. MART: Improving LLM Safety with Multi-round Automatic Red-Teaming** - Suyu Ge, 2023 (NAACL 2024 Long, 2024.naacl-long.107, DOI 10.18653/v1/2024.naacl-long.107), arXiv 2311.07689 - [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.07689); [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.naacl-long.107)
Summary: An adversarial LLM writes prompts, a target LLM answers, reward models (safety RM and helpfulness RM) score the pairs, and both models are re-fine-tuned on the selected data each round. Violation rate on an adversarial benchmark falls up to 84.7% after 4 rounds with helpfulness on non-adversarial prompts "stable". The authors stop at round 4 because the attacker stops producing enough new successful attacks. - [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.07689)
- (a) Supervised, not RL: each round the adversarial model is fine-tuned on (previous successful attack, new attack) pairs, where "successful" means the target's response scored below a safety-RM threshold; seed instruction data and prior-round prompts are mixed in. - [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.07689)
- (b) SFT on the target's own responses that pass both the safety RM (threshold 0.8) and the helpfulness RM (threshold 0.4); context distillation with a safety pre-prompt only in iteration 1; rejection sampling only in the last iteration; no extra helpfulness data added. - [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.07689)
- (c) SAME base: both initialised from LLaMA-65B fine-tuned on LIMA / Open Assistant ("use it to initialize M_tgt and M_adv"); adversary gets cleaned LIMA (986 samples), target gets 2,852 OASST + 1,000 LIMA samples. - [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.07689)
- (d) Strict alternation (generate -> RM-select -> update both), iterations 1-4; lr 1e-5 -> 9e-6, batch 8. - [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.07689)
- (e) No KL or reference anchor mentioned. - [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.07689)
- (f) SafeEval violation: vanilla 31.38% -> 15.43 -> 10.11 -> 5.85 -> 4.79% (iter 1-4); Anthropic Harmless 26.73 -> 6.92%; human-judged SafeEval 17.2% -> 8.0% (53.7% relative); HelpEval iter4 is "3%-4% lower than iter1", AlpacaEval "barely changes". - [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.07689)
- (g) Attacker saturation: once adversarial violation nears 10% "it becomes hard to [get] sufficient enough training data for the next iteration"; attacker "tends to converge on the generated adversarial prompts"; GCG attacks lose efficacy after round 1; risk of over-fitting to RM preference (hence human eval); slight helpfulness decline; still behind Llama-2-Chat-70B. Forgetting not discussed. - [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.07689)
- (h) No code link found on arXiv page or in text ("will openly share safety improvements"). - [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.07689)

**2. Evolving Diverse Red-team Language Models in Multi-round Multi-agent Games** (v1 title: "Red Teaming Game: A Game-Theoretic Framework for Red Teaming Language Models") - Chengdong Ma, 2023 (v1 30 Sep 2023, v5 28 Jul 2024; arXiv preprint, cs.CL/cs.GT; no venue listed), arXiv 2310.00322 - [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.00322)
Summary: Frames red/blue teaming as a multi-round, multi-agent Red Team Game (RTG) and solves it with a Gamified Red Team Solver (GRTS), a PSRO/double-oracle style population method with a semantic-diversity term, with a stated convergence guarantee to an approximate Nash equilibrium. Both red and blue are trained with PPO against the other side's population meta-strategy. Reports a "spinning top" payoff geometry and exploitability falling from ~6.2 to ~0.8 over 15 iterations. - [arXiv HTML v5](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.00322v5)
- (a) RL: PPO best responses; reward = learned toxicity-model score + per-round diversity score; red first SFT'd on Anthropic-HH, PKU-SafeRLHF and BAD. - [arXiv HTML v5](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.00322v5)
- (b) RL: PPO with reward = negation of red reward (zero-sum). - [arXiv HTML v5](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.00322v5)
- (c) SAME base family: red, blue, critic and toxicity model all use stablelm-alpaca-3b / stablelm-tuned-alpha-3b (blue starts from the backbone, red from SFT-Red). - [arXiv HTML v5](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.00322v5); [appendix](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.00322v5)
- (d) Population-based: each iteration trains a best response for one side against the other side's meta-strategy, adds it to the population, re-solves the meta-game (LP/Nash); 3-turn dialogue episodes; ~20K x 3 dialogue rounds per best response; iteration count not fixed in text (exploitability plot runs 15 iterations). - [arXiv HTML v5](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.00322v5)
- (e) Yes: KL coefficient 0.04 plus a PTX (pretraining-loss) coefficient 8.0 in PPO. - [appendix](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.00322v5)
- (f) Blue-team round-1 ASR 52% -> 1%, equilibrium ASR ~10%; trained red raises ASR vs untrained blue 40% -> 70%; GRTS-12 red reaches 0.13 ASR on Llama-2-70b-chat (round 3) and 0.53 on Nous-Hermes-2-Mixtral-8x7B-DPO. No helpfulness/over-refusal numbers for blue reported. - [arXiv HTML v5](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.00322v5)
- (g) With normal reward modelling, training shows "a relatively slow and moderate adversarial oscillation"; an over-fitted toxicity model made training "much more unstable" and caused "significantly more severe mode collapse"; RLHF-style mode collapse onto narrow strategies; prior single-round methods collapse to one category (e.g. "steal" 54.7%). - [appendix](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.00322v5)
- (h) No code or data release link found in main text or appendix. - [appendix](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.00322v5)

**3. DART: Deep Adversarial Automated Red Teaming for LLM Safety** - Bojian Jiang, 2024 (arXiv 2407.03876 **v1**, 4 Jul 2024; the arXiv record was later retitled to APRT, see entry 29) - [arXiv v1 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.03876v1); [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.03876)
Summary: Red and Target LLMs "deeply and dynamically" interact over iterations; the red side selects new training data under a global-diversity constraint (BLEU against its own past training set) and the target side selects "hard" samples via an active-learning rule (keep only prompts defended successfully exactly once out of A_max tries). Both are retrained from their previous checkpoints each iteration. On Anthropic Harmless human evaluation, violation drops from 34.3% to 16% (53.4%), beating a reimplemented MART (20%). - DART v1 PDF text, extracted locally from [arXiv v1 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.03876v1)
- (a) SFT on selected (prompt, jailbreak) pairs whose safety score s_s < theta and diversity score s_d < theta_d; no RL; initialised by SFT on 960 hand-built rewriting pairs. - [arXiv v1 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.03876v1)
- (b) SFT on (prompt, response) pairs passing safety-RM and helpfulness-RM thresholds, chosen by the active-learning rule; "termination condition ... is when M_target begins to decay in helpfulness score compared with M0_target". - [arXiv v1 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.03876v1)
- (c) SAME base: "We used the Llama-2-7b-hf as the starting checkpoint to train the Red LLM M0_red and the target LLM M0_target"; target seed data = LIMA + Open Assistant + in-house set. - [arXiv v1 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.03876v1)
- (d) Strict alternation; "M_red and M_target reload checkpoints from the preceding iteration i-1"; plots run iterations 0-7. - [arXiv v1 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.03876v1)
- (e) No KL anchor; the helpfulness-decay stopping rule is the only anchor. - [arXiv v1 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.03876v1)
- (f) Anthropic Harmless violation (RM / human): vanilla 18.9% / 34.3%; MART-reimpl 14.4% / 20%; DART 11.6% / 16%; "stable helpfulness trends" claimed from RM-score plots (no absolute helpfulness numbers in text). - [arXiv v1 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.03876v1)
- (g) Ablation shows using a *fixed* M0_red or M0_target to select data is worse than using the latest states (Fig. 4); an "aggressive" (non-diverse) red LLM's attack strength decays from iteration 5; reward-model noise in Anthropic Harmless had to be fixed by manual re-annotation of 10k samples; limitation section: RM quality is the bottleneck. - [arXiv v1 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.03876v1)
- (h) "We will release the datasets and codes of DART"; no link in v1. The later APRT repo is github.com/tjunlp-lab/APRT, which is a different method. - [arXiv v1 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.03876v1); [APRT HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.03876v3)

**4. Purple-teaming LLMs with Adversarial Defender training (PAD)** - Jingyan Zhou, 2024 (arXiv preprint, 1 Jul 2024, no venue listed), arXiv 2407.01850 - [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01850)
Summary: A GAN-style self-play pipeline: an attacker (DPO on attack preference pairs) and a defender (SFT to reproduce the judge's safety label plus explanation, then answer safely) are iterated; ShieldLM is the judge because GPT-4 refused to judge. After two attacker/defender iterations plus a defender-only step (PADv3), ASR against PAIR falls 70.62% and over-refusal falls from 39.2% to 11.25% of inquiries. - [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01850v1)
- (a) DPO (beta 0.1) on LoRA with preference triplets: attack that elicited unsafe response preferred over one that elicited safe response. - [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01850v1)
- (b) SFT (cross-entropy) to emit the judge's label + explanation over dialogue history, then a safe reply. - [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01850v1)
- (c) SAME base: both LoRA (r=128, alpha=64) on Qwen1.5-7B-Chat. - [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01850v1)
- (d) Alternation: PADv1, PADv2 (both roles), then PADv3 (defender only); 3 epochs per iteration on data from current and earlier iterations; ~3 h sampling + ~1.5 h training per iteration. - [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01850v1)
- (e) Only the implicit DPO reference (beta 0.1) on the attacker; no KL term on the defender. - [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01850v1)
- (f) Defender vs PADv2 attacker: HitbyT3 89.29% -> 60.71%; vs PAIR: 19.64% -> 8.93%; discrimination accuracy 61.88 -> 85.96; over-refusal 39.2% -> 11.25% of inquiries; GPT-4o quality win/lose vs base 60.2/36.0. - [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01850v1)
- (g) Defence weakens with turns (per-turn ASR cuts 41/37/29 points at turns 1-3); generation-discrimination gap (defender detects but still answers unsafely for most rules); only Qwen1.5-7B, Chinese; oscillation not discussed. - [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01850v1)
- (h) No PAD code link found. - [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01850v1)

**5. Adversarial Preference Learning for Robust LLM Alignment (APL)** - RECENT; first author per ACL Anthology listing (not extracted here, see Gaps), 2025 (Findings of ACL 2025, pp. 21865-21881), arXiv 2505.24369 - [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.24369); [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.1126/)
Summary: Attacker and defender both run DPO objectives; the attacker's reward is intrinsic, the defender's own log-odds of the dispreferred over the preferred response (minus a reference-model baseline), so no separate judge is needed. Over 30 alternating iterations on HH-RLHF harmless data, ASR under direct requests falls 56.25% -> 11.25% on Mistral-7B, but XSTest over-refusal doubles (20.00 -> 40.89). - [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.24369)
- (a) DPO on (most effective, least effective) adversarial rewrites; reward R = log[pi_def(y_dis|x')/pi_def(y_pre|x')] - alpha log[pi_ref(...)], alpha=0.2 best; beta_att 0.01-0.3. - [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.24369)
- (b) DPO with the strongest adversarial prompt replacing the original prompt; beta_def 0.01; lr 5e-7. - [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.24369)
- (c) SAME initialisation: Algorithm 1 initialises both pi_att and pi_def from pi_ref (Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3 or Llama-3-8B-Instruct); separately updated thereafter. - [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.24369)
- (d) N = 30 iterations, 1,024 prompts/iter, K = 8 candidates/prompt, 8 optimisation steps per model per iteration, alternating. - [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.24369)
- (e) Yes: KL to pi_ref via DPO beta on both sides, and pi_ref is the reward baseline; reset policy not stated. - [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.24369)
- (f) Mistral-7B: DR ASR 56.25 -> 11.25, GCG 67.50 -> 41.25, safety win-rate 50 -> 83.33, MT-Bench 6.78 -> 6.59, LC win-rate 50 -> 46.52, XSTest 20.00 -> 40.89 (worse). - [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.24369)
- (g) Over-refusal surge; win-rate plateaus after peak; untrained base attacker slows early convergence; beta sensitivity; oscillation not discussed. - [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.24369)
- (h) "will be open-sourced upon publication"; no link found. - [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.24369)

#### Group B. Self-play: one shared policy (or one frozen base) plays both roles, RL-trained, online

**6. Chasing Moving Targets with Online Self-Play Reinforcement Learning for Safer Language Models (Self-RedTeam)** - RECENT; Mickel Liu, 2025 (v1 9 Jun 2025; v4 6 Jul 2026; "ICML 2026 Poster" per arXiv comments field), arXiv 2506.07468 - [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07468)
Summary: A single policy alternates between attacker and defender roles within each round, trained fully online with a critic-free PPO variant ("Re++") and a WildGuard-7B judge, framed as a two-player zero-sum game whose Nash equilibrium implies safe answers to any adversarial input. Reports +17.80% SBERT attack diversity and up to 95% safety gain on RLHF-trained models; against a Defender-Only+SFT baseline, "an additional 17.33% improvement in safety robustness" with AlpacaEval-2 LC 38.09% vs 35.50%. - [arXiv HTML v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.07468v4)
- (a) RL (Re++, critic-free PPO variant) with reward from WildGuard-7B labels (query harm, response harm, refusal) plus format and revision-faithfulness terms; game-outcome terms are zero-sum (R_A = -R_D). - [arXiv HTML v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.07468v4)
- (b) Same RL, same model, role instruction I_D; auxiliary SFT (30,000 examples) to keep conversational quality. - [arXiv HTML v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.07468v4)
- (c) SAME WEIGHTS: one shared model pi_theta with role prompts; "role-specific advantage normalization" is "critical for allowing a single model to learn from the opposite reward signals of both roles"; bases Qwen2.5-3B/7B/14B-Instruct, Llama3.1-8B-Instruct (+ abliterated variant). - [arXiv HTML v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.07468v4)
- (d) Online, co-evolving; within each round attacker moves then defender; 26,000 WildJailbreak prompts, 50:50 harmful/benign; step counts not in the read portion. - [arXiv HTML v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.07468v4)
- (e) Yes: token-level KL penalty (weight beta) to a reference policy in the advantage (Eq. 2). - [arXiv HTML v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.07468v4)
- (f) +17.33% safety robustness over defender-only+SFT; AlpacaEval-2 LC 38.09 vs 35.50 (with SFT) but 20.99 vs 22.51 without SFT; abstract says 14 benchmarks, body tables show 12/11. - [arXiv HTML v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.07468v4)
- (g) Attacker-Only "converge[s] into a few dominant modes" (revision faithfulness ~60%); Defender-Only "rapidly converges" on static attacks but fails OOD; benign seeds needed to stop a refuse-everything defender; CoT-free Llama variants show "severe over-refusal"; reward-hacking rebutted via cross-judge checks; exact Nash "challenging in practice". - [arXiv HTML v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.07468v4)
- (h) Code: github.com/mickelliu/selfplay-redteaming. - [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07468)

**7. Be Your Own Red Teamer: Safety Alignment via Self-Play and Reflective Experience Replay (SSP)** - RECENT; Hao Wang (Beihang/PKU/Zhongguancun Lab; corresponding Lei Sha), 2026 (arXiv preprint, 15 Jan 2026, cs.CR), arXiv 2601.10589 - [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.10589)
Summary: One LLM "act[s] concurrently as both the Attacker and the Defender" in a unified RL loop with zero-sum coupled rewards from a 1-5 judge score; failures are stored in a replay pool and re-sampled with a UCB rule that prioritises low-reward items, which the authors say prevents "a cycle of catastrophic forgetting" seen without the pool. - [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.10589)
- (a) RL (algorithm unnamed); r_att = clip((Score-1)/4). - [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.10589)
- (b) Same loop; r_def = clip((5-Score)/4); r_att = 1 - r_def. - [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.10589)
- (c) SAME WEIGHTS (unified model); ablation "w/o UM" splits into two co-evolving models and is worse (GCG ASR 5.8 vs 1.7). Bases: Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Vicuna-7B, Llama3-8B-Instruct, Mistral3-8B-Instruct. - [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.10589)
- (d) Simultaneous (joint objective, weight lambda on attacker term); "3 iterations", lr 1e-6, batch 8; replay pools ~80 items each by step 75. - [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.10589)
- (e) No KL/reference term described. - [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.10589)
- (f) Qwen2.5-7B ASR: GCG 1.7, PAIR 2.4, TAP 1.4; capability cost: MMLU 79.5 -> 72.6, HumanEval 84.8 -> 78.0, GSM8K 91.6 -> 87.2; OR-Bench refusal 25.3% (vs 29.6% ACE-Safety). - [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.10589)
- (g) Without replay pool: "cycle of catastrophic forgetting", volatile mirrored reward curves; attack diversity bounded by base model; ablations: w/o UCB 3.8, w/o replay 4.7 GCG ASR. - [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.10589)
- (h) No code link found. - [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.10589)

**8. The Attacker in the Mirror: Breaking Self-Consistency in Safety via Anchored Bipolicy Self-Play (ABS)** - RECENT; Gabriele La Malfa (KCL) / Emanuele La Malfa (Oxford), 2026 (arXiv preprint, 8 May 2026), arXiv 2605.08427 - [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08427v1)
Summary: Critiques shared-weight self-play (Self-RedTeam): the combined update is grad J_D + grad J_A on the same parameters, so a defender step changes the attacker objective by ~eta<grad J_A, grad J_D> and the attacker is pushed "away from prompts it already defends" ("self-consistency"), yielding a "muddy" middle rather than a stronger attacker. Fix: freeze the base and train two role-specific LoRA adapters with Re++ and token-level KL; attacker tournament success vs its own base rises from 18% to 35% (3B) and 9% to 24% (7B) versus Self-RedTeam. - [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08427v1)
- (a) RL (Re++), WildGuard reward (r=-1 unsafe, 0 refusal, >=0 safe; attacker gets -r), own LoRA adapter. - [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08427v1)
- (b) Same, own LoRA adapter. - [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08427v1)
- (c) SAME FROZEN BASE, DISJOINT ADAPTERS (d phi_D / d phi_A = 0); Qwen2.5-3B/7B/14B-IT. - [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08427v1)
- (d) Each role's objective computed with the other held fixed; schedule/iteration count in appendix C.3 (not read); 14B rank-32 run "five hours" on 3 H200s. - [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08427v1)
- (e) Yes: token-level KL to a reference policy; theory uses a TV/KL neighbourhood around a reference adversarial policy. - [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08427v1)
- (f) WildGuard adversarial ASR 3B: base 0.282, Self-RedTeam 0.234, ABS 0.122; 7B: 0.279/0.178/0.169; 14B: 0.146/0.116/0.131 (ABS not uniformly better). XSTest benign compliance 3B: 0.960/0.932/0.952. Reasoning avg 3B: 0.745/0.671/0.738. - [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08427v1)
- (g) LoRA collapse into repeated "!" tokens; meta-text leakage; much longer attacks (232.7 vs 59.3 tokens); scale sensitivity. - [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08427v1)
- (h) Code github.com/EmanueleLM/AnchoredBipolicySelf-Play; checkpoints on HF (EmanueleLaMalfa). - [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08427v1)

#### Group C. Separate attacker and defender models, RL co-training (RECENT, 2025-2026)

**9. MAGIC: A Co-Evolving Attacker-Defender Adversarial Game for Robust LLM Safety** - RECENT; Xiaoyu Wen (Shanghai AI Lab / SJTU), 2026 (arXiv v2 6 Feb 2026, no venue), arXiv 2602.01539 - [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.01539)
Summary: Separate attacker and defender, SFT warm-up of the attacker (SorryBench pool with Gemini-2.5-Pro CoT), then alternating GRPO phases with Qwen3Guard rewards; explicitly argues shared-parameter self-play "inevitably leads to gradient conflicts". Qwen2.5-7B WildGuardTest ASR 36.5% -> 2.3%, AlpacaEval-2 LC 33.7 -> 33.2. - [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.01539)
- (a) SFT init then GRPO; R_A = -R_D + R_fmt (Qwen3Guard harm/refusal judge). - [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.01539)
- (b) GRPO; R_D = R_harm + R_ref, where R_ref penalises both refusing safe and answering unsafe queries. - [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.01539)
- (c) SEPARATE policies, same base family (Qwen2.5-7B/14B-Instruct, Llama3.1-8B-Instruct). - [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.01539)
- (d) Strict alternation per round: defender phase with attacker frozen, then attacker phase with defender frozen; K rounds, T_A/T_D steps (values in appendix C.1, not read); single-turn training. - [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.01539)
- (e) Yes: GRPO KL term -beta D_KL(pi_theta || pi_ref) (beta not read). - [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.01539)
- (f) WildJailbreak adversarial harm 70.1 -> 19.8 (7B), 58.3 -> 7.6 (14B); XSTest compliance 0.940 -> 0.945 (7B) but 0.936 -> 0.888 (14B); AlpacaEval-2 LC 38.1 -> 31.0 on 14B; X-Teaming multi-turn ASR 94.9 -> 85.4; GCG 43.90 -> 11.25. - [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.01539)
- (g) Gradient conflict in shared-weight self-play (cited as motivation); defender over-refusal collapse countered by R_ref; format reward on defender degraded capability; sensitivity to SFT init and attacker capacity; "new exploits resurface". - [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.01539)

### Inferences
- The field splits cleanly into three generations: (i) 2023-2024 SFT/DPO loops with RM filters and a shared base (MART, DART, PAD, APL); (ii) 2025-2026 online RL loops (Self-RedTeam, MAGIC, AdvGame, TriPlay-RL, AdvGRPO, CHASE, ARLAS, AgentSafeEvo, GPT-Red); (iii) a parallel optimiser-as-attacker line (Ziegler, CAT, LAT, RPO) that is "adversarial training" in Madry's sense but has no attacker *policy* that could share a base. Only generation (ii) and RTG/GRTS satisfy "RL attacker against an updating defender".
- The only papers in which one set of weights literally plays both roles are Self-RedTeam and Safety Self-Play; ABS keeps one frozen base but disjoint adapters; MART/DART/PAD/APL/TriPlay-RL/MAGIC/AgentSafeEvo share a base checkpoint but diverge after initialisation.
- Reported helpfulness cost correlates with how hard the defender is pushed: SFT-era loops report 0.5-4% helpfulness drops; RL co-training papers show large benign-compliance losses (AdvGRPO WJB 99.2 -> 72.8; AdvGame-Llama 98.8 -> 69.9; CHASE XSTest 44.4%, MT-Bench -1.92; APL XSTest 20 -> 41) unless a refusal/utility reward is explicit (MAGIC, TriPlay-RL, AgentSafeEvo).

## Key Question 2: Which papers use a single shared base for both roles, or literally one model playing both roles, and what do they say about the attacker exploiting weaknesses it shares with the defender?

### Takeaway
Shared *initialisation* is the norm (MART, DART, PAD, APL, RTG, TriPlay-RL, MAGIC, AgentSafeEvo all start both roles from one checkpoint), but only Self-RedTeam and Safety Self-Play train one set of weights for both roles; three 2026 papers (MAGIC, AdvGame, ABS) argue explicitly that weight sharing entangles the objectives, the attacker becomes "self-consistent" with its own defences, and a model that can attack "will necessarily be unsafe when used as a Defender". No paper reports a positive finding that a shared attacker finds *more* of the defender's own weaknesses; Safety Self-Play's ablation is the only evidence in favour of sharing.

### Cited Findings
- Literal one-model self-play: Self-RedTeam uses "a single shared model pi_theta" with role instructions and needs role-specific advantage normalisation so one model can "learn from the opposite reward signals of both roles". - [Self-RedTeam HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.07468v4)
- Literal one-model self-play: SSP uses "a single LLM to act concurrently as both the Attacker ... and the Defender"; its "w/o UM" ablation with two separate co-evolving models is worse (GCG ASR 5.8 vs 1.7 on Qwen2.5-7B). - [SSP HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.10589)
- Frozen shared base, disjoint adapters: ABS freezes Qwen2.5 and trains attacker/defender LoRAs with d phi_D/d phi_A = 0; argues the shared-weight update grad J_D + grad J_A makes the attacker move "away from prompts it already defends" (self-consistency), so attacker strength in tournament vs its own base is 35% (ABS) vs 18% (Self-RedTeam) at 3B and 24% vs 9% at 7B. - [ABS HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08427v1)
- Shared init, separate updates: MART initialises M_tgt and M_adv from the same LLaMA-65B LIMA/OASST model. - [MART HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.07689)
- Shared init: DART starts both from Llama-2-7b-hf. - [DART v1 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.03876v1)
- Shared init: PAD uses LoRA on Qwen1.5-7B-Chat for both. - [PAD HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01850v1)
- Shared init: APL Algorithm 1 initialises pi_att and pi_def from pi_ref. - [APL HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.24369)
- Shared family: RTG uses stablelm 3B for red, blue, critic and toxicity model. - [RTG HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.00322v5)
- Shared base, three instances: TriPlay-RL realises Red/Blue/Eval as "three separate model instances" of one Qwen3 size and lists shared-instance training as an open question. - [TriPlay-RL HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.18292)
- Against sharing: MAGIC says shared-parameter self-play "inevitably leads to gradient conflicts by imposing opposing objectives onto the same parameter space". - [MAGIC HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.01539)
- Against sharing: AdvGame says "a model capable of generating harmful attacks with the correct prompting will necessarily be unsafe when used as a Defender", warns sharing can "entangle objectives, leak gradients across roles, and bias exploration", and notes self-play can degenerate into the attacker refusing to attack; it uses an *abliterated* Llama attacker because the safety-tuned one refuses. - [AdvGame HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.20806v3); [search summary](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.20806)
- Attacker-safety coupling: TriPlay-RL notes Red optimisation "may degrade its own safety behavior" and strengthening Blue "may suppress attack effectiveness". - [TriPlay-RL HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.18292)
- Attacker must lack guardrails: Beutel et al. train a GPT-3.5-sized model "without safety guardrails" because otherwise the attacker may refuse to red-team. - [Beutel HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.18693)
- Shared weaknesses across *rounds* (not roles): Ziegler et al. found attacks targeting the final classifier worked nearly as well on earlier classifiers, i.e. failures were pre-existing rather than introduced. - [Ziegler HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2205.01663)
- Perez 2022 suggested, but did not run, "jointly train red and target LMs in a GAN-like setup". - [Perez HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2202.03286)

### Inferences
- The claim that "a shared attacker can introspect on shared weaknesses" is not made by any of the papers read; the literature's stated mechanism for shared weights is *efficiency* (one model, no extra memory), and the 2026 critiques treat the shared gradient as a liability (self-consistency, refusal to attack).
- Shared *initialisation* with separate updates is treated as unproblematic everywhere; the contested design choice is shared *parameters during training*.

### Gaps
- No paper measured whether attacks found by a shared-weight attacker transfer less or more to third-party defenders than attacks from a separate attacker; ABS's cross-play tournament (6% vs 17-18%) is the closest datum.
- Self-RedTeam's own discussion of role entanglement, if any, is in the unread second half of its HTML.

## Key Question 3: Which papers train the attacker with RL against a defender that is itself being updated? How many rounds, and does the attacker keep finding new breaches?

### Takeaway
RL-attacker-vs-moving-defender appears in RTG/GRTS (PPO, 2023) and then in every 2025-2026 co-training paper (Self-RedTeam, SSP, ABS: Re++/unnamed RL; MAGIC, TriPlay-RL, AdvGRPO, CHASE, ARLAS, AgentSafeEvo: GRPO; AdvGame: online DPO/IPO-MD with GRPO variant; Active Attacks: GFlowNet; GPT-Red: self-play RL at scale). Rounds range from 3 (SSP) to 15-30 (RTG exploitability plot, ARLAS, APL) or continuous online training; every paper that reports it finds the attacker's hit rate falls as rounds proceed (CHASE 0.47 -> 0.24 -> 0.22; MART saturates at ~10% by round 4; Adv-RM attack fails after 2 rounds), and new breaches continue only when diversity is explicitly forced (TriPlay-RL, Active Attacks, GRTS) or the defender keeps changing (Active Attacks' "adaptive environment").

### Cited Findings
- RL attacker vs RL defender, population-based: GRTS trains PPO best responses for red and blue in alternation against the other side's meta-strategy; exploitability ~6.2 -> ~0.8 over 15 iterations; blue round-1 ASR 52% -> 1%, equilibrium ASR ~10%. - [RTG HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.00322v5)
- SFT-era contrast: MART's attacker is supervised, not RL, and after 4 rounds "it becomes hard to [get] sufficient enough training data"; adversarial-set violation 29.74 -> 31.89 -> 20.33 -> 10.21% across iterations 1-4. - [MART HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.07689)
- DART's attacker is SFT; ablation with a *fixed* red or target for data selection is worse than using the latest states, and a non-diverse "aggressive" red's strength decays from iteration 5. - [DART v1 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.03876v1)
- Self-RedTeam: online RL, attacker and defender co-evolve each round; Attacker-Only (frozen defender) collapses to "a few dominant modes"; Defender-Only (static attacks) fails OOD; self-play gives +17.33% over defender-only. - [Self-RedTeam HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.07468v4)
- MAGIC: alternating GRPO phases with the other side frozen within a phase; safety fixes are "partial and new exploits resurface". - [MAGIC HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.01539)
- AdvGame: fixed attacker is worse than a continually trained one (WJB ASR 16.6 vs 8.5); run capped at 1000 steps/48 h. - [AdvGame HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.20806v3)
- TriPlay-RL: Red trained 16 iterations x 200 steps; ASR on Qwen3-8B 21.84% -> 67.75% and on Nemotron-Nano 60% -> 90%; without the closed loop Red's diversity (OD) falls from 0.588 to 0.156 even as ASR vs a weak target rises. - [TriPlay-RL HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.18292)
- AdvGRPO: alternation every 10 steps for 300 co-training steps; defender improves "after 7-8 alternations"; both-from-scratch "let the defender dominate", so the attacker is pre-trained 200 single-turn + multi-turn steps first. - [AdvGRPO HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09701v1)
- CHASE: attacker retrained against each defender checkpoint; attacker success 0.47 -> 0.24 -> 0.22 and convincingness 4.79 -> 2.83 -> 2.38 over 3 iterations while defender StrongREJECT falls 0.479 -> 0.272 -> 0.162 -> 0.115. - [CHASE HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.05523)
- Active Attacks: attacker reset and retrained from scratch each round against the updated victim; 4-5 rounds; diversity "converges after a small number of rounds"; cross-attack improvement ">400x". - [Active Attacks HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.21947)
- ARLAS: 15 iterations, attacker always faces the latest agent, agent faces all past attackers; "APD diversity consistently increases". - [ARLAS HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.05442)
- AgentSafeEvo: final attacker reaches 92.59% ASR on the base defender (from 55.81% for the SFT attacker) while the final defender holds it to 49.82%. - [AgentSafeEvo HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.19422)
- GPT-Red: attacker trained against simultaneously trained defenders; "defenders sometimes patch an attack, causing ASR dips before new strategies emerge". - [GPT-Red HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.26115)
- Adv-RM (RM side): "after 2 rounds of adversarial training, our attack procedure is no longer successful". - [Adv-RM HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.06141)
- Frozen-defender RL attackers for contrast: GFlowNet red-teaming only queries a black-box target - [GFlowNet HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.18540); Beutel et al. treat GPT-4 Turbo as fixed - [Beutel HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.18693); Perez 2022 does not train the target - [Perez HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2202.03286).

### Inferences
- "Does the attacker keep finding new breaches" has a consistent answer: not on its own. Each loop that runs long enough reports saturation; continued discovery is engineered via diversity penalties (TriPlay-RL, GRTS, Active Attacks' category metric), resets (Active Attacks), populations (GRTS, ARLAS, GPT-Red) or multi-defender rewards (TriPlay-RL).
- The number of *effective* rounds is small in every weight-level loop (3-8 alternations before the defender dominates), which is consistent with the attacker losing signal as ASR drops toward the judge's noise floor.

### Gaps
- No paper reports a per-round "novel breach" count (new attack category or cluster discovered per round) except TriPlay-RL's OD score and Active Attacks' categorical distance; most report only ASR trajectories.
- GPT-Red's iteration count, algorithm and judge are not in the read portion.

## Key Question 4: What is known about convergence, oscillation and catastrophic forgetting in these loops, and what tricks are used?

### Takeaway
Observed pathologies are: adversarial oscillation (RTG/GRTS), cyclic re-learning when the defender forgets old attacks (ARLAS, SSP), attacker mode/entropy collapse (Perez, Ziegler's PPO adversary, GFlowNet baselines, Beutel, Self-RedTeam attacker-only, TriPlay-RL, AdvGRPO, GPT-Red), GRPO instability (AdvGame, AdvGRPO, CHASE), judge/reward hacking (MART, Beutel, AdvGame point-wise judge, TriPlay-RL evaluator error amplification, CHASE intent drift), over-refusal/utility collapse (APL, AdvGRPO, AdvGame-Llama, CHASE, R2D2) and the defender dominating a from-scratch attacker (AdvGRPO). Tricks in use: KL to a reference (RTG 0.04, Self-RedTeam, ABS, APL, MAGIC, TriPlay-RL 0.01, AdvGRPO 0.05/0.1, AdvGame 0.1, ARLAS 0.05, CHASE 0.05, Active Attacks 0.1), populations/replay of past attackers or failures (GRTS, ARLAS, SSP UCB pool, Active Attacks global buffer, GPT-Red defender population), benign-data mixing or explicit refusal/utility rewards (MART helpfulness RM, Self-RedTeam 50:50 seeds + SFT, AdvGRPO 50:50 batches, CHASE 1:1 SFT, MAGIC R_ref, AgentSafeEvo utility term, LAT UltraChat/KL), EMA opponents (AdvGame), attacker warm-up curricula (MAGIC SFT, AdvGRPO staged, AgentSafeEvo SFT, ARLAS imitation), diversity penalties (GRTS, TriPlay-RL, Beutel, DART BLEU), and decoupled advantage normalisation (AdvGRPO; role-specific normalisation in Self-RedTeam).

### Cited Findings
- Oscillation: RTG appendix reports "a relatively slow and moderate adversarial oscillation" with a normal toxicity model and "significantly more severe mode collapse" with an over-fitted one; payoff variance rose then fell ("spinning top"). - [RTG appendix](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.00322v5)
- Cyclic learning / forgetting: ARLAS says that without population training the agent "can forget earlier attack patterns and must re-learn defenses when the attacker reverts"; ablation w/o PBL left ASR at 6.6 vs 6.3 base. - [ARLAS HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.05442)
- Forgetting: SSP reports that training without the replay pool "leads to a cycle of catastrophic forgetting" with volatile mirrored reward curves; the UCB replay pool (score (1 - r_bar) + c sqrt(ln N/(n+1)), c = sqrt 2) is the fix; ablation w/o replay GCG ASR 4.7 vs 1.7. - [SSP HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.10589)
- Attacker collapse: Perez RL at alpha 0.3 yields 78% prompts containing "invisible". - [Perez HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2202.03286); Ziegler's PPO/FUDGE LM adversary "suffered from mode collapse" - [Ziegler HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2205.01663); REINFORCE "collapses to deterministically generating a single reward-maximizing prompt" - [GFlowNet HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.18540); vanilla RL diversity 0.02 - [Beutel HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.18693); Attacker-Only converges "into a few dominant modes" - [Self-RedTeam HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.07468v4); TriPlay-RL OD 0.588 -> 0.004 without closed loop and diversity penalty - [TriPlay-RL HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.18292); AdvGRPO entropy collapse, and entropy regularisation "explodes" at 0.15 - [AdvGRPO HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09701v1); GPT-Red attacker-only "mode collapse", multi-defender training reduced it - [GPT-Red HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.26115).
- GRPO instability: AdvGame found GRPO "pronounced instability" and higher harm rates - [AdvGame HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.20806v3); AdvGRPO attributes it to group normalisation coupling advantage scale to a shifting reward distribution and proposes per-channel z-scoring (GDPO) - [AdvGRPO HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09701v1); CHASE notes near-zero GRPO gradients on confident models, hence SFT consolidation - [CHASE HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.05523).
- Judge gaming: MART flags "overfitting on the RM preference" and uses human eval - [MART HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.07689); Beutel calls grader reward hacking "an open problem" - [Beutel HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.18693); AdvGame's point-wise judge variant degraded safety (WJB 30.8 vs 8.5), suspected reward hacking - [AdvGame HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.20806v3); TriPlay-RL: Eval errors "may be amplified over iterations" - [TriPlay-RL HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.18292); CHASE: intent drift and over-sanitising, fixed by multiplicative reward - [CHASE HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.05523); DART had to re-annotate 10k Anthropic Harmless samples because RM scores "were not credible" - [DART v1 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.03876v1); Active Attacks uses the same classifier as reward and evaluator (circularity) - [Active Attacks HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.21947).
- Defender dominance / non-convergence: AdvGRPO: initialising both from scratch "let the defender dominate" - [AdvGRPO HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09701v1); Self-RedTeam: exact Nash "challenging in practice" - [Self-RedTeam HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.07468v4); APL: win rate plateaus after peak - [APL HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.24369).

### Inferences
- Catastrophic forgetting is reported mainly as *cyclic* forgetting of earlier attack families (ARLAS, SSP), not as loss of general capability; general-capability loss is reported as over-refusal/utility drop and is handled with benign data rather than replay.
- The two most recent method papers (AdvGRPO, ABS) both locate the instability in the *optimiser coupling* (advantage normalisation across channels; shared gradients across roles) rather than in the game itself.

### Gaps
- No paper provides a formal convergence/oscillation analysis of the weight-level loop beyond RTG's Proposition 1 and ABS's TV-neighbourhood lemma; empirical oscillation plots were only found in RTG's appendix and SSP's appendix D (not read).
- Forgetting of *pre-existing* safety behaviours (e.g. refusals learned in RLHF) during co-training is not measured anywhere I read.

## Key Question 5: What 2025-2026 (last 18 months) work exists: online self-play safety RL, GRPO co-training, adversarial RLHF, agent safety self-play, industry reports?

### Takeaway
RECENT work is dense: online self-play (Self-RedTeam Jun 2025, SSP Jan 2026, ABS May 2026), GRPO co-training (MAGIC Feb 2026, TriPlay-RL Jan 2026, AdvGRPO Jun 2026, CHASE Jun 2026, ARLAS Oct 2025, AgentSafeEvo Oct 2026), preference-based adversarial alignment (APL ACL-Findings 2025, AdvGame Dec 2025/May 2026 from Meta FAIR, Adv-RM Apr 2025 from NVIDIA on the reward-model side), GFlowNet adaptive environments (Active Attacks Sep 2025), agent settings (ARLAS, AgentSafeEvo, GPT-Red prompt injection), and industry reports (Anthropic Constitutional Classifiers Jan 2025, Google DeepMind Gemini IPI report May 2025, OpenAI GPT-Red Jul 2026, Microsoft AdvGRPO Jun 2026). Training-free "co-evolution" variants (RvB, EvoSafety, Stay-in-Character) also appeared in 2026 but do not update defender weights.

### Inferences
- The 2025-2026 methodological trend runs from shared-weight self-play (Self-RedTeam) toward separate policies with explicit anti-entanglement arguments (MAGIC, AdvGame, ABS) and toward fixing GRPO's variance pathologies (AdvGRPO, CHASE); every RECENT paper that reports benign compliance shows a measurable over-refusal cost except MAGIC-7B, TriPlay-RL (unmeasured) and ABS.
- Industry adoption is real but asymmetric: OpenAI (GPT-Red) and Google DeepMind (Gemini IPI) describe weight-level adversarial RL loops; Anthropic's published loop is classifier-side and human-in-the-loop.
