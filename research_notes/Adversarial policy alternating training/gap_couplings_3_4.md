# Gap check: couplings 3 (prefix) and 4 (weights / activations)

Date: 2026-10-17. Adversarial novelty check: the aim was to find a paper that falsifies the claim, not to confirm it.

**Claim under test.** No published paper trains a separate policy, initialised from the same base model as the main LM, with RL, to generate harmful RESPONSES (not prompts). The policy's reward is defined by the main model's failure, and its updates alternate with the main model's updates (GAN / minimax style).

**Scope of this note.** It covers only two couplings.
- **Coupling 3 (prefix / prefill).** The harm policy writes the start of a response. The main model continues it and is trained to recover.
- **Coupling 4 (weights / activations).** The harm policy acts on the main model's parameters or hidden states.

It does not re-report these, which the earlier survey covered: Token Buncher 2508.20697, AntiDote 2509.08000, DR-SFT 2606.30263, TAR 2408.00761, LAT (Casper 2024, Sheshadri 2024), continuous AT 2405.15589, and the prompt-generator co-training loops (MART, Self-RedTeam, MAGIC, TriPlay-RL, AdvGRPO, CHASE, Active Attacks, GPT-Red). AdvGame 2512.20806 is also a prompt generator and belongs with that group. Learned negative-sample generators, SPIN-style self-play and likelihood-targeted negatives are covered by another researcher.

**Method.** I ran about 30 web searches (extended mode for 2025-2026). I fetched arXiv abs pages for every candidate, and the HTML method sections for the closest hits. All IDs marked "verified" were checked against the arXiv API on 2026-10-17 for title, first author and date.

**Axes used in every verdict:**
- (a) separate learned adversary
- (b) same base as the main model
- (c) trained with RL
- (d) acts through response prefixes (coupling 3) or through weights / activations (coupling 4)
- (e) reward defined by the main model's failure
- (f) alternated or iterated with the main model's updates

Y = yes, N = no, P = partial, n/a = no adversary.

## Coupling 3: prefix / prefill

### A. Static precedents (fixed prefix source, no adversary)

These meet (d) only. They are catalogued because the earlier survey's "no trained precedent" hides them.

**1. Qi et al., "Safety Alignment Should Be Made More Than Just a Few Tokens Deep."** Xiangyu Qi, 2024, ICLR 2025. arXiv 2406.05946 (verified).
- **Mechanism.** Data augmentation: a harmful prompt, then a truncated harmful response prefix taken from an existing harmful-response dataset, then a refusal ("safety recovery examples"). A second component is a token-wise constrained fine-tuning objective.
- **Prefix source.** Fixed offline data.
- **Axes.** a N, b n/a, c N, d Y, e N, f N.

**2. DeRTa, "Refuse Whenever You Feel Unsafe: Improving Safety in LLMs via Decoupled Refusal Training."** Youliang Yuan, 2024, ACL 2025 (long). arXiv 2407.09121 (verified).
- **Mechanism.** MLE with a harmful response prefix of random length, followed by a refusal. Reinforced Transition Optimization (RTO) adds an objective to transition to refusal at every position of the harmful response.
- **Prefix source.** Fixed harmful responses.
- **Axes.** a N, c N, d Y, e N, f N.

**3. "Backtracking Improves Generation Safety."** Yiming Zhang, 2024, ICLR 2025. arXiv 2409.14586 (verified).
- **Mechanism.** The model learns to emit [RESET] after a partial unsafe response and then regenerate. Training is SFT plus DPO on preference pairs.
- **Partial unsafe responses.** They come from fixed data and model samples. No adversary.
- **Axes.** a N, c N (main model trained with DPO), d Y, e N, f N.

**4. "Course-Correction: Safety Alignment Using Synthetic Preferences."** Rongwu Xu, 2024, EMNLP 2024. arXiv 2407.16637 (verified).
- **Mechanism.** C2-Eval benchmark plus C2-Syn, an automated pipeline producing 750K preference pairs that reward correcting course after a harmful start. Trained with DPO.
- **Prefix source.** A fixed synthetic pipeline. The abs page gives no detail of a learned adversary, and none is described.
- **Axes.** a N, d Y, e N, f N.

**5. "Backtracking for Safety" (BSAFE).** Bilgehan Sel, 2025, arXiv preprint. arXiv 2503.08919 (verified).
- **Mechanism.** Category-specific violation-flag tokens, then the harmful span is rewritten. The authors report robustness to prefilling.
- **Prefix source.** Static, programmatic insertion.
- **Axes.** a N, d Y, e N, f N.

**6. "Reinforcement Learning with Backtracking Feedback" (RLBF / BSAFE+).** Bilgehan Sel, 2026. arXiv 2602.08377 (verified). The comments field says NeurIPS 2025.
- **SFT stage.** Harmful segments are "programmatically insert[ed]" into safe answers. They are generated by a prompted LLM that the paper does not name.
- **RL stage.** The model is trained on its OWN live outputs with an LLM critic.
- **No adversary.** The paper describes no separate learned or RL-trained adversary that writes harmful prefixes. Prefilling is not used in RL rollouts.
- **Axes.** a N, c N for the adversary (the main model is RL-trained), d P, e N, f N.

**7. "Improving LLM Safety Alignment with Dual-Objective Optimization" (DOOR / W-DOOR).** Xuandong Zhao, 2025, ICML 2025. arXiv 2503.03710 (verified).
- **Mechanism.** Robust refusal training on generations after harmful prefixes, plus targeted unlearning. A token-weighted variant up-weights transition tokens.
- **Prefix source.** Static.
- **Axes.** a N, d Y, e N, f N.

**8. "No Free Lunch for Defending Against Prefilling Attack by In-Context Learning."** Zhiyu Xue, 2024, arXiv. arXiv 2412.12192 (verified).
- **Mechanism.** An inference-time defence: adversative ("but ...") in-context demonstrations.
- **Axes.** a N, d Y, e N, f N. No training.

**9. "Large Reasoning Models Learn Better Alignment from Flawed Thinking" (RECAP).** ShengYun Peng, 2025, arXiv. arXiv 2510.00938 (verified).
- **Mechanism.** Online RL (DAPO) on the main model. Counter-aligned chain-of-thought (CoT) prefills are injected into rollouts, and the loss covers only the tokens after the prefix.
- **Prefill source.** Prefills come from a FIXED weakly aligned model (DSQwen-7B, or LLaMA-3.2-1B in the cross-family test). They are sampled at temperature 0.9 and filtered with LlamaGuard. Quote: "generated before RL training and are treated as fixed context rather than optimized tokens."
- **Adaptive attacks.** The IPR attack and full-CoT hijacking also reuse fixed prefills.
- **Closeness.** This is the closest RL-on-main-model precedent. The main model is RL-trained to recover after a harmful prefix written by ANOTHER model, but that model is frozen and not trained against the main model.
- **Axes.** a P (a separate model writes the prefix, but it is not learned against the target), b N (a different model), c N for the adversary, d Y, e N, f N.

**10. "Consistency Training Along the Transformer Stack."** Sukrati Gautam, 2026, submitted to EMNLP 2026. arXiv 2606.05817 (verified).
- **Mechanism.** Consistency training against prefill attacks, using 23 prefills per ClearHarm prompt. The prefills were "generated using an abliterated Gemma-3-12B" following the Struppek et al. taxonomy.
- **Prefill source.** A fixed pre-built set.
- **Axes.** a P (an abliterated generator, not trained against the target), c N, d Y, e N, f N.

### B. Adaptive but non-learned prefix sources (closest coupling-3 hits)

**11. "When Autoregressive Consistency Hurts Safety Alignment."** Bochen Lyu, 2026, arXiv preprint (cs.LG). arXiv 2606.04168 (verified).
- **Attack.** The random insertion attack places a 50-token harmful span inside a refusal trajectory.
- **Defence.** "Adversarial safety alignment," instantiated as random worst-insertion training. For each example, k=32 candidate insertion positions are sampled, using spans from fixed harmful responses (the Qi et al. data). Each candidate is scored against the CURRENT model with Phi = H - R: the log-probability of the harmful continuation minus the best log-probability over m=4 refusal restarts. The argmax is used for SFT toward refusal.
- **Adaptivity.** The worst case is chosen against the current parameters (written tau*_theta), but by search over fixed spans, not by a learned generator. The text found gives no schedule for re-running the selection.
- **Axes.** a N (search, not a policy), b n/a, c N, d Y, e Y (the selection score is the main model's preference for harm over recovery), f P (implicitly per step).

**12. "Inference-Time Vulnerability Beyond Shallow Safety: Alignment Along Generation Trajectories."** Kyungmin Park, 2026, arXiv. arXiv 2606.04778 (verified).
- **Mechanism.** Trajectories are built by injecting FIXED phrases mid-sequence. The harmful phrase was picked from a candidate pool by base-model attack success rate (ASR). Injection timing is triggered when the hidden state's similarity to a behaviour prototype exceeds a threshold.
- **Iteration.** "At each round, we recompute prototypes from the updated model and re-run the augmentation." There are 2 rounds.
- **Axes.** a N, c N, d Y, e P (timing adapts to the current model's state, the content does not), f Y (iterated rounds).

**13. "Self-ReSET: Learning to Self-Recover from Unsafe Reasoning Trajectories."** Dongcheng Zhang, 2026, arXiv. arXiv 2605.08936 (verified).
- **Mechanism.** The CURRENT policy's own on-policy rollouts are monitored by Qwen3Guard-Stream. Unsafe trajectories are truncated at the first unsafe point and stored in a FIFO replay buffer (capacity 256). Rollouts then restart from these prefixes with a binary safety reward, using RL.
- **Adaptivity.** The prefix source is adaptive: it is refreshed from the current policy throughout training. The authors explicitly criticise "fixed unsafe prefill states" as brittle.
- **Gap.** The prefix writer is the main model itself, not a separate harm-rewarded policy.
- **Axes.** a N (self-generated), b Y trivially (it is the same model), c N for the adversary (the main model is RL-trained), d Y, e P (prefixes are kept because the main model failed), f Y (continuous).
- **Closeness.** This is the strongest "adaptive prefix source" precedent.

### C. Prefix / prefill ATTACKS with a generator (no co-training)

**14. "Bypassing the Safety Training of Open-Source LLMs with Priming Attacks."** Jason Vega, 2023, arXiv. arXiv 2312.12321 (verified).
- **Mechanism.** A helper LLM, few-shot prompted (from memory; not re-read here), writes a partial harmful "priming" response that the target then continues.
- **Axes.** a P (the helper is not trained), c N, d Y, e N, f N.

**15. "Prefill-level Jailbreak: A Black-Box Risk Analysis of Large Language Models."** Yakai Li, 2025, arXiv. arXiv 2504.21038 (verified).
- **Mechanism.** Static and Optimized Prefilling. In the optimized variant, an attacker LLM iteratively refines the prefill from the previous response. This is in-context refinement, not RL weight updates. Attack only.
- **Axes.** a P (an LLM attacker, not trained), c N, d Y, e Y (iterates on target failure), f N (the target is not trained).

**16. "Sockpuppetting: Jailbreaking LLMs by Combining Prefilling with Optimization."** Asen Dotsinski, 2026, arXiv. arXiv 2601.13359 (verified).
- **Mechanism.** Trivial prefill ensembles, plus an adversarial suffix optimized inside the assistant block (a GCG-style baseline). Attack only.
- **Axes.** a N, c N, d Y, e Y, f N.

**17. "Exposing the Systematic Vulnerability of Open-Weight Models to Prefill Attacks."** Lukas Struppek, 2026, ICML 2026 listing. arXiv 2602.14689 (verified).
- **Mechanism.** A taxonomy of 23 prefill strategies tested on 50 models. Attack evaluation only.
- **Axes.** a N, d Y, f N.

**18. "Response Attack: Exploiting Contextual Priming to Jailbreak LLMs."** Ziqi Miao, 2025, arXiv. arXiv 2507.05248.
- **Mechanism.** Mildly harmful responses are placed in the dialogue history as primers. The paper proposes safety fine-tuning on such data, which is static.
- **Axes.** a N, d P (history, not the current assistant turn), f N.

**19. "Weak-to-Strong Jailbreaking on Large Language Models."** Xuandong Zhao, 2024. arXiv 2401.17256 (verified). Related: "Emulated Disalignment," Zhanhui Zhou, 2024, arXiv 2402.12343 (verified).
- **Mechanism.** A small unsafe model steers the large model's decoding through log-probability ratios. It acts on the start of the response, but at decoding time. The small model is fine-tuned to be unsafe, not trained against the target, and there is no alternation.
- **Axes.** a P, b P (same family), c N, d P, e N, f N.

**20. "LLMStinger: Jailbreaking LLMs using RL fine-tuned LLMs."** Piyush Jha, 2024, AAAI 2025 student abstract. arXiv 2411.08862 (verified).
- **Mechanism.** An RL-trained attacker LLM writes jailbreak SUFFIXES appended to the prompt, not response prefixes. There is no defender training.
- **Axes.** a Y, c Y, d N, e Y, f N.

**Searched for and not found:** an RL-trained (or otherwise learned) PREFILL attacker. LLMStinger, RL-Hammer and Jailbreak-R1 all emit prompt-side text.

### Coupling 3 verdict: NOT FALSIFIED (closest papers listed in summary 2)

No paper found makes the prefix source a separate learned (let alone RL-trained) policy that is re-trained against the current main model across rounds. The adaptive pieces exist separately:
- adaptive selection of fixed harmful spans against the current model (2606.04168);
- iterated re-augmentation rounds (2606.04778);
- prefixes harvested from the current policy's own failures (Self-ReSET 2605.08936);
- RL training of the main model after a prefill written by a separate frozen model (RECAP 2510.00938, consistency training 2606.05817).

The axis every one of them misses is (a)+(c): a learned prefix writer whose reward is the main model's failure. The novelty claim for coupling 3 therefore narrows to "a learned, RL-trained prefix writer". Adaptive prefix sources and recovery training themselves have prior art and must be cited.

## Coupling 4: weights / activations

**21. ReFAT, "Robust LLM safeguarding via refusal feature adversarial training."** Lei Yu, 2024, ICLR 2025. arXiv 2409.20089 (verified).
- **Mechanism.** During training, the refusal direction is ablated from the residual stream, simulating the effect of jailbreaks. The direction is recomputed from difference-in-means on the current model as training goes on. The defender is trained to refuse anyway.
- **Adversary.** It is computed (closed form), not learned.
- **Axes.** a N, b n/a, c N, d Y (activations), e N (not reward-driven; fixed ablation), f P (re-computed periodically).

**22. "Beyond Surface Alignment: Rebuilding LLMs Safety Mechanism via Probabilistically Ablating Refusal Direction" (DeepRefusal).** Yuanbo Xie, 2025, Findings of EMNLP 2025. arXiv 2509.15202 (verified).
- **Mechanism.** A ReFAT follow-up: the refusal direction is probabilistically ablated across layers and token positions during fine-tuning. The authors report robustness to prefilling as well. The perturbation is computed and random.
- **Axes.** a N, c N, d Y, e N, f P.

**23. "Latent Adversarial Training Improves the Representation of Refusal."** Alexandra Abbas, 2025, arXiv. arXiv 2504.18872 (verified).
- **Mechanism.** An analysis of how LAT reshapes the refusal direction. The adversary is PGD, as in standard LAT.
- **Axes.** a N, c N, d Y, e Y (PGD targets harmful output), f Y (inner/outer).
- **Relation to LAT.** Same class as the LAT papers the survey covered.

**24. "Steering Beyond the Support: Adversarial Training on Unsupervised Jailbroken Activation Simulation."** Luoyu Chen, 2026, ICML 2026 (PMLR v306). arXiv 2605.24535.
- **Mechanism.** A bi-level loop. The inner adversary is a parameterised set of latent directions V (unit vectors, randomly initialised, optimised by argmax of a discovery objective). It adds R*v to refusal-state activations at an early layer to simulate jailbroken states. The outer step trains a separate tiny MLP potential f_phi whose gradient is a test-time steering field. The LLM weights are NOT trained.
- **Axes.** a P (learned direction parameters, not a policy network), b N, c N, d Y (activations), e P (the objective is shift / coverage of jailbreak subspace, not the harmfulness of generated text), f Y (alternating inner/outer).
- **Closeness.** This is the nearest activation-space alternating game, but neither player is the LM and the adversary is not RL.

**25. "Adversarial Representation Engineering: A General Model Editing Framework for LLMs" (ARE).** Yihao Zhang, 2024, arXiv (v3). arXiv 2404.13752 (verified).
- **Mechanism.** A GAN-style alternation in representation space. A representation discriminator is trained, and the LLM (LoRA) is tuned so its representations fool the discriminator. Used to strengthen or remove alignment.
- **Gap.** The adversary is a discriminator, not a harm-generating policy.
- **Axes.** a Y (a learned discriminator), b N, c N, d Y (representations), e N (the adversary is not rewarded by harmful output), f Y.

**26. "Open-Weight LLM Fine-Tuning Defenses are Susceptible to Simple Attacks."** Kevin Kuo, 2026, arXiv. arXiv 2605.26526 (verified).
- **Mechanism.** Shows that abliteration and prefilling break TAR, SEAM and similar defences. Proposes Abliteration-Resistant Tuning, which simulates the worst-case abliteration on the current parameters during training.
- **Adversary.** It is computed, not learned.
- **Axes.** a N, c N, d Y, e P, f P.

**27. "Dynamic Adversarial Fine-Tuning Reorganizes Refusal Geometry."** Wenhao Lan, 2026, arXiv. arXiv 2604.27019 (verified). Companion paper arXiv 2606.16349.
- **Mechanism.** An analysis of R2D2 (HarmBench's dynamic adversarial training with a GCG test-case pool) and of refusal geometry. The adversary is a GCG prompt search.
- **Axes.** a N, d N (prompts), f Y.

**28. "Red Teaming with Mind Reading: White-Box Adversarial Policies Against RL Agents."** Stephen Casper, 2022, arXiv. arXiv 2209.02167 (verified).
- **Mechanism.** An RL adversary policy OBSERVES the target's latent state, and is also applied to LM red teaming. It acts through inputs, not through weights or activations. No alternation with target training.
- **Axes.** a Y, c Y, d N (it reads activations but does not act on them), e Y, f N.

**29. "Bait-and-Recover: Poisoning Internal Refusal Signals to Defend LLMs against White-Box Editing Jailbreaks."** Tian Gao, 2026, arXiv. arXiv 2609.05794.
- **Mechanism.** Bait and recovery adapters defend against refusal-direction edit searches. The attacker is a non-learned search, and the training described is not adversarial.
- **Axes.** a N, d Y, f N.

**30. "Fight Fire with Fire: Defending Against Malicious RL Fine-Tuning via Reward Neutralization."** Wenjun Cao, 2025, arXiv. arXiv 2505.04578.
- **Mechanism.** The defence trains concise minimal-information refusals so that a harmful-RL fine-tuning attacker gets no reward signal. The abs page describes no simulated-RL inner loop or alternation.
- **Axes.** a N (the attacker is threat-model only), c (attacker) Y, d Y (weights, via fine-tuning), e Y, f N.

**Searched for and not found:**
- a hypernetwork adversary other than AntiDote (the hits were only benign Text-to-LoRA / Code2LoRA hypernetworks);
- an RL or policy-gradient weight-space attacker co-trained with a defender;
- a learned or amortised LAT adversary replacing PGD;
- a learned multi-direction (refusal-cone) adversary used for training. Wollschläger et al. 2502.17420 finds cones by gradient, as analysis only.

### Coupling 4 verdict: PARTIALLY ANTICIPATED (closest papers listed in summary 3)

AntiDote (from the survey) remains the closest paper. It is a learned hypernetwork that emits LoRA patches from the defender's activations, rewarded by the defender's harmful behaviour, and alternated with defender updates. It misses (b), since the hypernetwork is not a copy of the base, and (c), since it is trained with DPO rather than RL.

The new finds add:
- learned-parameter alternating games in activation space (2605.24535, which misses b, c and the LM-as-defender; ARE 2404.13752, whose adversary is a discriminator, not harm-rewarded);
- computed activation adversaries re-derived from the current model (ReFAT, DeepRefusal, ART in 2605.26526).

None meets all six axes. No RL-trained weight- or activation-space adversary co-trained with an LLM defender was found.

## Summary
1. **Coupling 3: NOT FALSIFIED.** Static prefix-recovery training is well established (Qi 2406.05946, DeRTa 2407.09121, Backtracking 2409.14586, C2 2407.16637, DOOR 2503.03710, BSAFE / RLBF, RECAP 2510.00938). Adaptive but non-learned prefix sources now exist: worst-insertion search (2606.04168), iterated re-augmentation (2606.04778), and self-harvested failure prefixes (Self-ReSET 2605.08936). No learned or RL-trained prefix writer co-trained with the target was found.
2. **Coupling 4: PARTIALLY ANTICIPATED.** The closest papers are AntiDote (misses b and c), Steering Beyond the Support (misses b, c, and the LLM is not the trained player), ARE (the adversary is a discriminator), and ReFAT / DeepRefusal / ART (computed adversaries). No paper meets all six axes.
3. **Caveats.** Recall from web search is not exhaustive. These pages were read only at abstract level: 2407.16637, 2503.03710, 2507.05248, 2505.04578 and 2609.05794. Vega 2312.12321 was described from memory.
