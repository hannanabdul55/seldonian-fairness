# Adversarially trained LM defences: evaluation, failure modes, 2025-26 frontier, safe-RL connections

Compiled 2026-10-17. Scope: how adversarially / self-play trained safety defences are evaluated, what breaks, what the most recent work (2025 to October 2026) does, and how it connects to constrained / Seldonian RL. Written to support a survey of a scheme in which a harm policy (same base as the main model, RL-trained to make the main model breach a safety constraint) and the main instruction-tuned model are updated in alternation.

Conventions. Every source entry gives: title; first author; year; venue; arXiv ID or DOI as shown on arxiv.org; 2-4 sentence summary; and a line "Alternation:" stating what the source says about alternating attacker/defender training or about evaluating it. Items first posted in 2025 or 2026 are tagged **[RECENT]**. Anything I could not confirm on arxiv.org or a primary page is marked UNVERIFIED. Numbers are quoted with their exact setting; where a paper's headline numbers changed between arXiv versions, both are given.

## Key Question 1: Benchmarks and protocols for jailbreak robustness, over-refusal cost, and judge reliability; how adversarial-training papers report held-out attacks

### Takeaway
The field has converged on a small set of harmful-behaviour suites (HarmBench, JailbreakBench, StrongREJECT, AdvBench, WildJailbreak, SORRY-Bench) paired with over-refusal suites (XSTest, OR-Bench, WildJailbreak's adversarial-benign split) and LLM/classifier judges; but the judges disagree with one another and are themselves attackable, and most adversarial-training papers evaluate on the same attack family they trained on, so "held-out attack" numbers must be read per family. The 2026 self-play papers do now report per-family held-out results (CHASE, MAGIC, AdvGame), and those show large but uneven transfer (e.g. CHASE: AutoDAN -74.9% vs PAIR -19.9% on JailbreakBench; Translation worsened).

### Cited Findings

**Robustness benchmarks**

- **HarmBench: A Standardized Evaluation Framework for Automated Red Teaming and Robust Refusal.** Mantas Mazeika. 2024. ICML 2024 (PMLR v235). arXiv:2402.04249. Introduces a standardized harmful-behaviour benchmark and classifier; the arXiv version compares 18 red-teaming methods against 33 target models and defences and finds attacks and defences work unevenly across models. Its Llama-2-13B classifier is the de facto ASR judge in later adversarial-training work (used by AdvGRPO below). Alternation: proposes adversarial training (R2D2) as a baseline defence, trained and tested on GCG (per CHASE's characterisation). — [ICML](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/mazeika24a.html); [HF mirror](https://huggingface.co/papers/2402.04249.md)
- **JailbreakBench: An Open Robustness Benchmark for Jailbreaking Large Language Models.** Patrick Chao. 2024. NeurIPS 2024 Datasets and Benchmarks. arXiv:2404.01318 (v1 28 Mar 2024, v5 31 Oct 2024). 100 misuse behaviours aligned with OpenAI usage policies, an evolving repository of "jailbreak artifacts", a standardized evaluation framework (threat model, system prompts, chat templates, scoring functions) and a leaderboard. Judge: Llama-3-70B with a custom prompt, selected on a 300-example human-preference set; a Llama-3-8B refusal judge; camera-ready adds an over-refusal set (benign counterparts, per secondary sources). Alternation: none; it is the evaluation harness CHASE uses for held-out families. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.01318); [NeurIPS](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2024/poster/97459)
- **A StrongREJECT for Empty Jailbreaks.** Alexandra Souly. 2024. NeurIPS 2024. arXiv:2402.10260. Argues prior evaluations overstate jailbreak success because they count non-refusal as success even when the response is incoherent or useless; proposes specific, answerable forbidden prompts (six harm categories) and a grader scoring usefulness to the harmful goal, which agrees better with humans than prior graders. Also finds jailbreaks that bypass safety tuning tend to degrade capability. Alternation: none; used as the primary metric by CHASE. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.10260v2); [NeurIPS](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/e2e06adf560b0706d3b1ddfca9f29756-Abstract.html); [BAIR blog](https://bair.berkeley.edu/blog/2024/08/28/strong-reject/)
- **Universal and Transferable Adversarial Attacks on Aligned Language Models.** Andy Zou. 2023. arXiv:2307.15043 (v1 27 Jul 2023). Gradient+greedy search for adversarial suffixes (GCG) trained on Vicuna-7B/13B that transfer to ChatGPT, Bard, Claude and open models. AdvBench (the harmful-behaviours list used everywhere) is commonly attributed to this paper; the abs page I fetched does not name it, so attribution is from the field's usage (UNVERIFIED on the abs page). Alternation: none; GCG is the canonical "unseen optimisation-based" attack used to test transfer of adversarially trained defences. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15043)
- **WildTeaming at Scale: From In-the-Wild Jailbreaks to (Adversarially) Safer Language Models.** Liwei Jiang. 2024. arXiv:2406.18510 (26 Jun 2024). Mines 5.7K clusters of jailbreak tactics from real user-chatbot logs, composes them into new jailbreaks, and releases WildJailbreak, 262K prompt-response pairs with vanilla and adversarial, harmful and benign splits; the adversarial-benign split exists specifically to measure over-refusal. A secondary source gives the evaluation split as 2,213 adversarial prompts (UNVERIFIED on the primary page). Alternation: static training set; it is Self-RedTeam's RL prompt source and also its benchmark, so WildJailbreak is not held out there. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18510)
- **SORRY-Bench: Systematically Evaluating Large Language Model Safety Refusal.** Tinghao Xie. 2024/2025. ICLR 2025. arXiv:2406.14598 (v1 20 Jun 2024, v2 1 Mar 2025). 440 class-balanced unsafe instructions over 44 fine-grained categories with 20 linguistic/format mutations; 7K+ human annotations used to meta-evaluate judges; finds fine-tuned 7B judges can match GPT-4-scale judges. Alternation: none. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.14598)

**Over-refusal / helpfulness-cost benchmarks**

- **XSTest: A Test Suite for Identifying Exaggerated Safety Behaviours in Large Language Models.** Paul Röttger. 2023/2024. NAACL 2024. arXiv:2308.01263 (v1 2 Aug 2023, v3 1 Apr 2024). 250 safe prompts across ten prompt types that a well-calibrated model should answer, plus 200 unsafe contrast prompts. Alternation: none; it is the standard over-refusal check in every 2025-26 self-play paper below (reported as "compliance on 250 safe prompts"). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.01263)
- **OR-Bench: An Over-Refusal Benchmark for Large Language Models.** Justin Cui. 2024/2025. ICML 2025. arXiv:2405.20947 (v1 31 May 2024, v5 15 Jun 2025). Automatic generation of about 80,000 over-refusal prompts in 10 categories, a ~1,000-prompt hard subset and 600 toxic prompts; evaluates 32 LLMs from 8 families and states that "enhanced safety often come[s] with the side effect of over-refusal". The quantified safety/over-refusal correlation is in the full paper (not captured; UNVERIFIED). Alternation: none. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.20947)
- Helpfulness is reported in the self-play papers with AlpacaEval-2 LC win rate, MT-Bench, Arena-Hard, MMLU, IFEval/IFBench, ARC-C and TruthfulQA; see Key Question 3 for the per-paper numbers. — [Self-RedTeam](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.07468v4); [AdvGRPO](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09701v2); [AdvGame](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.20806)

**Judge reliability and judge gaming**

- **LLM-Safety Evaluations Lack Robustness.** Tim Beyer. 2025 **[RECENT]**. arXiv:2503.02574 (v1 4 Mar 2025, v2 18 May 2026). Audits the whole pipeline (dataset curation, red-team optimisation, generation, LLM-judge evaluation) and finds "many intertwined sources of noise". Per the search snippet of the body, StrongREJECT's scorer and HarmBench's classifier disagree enough to change scores and rankings of models, defences and attacks; the authors note that if two standard classifiers disagree, at least one disagrees with human ground truth. Alternation: a direct warning that round-to-round "improvements" in a loop can be judge noise. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.02574); [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.02574)
- **Adversarial Attacks on LLM-as-a-Judge Systems: Insights from Prompt Injections.** Narek Maloyan. 2025 **[RECENT]**. arXiv:2504.18333. Separates content-author attacks from system-prompt attacks on judges; five judge models, four tasks, attack success up to 73.8%; recommends multi-model committees and comparative (pairwise) scoring. Alternation: relevant because an RL attacker that is rewarded by a judge can learn these exploits. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.18333)
- **Investigating the Vulnerability of LLM-as-a-Judge Architectures to Prompt-Injection Attacks.** Narek Maloyan. 2025 **[RECENT]**. arXiv:2505.13348. GCG-optimised suffixes flip a pairwise judge's decision (Comparative Undermining Attack) with success above 30% on MT-Bench Human Judgments with Qwen2.5-3B and Falcon3-3B judges, and manipulate its justification. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.13348v1)
- StrongREJECT's core finding stands as the baseline critique: "existing evaluation methods significantly overstate jailbreak effectiveness compared to human judgments". — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.10260v2)
- Judge choice shifts adversarial-training results: in Self-RedTeam, swapping the WildGuard-7B reward model for Qwen3Guard improved 8 of 11 safety metrics (average relative gain 4.0%). — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.07468)
- CHASE validates its intent-fidelity judge against humans (Pearson 0.841, MAE 0.169 on 100 pairs; inter-annotator Cohen's kappa 0.467 to 0.702) — a rare example of a self-play paper reporting judge calibration. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.05523v1)

**Certified / statistical evaluation**

- **How Catastrophic is Your LLM? Certifying Risk in Conversation (C3LLM).** Chengxiao Wang. 2025/2026 **[RECENT]**. ICLR 2026. arXiv:2510.03969 (v1 4 Oct 2025, v3 5 Feb 2026). Models multi-turn conversations as a Markov process on a semantic-similarity query graph and gives confidence-interval bounds on the probability of a catastrophic response under three fixed sampling distributions (random node, graph path, adaptive-with-rejection); reports certified lower bounds up to 70% for the worst model. The distribution is predefined, not adversarially learned. Alternation: none, but it is the closest published object to "a high-confidence bound on breach rate under a declared evaluation distribution". — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.03969)

**How adversarial-training papers report held-out attacks (summary of what they actually do)**

- CHASE trains the attacker only on prompt rewrites and evaluates on five families it never trained on (PAIR, TAP, AutoDAN, PAP, Translation) on BeaverTails and JailbreakBench; mean StrongREJECT score 0.479 to 0.272 (-43.2%) after one red-blue iteration, with per-family iteration-1 results of PAIR 0.705 to 0.565 (-19.9%), TAP 0.501 to 0.229 (-54.3%), AutoDAN 0.509 to 0.128 (-74.9%), PAP 0.646 to 0.298 (-53.9%), Translation 0.040 to 0.115 (+187.5%, negligible absolute) on JailbreakBench. CHASE also states earlier adversarial training (R2D2 on GCG; CAT/CAPO on continuous perturbations) trained and evaluated on the same family. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.05523v1)
- MAGIC reports unseen attackers on HarmBench for Qwen2.5-7B: GCG 43.90% to 11.25%, PAIR 44.38% to 25.31%; multi-turn X-Teaming only 94.9% to 85.4%. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.01539v2)
- AdvGame reports adaptive attacks on Qwen2.5-7B: PAIR/TAP/GCG 45.0/48.8/61.6 to 7.2/10.0/25.3. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.20806)
- Self-RedTeam reports "dynamic" attacks (PAIR, AutoDAN, X-Teaming) separately from its static WildJailbreak-derived training: PAIR ASR on Qwen2.5-14B-IT 0.218 to 0.108 (-50.7%), average 31.3% relative reduction across dynamic attacks; GCG not evaluated in the visible text. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.07468v4)
- Google DeepMind's Gemini report is explicit that training and evaluation used the same attack algorithms (TAP, Beam Search, Actor-Critic) and that "protection against unknown attacks cannot be quantified"; held-out tool (Calendar) generalised partially (TAP still 94.6% ASR). — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.14534v1)

### Inferences
- A survey can state fairly that per-family held-out reporting only became standard in late 2025 / 2026 (CHASE, MAGIC, AdvGame); 2023-24 adversarial-training work (MART, R2D2) reported in-family or single-judge numbers.
- Multi-turn attacks (PAIR in CHASE, X-Teaming in MAGIC) are consistently the least reduced family, so a single-turn harm policy will overstate robustness unless the evaluation includes a multi-turn family.
- Because at least two standard judges disagree (Beyer) and judges are injectable (Maloyan), any alternating loop that uses the same judge for the attacker reward and the final evaluation has an unmeasured circularity; CHASE and Self-RedTeam partially address it with a second judge or human calibration.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve OR-Bench's quantified safety/over-refusal correlation or the specific numbers in Beyer et al.'s judge-disagreement tables (abs pages only).
- No source found that quantifies how much a gamed judge inflates ASR specifically inside an RL red-teaming loop; the judge-attack papers target evaluation judges in general.
- HarmBench and JailbreakBench leaderboard pages were not fetched; numbers above come from the papers.

## Key Question 2: Documented failure modes of adversarial safety training

### Takeaway
The documented failures are: attacker mode collapse under RL (Hong 2024, Lee 2024/25, Ma 2023, Active Attacks 2025, and in 2026 AdvGRPO's own GRPO finding), judge/reward gaming (AdvGame's pointwise-vs-pairwise ablation; CHASE's attacker raising raw ASR while intent fidelity collapses), shallow alignment that fine-tuning or a single activation direction undoes (Qi 2024, Arditi 2024, Qi 2023), over-refusal as the price of robustness (LLM-LAT 0.004 benign compliance; CHASE 44.4% XSTest compliance; AdvGame Llama 98.8 to 69.9 benign compliance), and capability loss (CHASE MT-Bench 7.42 to 5.50; Self-RedTeam Llama AlpacaEval-2 24.74 to 21.41). Oscillation / cycling across rounds is asserted as a risk by several 2025-26 papers (AdvGame, ARLAS) but I found no paper that demonstrates it quantitatively for LLM safety loops.

### Cited Findings

**Why safety training fails (conceptual)**

- **Jailbroken: How Does LLM Safety Training Fail?** Alexander Wei. 2023. NeurIPS 2023 (oral). arXiv:2307.02483. Proposes two failure modes: competing objectives (pretraining/instruction-following vs safety) and mismatched generalisation (inputs out-of-distribution for safety data but in-distribution for pretraining); attacks built from these succeed on every prompt in GPT-4 and Claude v1.3 red-team sets. Alternation: argues scaling alone will not fix it; "safety-capability parity" is needed, which motivates adversaries as capable as the defender. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.02483v1); [NeurIPS](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2023/oral/73831)
- **Safety Alignment Should Be Made More Than Just a Few Tokens Deep.** Xiangyu Qi. 2024/2025. ICLR 2025 (oral). arXiv:2406.05946. Shows alignment mostly changes the first few output tokens ("shallow safety alignment"), which explains suffix, prefilling, decoding-parameter and fine-tuning attacks; deepening alignment and a token-wise constrained fine-tuning objective improve persistence. Alternation: implies a harm policy that only needs to move the first tokens will find cheap wins; evaluation should include prefilling/decoding attacks. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.05946); [ICLR](https://www.iclr.cc/virtual/2025/oral/31915)
- **Refusal in Language Models Is Mediated by a Single Direction.** Andy Arditi. 2024. arXiv:2406.11717 (v1 17 Jun 2024, v3 30 Oct 2024). Across 13 open chat models up to 72B, one residual-stream direction mediates refusal; ablating it removes refusal, adding it induces refusal on harmless prompts; adversarial suffixes work by suppressing it. Authors say this "underscore[s] the brittleness of current safety fine-tuning". Alternation: none; a white-box probe that an adversarial-training paper could use to check whether robustness is more than a stronger refusal direction. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11717)
- **Fine-tuning Aligned Language Models Compromises Safety, Even When Users Do Not Intend To!** Xiangyu Qi. 2023/2024. ICLR 2024 (oral). arXiv:2310.03693. Fine-tuning GPT-3.5 Turbo on 10 adversarial examples for under $0.20 removes refusal for nearly any harmful instruction; benign fine-tuning also degrades safety, to a lesser degree. Alternation: any robustness bought by an adversarial loop is subject to the same erasure unless the loop targets fine-tuning persistence (cf. Qi 2024, Patcher 2606.07970 below). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03693v1); [ICLR](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2024/oral/19735)

**Attacker mode collapse / low diversity in RL red teaming**

- **Evolving Diverse Red-team Language Models in Multi-round Multi-agent Games.** Chengdong Ma. 2023/2024. arXiv:2310.00322 (v1 30 Sep 2023, v5 28 Jul 2024). Argues single-round prompts and one-sided optimisation against a fixed blue team cause mode collapse; proposes a multi-round Red Team Game and a Gamified Red Team Solver with diversity measures, claimed to converge to an approximate Nash equilibrium; finds the red-team game geometry matches the "spinning top" hypothesis, supporting a population of red-teamers. Alternation: the earliest LLM paper to frame red/blue as a multi-round game and to tie collapse to a fixed opponent. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.00322)
- **Curiosity-driven Red-teaming for Large Language Models.** Zhang-Wei Hong. 2024. ICLR 2024. arXiv:2402.19464. RL attackers "keep producing the same few successful test cases"; adds novelty rewards (SelfBLEU + embedding novelty) and gets more diversity at similar effectiveness. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.19464v1); [ICLR](https://dev.iclr.cc/virtual/2024/poster/19471)
- **Learning diverse attacks on large language models for robust red-teaming and safety tuning.** Seanie Lee. 2024/2025. ICLR 2025. arXiv:2405.18540 (v1 28 May 2024, v3 31 Aug 2026). States that RL attackers "suffer from mode collapse or fail to generate effective attacks" even with explicit novelty incentives; GFlowNet fine-tuning plus MLE smoothing yields diverse, transferable attacks, and models safety-tuned on them resist other RL red-teamers. Alternation: offline (attack set then safety tune). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.18540)
- **Diverse and Effective Red Teaming with Auto-generated Rewards and Multi-step Reinforcement Learning.** Alex Beutel (OpenAI). 2024. arXiv:2412.18693 (24 Dec 2024). Splits red teaming into generating diverse goals and then effective attacks per goal; rule-based rewards per goal plus a multi-step RL diversity reward against the attacker's own past attempts. OpenAI's blog states the attacker was rewarded by moderation models, rule-based rewards and a diversity reward, and (per a secondary summary) was a GPT-3.5-class model targeting a fixed GPT-4 Turbo. Alternation: attacker trained against a fixed target; no defender update. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.18693); [OpenAI blog](https://openai.com/index/advancing-red-teaming-with-people-and-ai/)
- **Active Attacks: Red-teaming LLMs via Adaptive Environments.** Taeyoung Yun. 2025 **[RECENT]**. arXiv:2509.21947 (v1 26 Sep 2025, v2 4 Oct 2025). Diversity-seeking RL and GFlowNet attackers "often collapse to limited modes" once high-reward prompts are found; periodically safety-fine-tuning the victim on collected attacks lowers reward in exploited regions and pushes the attacker to new ones (an "easy-to-hard" curriculum); cross-attack success vs GFlowNets rises from 0.07% to 31.28% (>400x) for about 6% extra compute. Alternation: this is alternation used as an exploration device for the attacker, not as a defence; per-round forgetting is not measured. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.21947)
- In 2026 co-training: AdvGRPO finds "GRPO tends to reduce attacker diversity over training" (attacks converge on fictional thriller / crime-novel framings); controlled test Self-BLEU 0.42, embedding diversity 0.30, token entropy 1.02 without regularisation; entropy regularisation raised diversity (Self-BLEU 0.30) but cut ASR about 10 points (83.9 to 73.4). — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09701v2)
- Self-RedTeam measures the collapse it avoids: an Attacker-Only baseline's dominant tactic covers 86.3% of attacks (effective strategy count 1.9, strategy entropy 0.90) versus 29.8% (4.6 strategies, entropy 2.20) under self-play; attacker "revision faithfulness" in Attacker-Only drops to about 60%. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.07468v4)

**Reward / judge hacking inside the loop**

- AdvGame (Paulus 2025/26) argues pointwise judge scores are easier to hack than pairwise preferences; in its ablation a pointwise judge raised WildJailbreak ASR from 8.5 to 30.8 and lowered Arena-Hard 61.3 to 56.3. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.20806)
- CHASE observes a form of attacker reward gaming across iterations: raw attacker ASR rises 0.51 to 0.76 while Convincingness falls 4.79 to 2.38 and attacker StrongREJECT falls 0.47 to 0.22, i.e. the attacker bypasses the defender by sanitising intent; the multiplicative reward (bypass x intent) is the proposed fix. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.05523v1)
- AdvGRPO installs guardrails against anticipated exploits (zero attacker reward if the "prompt" is a direct answer; zero defender reward for language-switching evasion; gate the thinking reward on commitment to prevent self-censoring) but reports no observed hacking. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09701v2)
- Self-RedTeam says it does not observe reward hacking and offers judge-independent checks (details in a section I could not retrieve). — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.07468v4)

**Over-refusal and capability cost after adversarial training**

- Self-RedTeam: LLM-LAT (a latent adversarial training baseline) drops XSTest benign compliance from 0.924 to 0.004 on Llama3.1-8B-IT and AlpacaEval-2 to 9.68; Self-play + SFT keeps XSTest benign 0.948 but lowers WildJailbreak adversarial-benign compliance 0.984 to 0.908; Llama3.1-8B AlpacaEval-2 LC falls 24.74 to 21.41 (-13.5%); CoT-free Llama variants "show severe over-refusal"; pure-RL self-play on Qwen2.5-7B lowers AlpacaEval-2 to 20.73 and the SFT phase restores it to 34.93. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.07468v4)
- CHASE: 0% false refusal on 100 held-out Alpaca prompts but only 44.4% XSTest compliance on 250 safe prompts (96% on public-figure privacy, 8% on safe fiction/game contexts); MT-Bench 7.42 to 5.50. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.05523v1)
- AdvGame: WildJailbreak benign compliance Qwen 100.0 to 94.4, Llama 98.8 to 69.9 (the Llama drop is not discussed at length by the authors); Arena-Hard rises (Qwen 55.5 to 61.3, Llama 33.6 to 41.0). — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.20806)
- MAGIC: XSTest benign compliance "close to base levels"; MMLU falls for Qwen2.5-7B (0.342 to 0.308) and 14B (0.381 to 0.337) as reported. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.01539v2)
- AdvGRPO: XSTest compliance 95.6 vs 82.8 for Self-RedTeam; MMLU 73.4 (base 73.5); IFBench 29.7 (base 31.3). — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09701v2)
- Constitutional Classifiers (Anthropic): +0.38% absolute refusals on production traffic and 23.7% inference overhead (2025); CC++ 0.05% refusal rate and 40x compute reduction (2026). — [arXiv 2501.18837](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.18837); [arXiv 2601.04603](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04603)

**Oscillation / non-convergence / forgetting across rounds**

- AdvGame states the standard sequential procedure (train attacker, train defender on its prompts, repeat) "suffers from inefficiencies and potential instabilities inherent in alternating optimization", and that a fixed attacker yields lower safety reward and worse jailbreak robustness than a co-trained one; no oscillation curve is shown. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.20806)
- ARLAS trains the agent against attacker checkpoints sampled uniformly from all previous iterations "to prevent cyclic learning, where an agent forgets defenses against earlier attacks"; the evidence is indirect (ARLAS without population-based learning performed like the ART baseline and did not reduce attack success on out-of-distribution AgentDojo). — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.05442)
- Self-RedTeam reports the defender's harmlessness win rate "stabilizes near 100%", an early phase where the defender underperforms before improving, and a degenerate mode where a Llama checkpoint began refusing to play attacker around step 200 (in under 10% of checkpoints; attacker refusal about 25% for Llama3.1-8B-IT vs about 0.1% for Qwen). Training is 300 steps with early stopping at 200. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.07468v4)
- Self-RedTeam also reports the opposite failure for the non-adversarial baseline: Defender-only "converges quickly to near-perfect harmlessness on static attacks but generalizes poorly out of distribution". — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.07468v4)
- CHASE's GRPO-variance problem: safety-aligned defenders are overconfident, so within-prompt reward variance is near zero and GRPO gives weak gradients; after partial hardening the defender still refused only 48% of adversarial rewrites, which is why an SFT consolidation stage was added. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.05523v1)
- MART reports violation rate "up to 84.7%" lower after 4 rounds on a weakly aligned model with stable helpfulness; its abs page says nothing about diminishing returns per round. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.07689)
- Classic RL precedent: Zhang et al. (NeurIPS 2020) found that naively transplanting classification-style adversarial training "is ineffective for many RL tasks" and formalised the SA-MDP; the ICLR 2021 follow-up ATLA alternates a learned optimal adversary and the policy, with no convergence guarantee for the alternation. — [arXiv 2003.08938](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.08938); [arXiv 2101.08452](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.08452)

**Robustness that does not survive fine-tuning**

- Qi 2023 (above) and Qi 2024 (above). Also **Defending Against Malicious Finetuning by Scaling Train-time Adversarial Attacks (Patcher).** 2026 **[RECENT]**. arXiv:2606.07970 (v1). Bi-level adversarial training against the fine-tuning threat model; details beyond the search snippet UNVERIFIED. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07970v1)

### Inferences
- "Collapse" shows up in two forms that an alternating scheme must monitor separately: tactic collapse (few modes) and intent collapse (bypass without harm, which the judge still rewards). CHASE's multiplicative reward and AdvGame's pairwise reward are the two existing mitigations.
- The over-refusal cost is model-family dependent (Llama far worse than Qwen in Self-RedTeam and AdvGame), so reporting a single family's XSTest number is not sufficient.
- No LLM paper I found measures round-over-round breach-rate oscillation directly; the claims are prospective. This is a genuine gap a new study could fill with per-round curves.

### Gaps
- No quantitative demonstration of cycling/oscillation between rounds in an LLM safety loop (ARLAS calls it hypothetical; AdvGame asserts it).
- DART: the arXiv ID 2407.03876 that search engines label "DART: Deep Adversarial Automated Red Teaming for LLM Safety" (ar5iv title) resolves on arxiv.org to "Automated Progressive Red Teaming" (APRT), first author Bojian Jiang, v3 21 Dec 2024, COLING 2025; I infer the paper was renamed between versions but did not confirm this. APRT numbers: 54% unsafe-but-useful responses from Llama-3-8B-Instruct, 50% GPT-4o, 39% Claude-3.5. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.03876); [ar5iv (old title)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2407.03876)
- PAD (**Purple-teaming LLMs with Adversarial Defender Training**, Jingyan Zhou, 2024, arXiv:2407.01850): GAN-style attacker/defender loop where the defender flags unsafe responses and explains why; search snippet only, numbers UNVERIFIED. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01850v1)

## Key Question 3: The 2025-2026 frontier: online self-play safety RL, GRPO/RLVR attacker and defender training, agents and tool use, industry reports

### Takeaway
Between June 2025 and October 2026 the field moved from offline multi-round loops (MART-style) to online two-player RL: Self-RedTeam (one shared policy, REINFORCE++), then a wave of 2026 papers that deliberately decouple attacker and defender parameters and alternate GRPO updates (MAGIC, AdvGRPO, CHASE), or train jointly with a non-zero-sum game and pairwise rewards (AdvGame), with ARLAS and GPT-Red extending the recipe to tool-using agents and to population-based defenders at OpenAI's largest-ever safety RL scale. Only two papers explicitly use one base for both roles (Self-RedTeam: shared weights; ACE-Safety: same backbone), and the 2026 decoupled papers argue shared weights cause gradient conflict.

### Cited Findings

**Online self-play and co-training (text)**

- **Chasing Moving Targets with Online Self-Play Reinforcement Learning for Safer Language Models (Self-RedTeam).** Mickel Liu. 2025/2026 **[RECENT]**. ICML 2026 poster. arXiv:2506.07468 (v1 9 Jun 2025, v4 6 Jul 2026). One policy plays attacker and defender with hidden chain-of-thought for each role, trained online with REINFORCE++ (critic-free, token-level KL 0.01, role-specific advantage normalisation, lr 5e-7 to 5e-8), WildGuard-7B as the three-label judge (query harm, response harm, refusal), 26,000 RL prompts (50:50 harmful/benign WildJailbreak) plus 30,000 SFT examples, 300 steps with early stop at 200; within each round the attacker moves first and the defender responds, and one summed loss updates the shared policy. - **MAGIC: A Co-Evolving Attacker-Defender Adversarial Game for Robust LLM Safety.** Xiaoyu Wen. 2026 **[RECENT]**. arXiv:2602.01539 (v1 2 Feb 2026, v2 6 Feb 2026). ### Inferences
- The design space in 2026 has three axes: (i) shared vs decoupled parameters (Self-RedTeam/SSP/ACE-Safety vs MAGIC/AdvGRPO/CHASE/AdvGame/ARLAS); (ii) simultaneous vs alternating updates (Self-RedTeam, AdvGame, GPT-Red vs MAGIC, AdvGRPO every 10 steps, CHASE per iteration, ARLAS per iteration with a checkpoint population); (iii) zero-sum vs non-zero-sum reward (Self-RedTeam, ARLAS vs AdvGame). A scheme with same-base but separately updated attacker and defender in alternation sits between ACE-Safety and MAGIC and is not identical to any paper I found.
- Across papers the strongest robustness numbers on WildJailbreak/DAN come from joint non-zero-sum training with pairwise rewards (AdvGame 8.5/10.3 vs AdvGRPO 21.2/24.0 per AdvGRPO's own table), while the strict-alternation papers report better over-refusal (AdvGRPO XSTest 95.6).
- The per-round reporting the scheme needs already exists in CHASE (3 iterations) and ACEA (per-round attack/defence decomposition); Self-RedTeam and MAGIC do not give per-round curves in the text I could retrieve.

### Gaps
- MAGIC's K rounds and per-phase step counts (appendix not retrievable in this session).
- ACE-Safety and SSP numbers and RL algorithms (abs pages only).
- GPT-Red: no numbers, no primary OpenAI page, affiliation inferred from the models named.
- No Meta/Llama or Anthropic policy-training (as opposed to classifier) report on in-loop RL red teaming was found in this pass.
- No paper found that trains attacker and defender from the same base with separate weights updated in strict alternation and reports per-round breach rates; ACE-Safety (same backbone, joint) and MAGIC (separate, alternating) are the nearest.

## Key Question 4: Connections to constrained / safe RL with high-confidence guarantees, and adversaries that define the evaluation distribution

### Takeaway
The Seldonian line (Thomas et al. 2019; HC-RLHF 2025) gives high-confidence guarantees on a constraint evaluated on held-out data from a fixed distribution; Safe RLHF / constrained RLHF give Lagrangian expected-cost constraints without confidence bounds; robust constrained RL (Bossens 2023, SA-MDP/ATLA 2020-21) and the 2026 LLM game papers supply the adversary. I found no paper that closes the loop by certifying a constraint under a distribution produced by a learned adversary; C3LLM certifies under fixed distributions, and the self-play papers give equilibrium (not finite-sample) guarantees.

### Cited Findings

- **Preventing undesirable behavior of intelligent machines.** Philip S. Thomas. 2019. Science 366(6468):999-1004. DOI 10.1126/science.aag3311. Defines Seldonian algorithms: the user specifies undesirable behaviour and a probability bound; the algorithm returns a solution only if a safety test on held-out data certifies, with probability at least 1-delta, that the constraint holds, else "No Solution Found". Alternation: none; the guarantee is for the data distribution the safety set was drawn from, which is exactly what a learned adversary would change. — [UMass open-access page](https://aisafety.cs.umass.edu/paper); [PDF](https://people.cs.umass.edu/~brun/pubs/pubs/Thomas19science.pdf)
- **Safe Policy Improvement with Baseline Bootstrapping (SPIBB).** Romain Laroche. 2017/2019. ICML 2019 (long oral). arXiv:1712.06924. Batch RL that falls back to the baseline where state-action counts are low; Pi_b-SPIBB carries a safe-policy-improvement guarantee (bound statement not captured on the abs page). Alternation: none; relevant as the "knows-what-it-knows" template for restricting defender updates to well-covered regions of the adversary's distribution. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.06924)
- **Safe RLHF: Safe Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback.** Josef Dai. 2023/2024. ICLR 2024. arXiv:2310.12773. Separates helpfulness and harmlessness preferences, trains a reward model and a cost model, and solves max reward s.t. expected cost constraint by Lagrangian dual ascent during PPO on Alpaca-7B. Alternation: the dual variable is updated in alternation with the policy, but the cost distribution is fixed (no adversary). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12773v1); [ICLR](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/dd1577afd396928ed64216f3f1fd5556-Abstract-Conference.html)
- **Confronting Reward Model Overoptimization with Constrained RLHF.** Ted Moskovitz. 2023/2024. ICLR 2024. arXiv:2310.04373 (v1 6 Oct 2023, v2 10 Oct 2023). Composite rewards built from several reward models over-optimise individual components; constrained RL with Lagrange multipliers keeps each component below its usefulness threshold. Alternation: primal-dual alternation; no adversary. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.04373v2); [ICLR](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2024/poster/18133)
- **Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback with High-Confidence Safety Constraints (HC-RLHF).** Yaswanth Chittepu (senior author Philip S. Thomas). 2025 **[RECENT]**. RLC 2025. arXiv:2506.08266 (9 Jun 2025). Seldonian-style RLHF: reward and cost models as in Safe RLHF, optimisation under a pessimistic cost constraint, then a safety test against an upper confidence bound on true cost; proven not to return an unsafe model with probability above a user threshold; Qwen2-1.5B, Qwen2.5-3B, LLaMa3.2-3B. Numbers not on abs page. Alternation: none; the cost distribution is fixed human-preference data. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.08266)
- **Robust Lagrangian and Adversarial Policy Gradient for Robust Constrained Markov Decision Processes.** David M. Bossens. 2023/2024. arXiv:2308.11267 (v1 22 Aug 2023, v3 15 May 2024). Robust CMDPs with transition uncertainty; the adversary picks worst-case dynamics with respect to the Lagrangian (objective plus constraint), learned incrementally by gradient descent (Adversarial RCPG), rather than by sorting values; top-two on all inventory and safe-navigation tests. Alternation: adversary and policy updated by alternating gradient steps; the constraint is enforced under the adversary's chosen dynamics. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.11267)
- **Robust Deep Reinforcement Learning against Adversarial Perturbations on State Observations.** Huan Zhang. 2020. NeurIPS 2020. arXiv:2003.08938. Formalises the state-adversarial MDP; finds classification-style adversarial training ineffective in many RL tasks and proposes policy regularisation instead. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.08938)
- **Robust Reinforcement Learning on State Observations with Learned Optimal Adversary (ATLA).** Huan Zhang. 2021. ICLR 2021. arXiv:2101.08452. For a fixed policy an optimal adversary exists and can be learned; ATLA alternates policy-gradient updates of the learned adversary and the agent; no convergence guarantee for the alternation on the abs page. Alternation: the direct RL precedent for "train the worst-case adversary against the current policy, then the policy against it". — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.08452)
- 2026 robust-constrained items seen as snippets only (UNVERIFIED): TCRL, temporally coupled adversarial perturbations in constrained RL, arXiv:2602.13040 (Feb 2026); Optimistic Policy Learning under Pessimistic Adversaries with regret and violation guarantees (RHC-UCRL), arXiv:2604.14243; RADAR, Lagrangian min-max adversarial training for safety-critical control (preprint, not on arXiv). — [arXiv 2602.13040](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2602.13040); [arXiv 2604.14243](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.14243)
- Equilibrium-level guarantees in the LLM papers: Self-RedTeam (Nash equilibrium implies safe defender, expectation-level), MAGIC (subgame-perfect NE implies pointwise non-negative safety reward given a safe fallback), GRTS (approximate NE convergence claim). None is a finite-sample bound. — [Self-RedTeam](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07468); [MAGIC](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.01539v2); [GRTS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.00322)
- Certified evaluation under a fixed distribution: C3LLM (Key Question 1) and a probabilistic SmoothLLM certificate (arXiv:2511.18721, revised Mar 2026; "(k, epsilon)-unstable" assumption; UNVERIFIED beyond snippet). — [C3LLM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.03969); [SmoothLLM certificate](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2511.18721v3)

### Inferences
- The natural synthesis for the scheme under survey is Seldonian safety test + adversary-generated safety set: HC-RLHF already has the test and the cost model; ATLA/Bossens already have the alternating adversary; what is missing in the literature is the statement that the certified quantity is the breach rate under the adversary's final (or population-mixture) distribution, and an argument for why that distribution is the right one to certify against (it is non-stationary across rounds, which is precisely the ARLAS "cyclic forgetting" concern).
- The 2026 LLM game papers' equilibrium guarantees are orthogonal to Seldonian guarantees: one is about the limit of training, the other about a held-out test on finite data. A paper could offer both.

### Gaps
- No source found that uses a learned adversary to define the distribution over which an LLM safety constraint is certified or bounded with a confidence level (searched arXiv terms "high-confidence", "certified", "learned adversary", "worst-case distribution").
- HC-RLHF's numerical results and its relation to Safe RLHF were not retrieved (abs page only).
- SPIBB's exact bound statement not captured.
