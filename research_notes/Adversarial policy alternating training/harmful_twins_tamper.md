# Harmful twins of the same base model: tamper-resistance inner loops and harmful-twin contrast methods (2022 to Oct 2026)

Scope note. "Harmful twin" = a deliberately harmful version of the SAME base (or instruct) model used while training, defending, or attacking the aligned model. Two families: (1) tamper-resistance / harmful-fine-tuning (HFT) defences whose training loop simulates harmful fine-tuning of the model itself; (2) methods that use a harmful twin at training or decoding time as a contrast, reference, or adversarial signal. A third group (adversarial co-training / self-play) is included because it is the only place where the twin is trained AGAINST the aligned model with RL.

Verification status. All arXiv IDs below were pulled from the arXiv API (export.arxiv.org) on 2026-10-17 unless marked UNVERIFIED. Details marked "(abstract only)" come from the arXiv abstract; details with no such mark come from the paper's full text (arxiv.org/html). Two full-text fetches were refused by the fetch tool's content filter (AntiDote via WebFetch, SafeAligner); AntiDote was recovered by direct text extraction, SafeAligner was not.

The two fields that matter most are given for EVERY record as **RL-against-aligned?** and **Alternating?**.

## Key question 1: The tamper-resistance line (inner-loop simulated harmful fine-tuning, meta-learning / bilevel / alternating schemes), plus the attacks and evaluations that break it

### Takeaway
The tamper-resistance line runs from MLAC (BERT-tiny, 2022) through SOPHON (vision, 2024) to TAR (Llama-3-8B, 2024) and AntiDote (2025); only TAR, MLAC, SOPHON, MUDMAN and AntiDote have a genuinely multi-step or co-evolving inner adversary, and only AntiDote alternates adversary and defender updates in a GAN-like k:k schedule. Booster, CTRAP and SEAM use a one-step (first-order) simulated harmful update recomputed at every defender step; Vaccine, RepNoise, T-Vaccine, circuit breakers and LAT have no weight-space twin at all. No published defence in this line trains the twin with RL, and every one that has been re-evaluated independently (TAR, RepNoise, Booster, T-Vaccine, SEAM) has been broken by changed fine-tuning hyper-parameters, more seeds, abliteration, or prefilling.

#### Records, tamper-resistance line

**1. Self-Destructing Models: Increasing the Costs of Harmful Dual Uses of Foundation Models** — Peter Henderson (first author), 2022 (v1 ICML 2022 workshops) / 2023 (v2 AIES 2023); arXiv 2211.14946. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14946)
- Summary: Proposes "task blocking" and the MLAC algorithm (meta-learned adversarial censoring). At each meta-step an adversary fine-tunes a harmful-task head and the representation for K Adam steps (with a learned inner learning rate); the blocked parameters are updated to minimise the desired-task loss minus the average harmful-task loss across the inner steps, i.e. the harmful gradient is negated. A calibration step (linear projection with weights in [-1,1]) prevents the degenerate label-flipping solution. Experiments are on BERT-tiny with the Bias in Bios data (desired task: profession; blocked task: gender) and show harmful-task accuracy near a randomly-initialised model while profession accuracy is preserved; 16 inner steps already give near-random blocked-task performance. — [arXiv html](https://arxiv.org/html/2211.14946v2)
- (a) Twin produced by inner-loop fine-tuning (Adam, K steps, learned LR) on the harmful task. (b) Same base: yes, the copy starts from the current protected parameters at every meta-step. (c) Inner-loop adversary. (d) Nested meta-learning via the `higher` library; 50k meta-training steps; NOT GAN-style alternation (the adversary is re-run from the current model each step, not persisted). (e) Blocking holds up to the tested regimes; authors list stronger adversaries (prefix tuning, adapters) and scaling as untested. (f) Desired-task performance preserved or improved (model is trained on it). (g) Code: https://github.com/Breakend/SelfDestructingModels. **RL-against-aligned? No. Alternating? Nested inner/outer, not alternating.**

**2. SOPHON: Non-Fine-Tunable Learning to Restrain Task Transferability For Pre-trained Models** — Jiangyi Deng, 2024; IEEE S&P 2024; arXiv 2404.12699. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.12699)
- Summary: MAML-inspired "non-fine-tunable learning" for vision models. A fine-tuning-simulation loop samples (strategy, dataset, eval set) triplets from the restricted domain, runs K=50 simulated Adam steps from the current protected model with sampled LR (1e-6 to 1e-2) and batch sizes, and the outer loss is the restricted-domain loss after simulated fine-tuning (inverse cross-entropy or KL-to-uniform for classifiers; a denial-of-service noise-norm loss for diffusion) plus an original-task retention term, first-order (FOMAML-style). On CAFormer ImageNette→CIFAR-10, fine-tuned accuracy drops to 10.4-15.2% versus 62.7% train-from-scratch, with 96.2% original accuracy. — [arXiv html](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.12699)
- (a) Inner-loop SGD/Adam fine-tuning simulation. (b) Same base: yes (copy initialised from current model each loop). (c) Inner-loop adversary. (d) Nested; Iter=800 outer iterations, first-order approximation. (e) Limits: with 40-50 epochs and Adagrad/Adadelta/Adam the adversary reaches 49.8/78.8/75.6%, "only slightly better than training from scratch at ~10x effort"; no language models evaluated. (f) Original-domain accuracy 91-96% across five architectures. (g) https://github.com/ChiangE/Sophon. **RL-against-aligned? No. Alternating? Nested, not alternating.**

**3. Tamper-Resistant Safeguards for Open-Weight LLMs (TAR)** — Rishub Tamirisa, 2024; ICLR 2025 (per official repo title); arXiv 2408.00761. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.00761); [GitHub](https://github.com/rishub-tamirisa/tamper-resistance)
- Summary: Builds safeguards that survive "hundreds of steps" of fine-tuning on Llama-3-8B-Instruct. Each outer step starts a simulated SFT attack from the current defended model θ_{i-1}, runs K=64 inner steps (inner optimisers re-created each outer iteration), and reuses the K-1 intermediate checkpoints as extra non-independent attacks. The defender minimises λ_TR·E[L_TR(attack(θ))] + λ_retain·L_retain, where L_retain is an LM loss plus an ℓ2 penalty pulling retain-set hidden states toward the base model; the meta-gradient uses a straight-through / first-order MAML approximation. For weaponisation (WMDP bio/chem/cyber) L_TR is an entropy-based loss that is "eventually maximized in all inner loop steps"; for refusal it is a DPO loss. N=750 outer steps (weaponisation, LR 2e-5, λ_TR 3-4) or 100 (refusal, LR 6e-5, λ_TR 0.1); 8×A100-80GB. Reported post-attack WMDP forget accuracy averaged over 26 attacks: 28.1% bio, 28.4% chem, 23.6% cyber (chance 25%, undefended ~70%); refusal post-attack HarmBench ASR 63.9% vs 72.5% refusal-trained baseline. — [arXiv html](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.00761)
- (a) Twin = SFT fine-tuning attack trajectory (64 steps) on harmful/forget data (weaponisation) or Anthropic-HH (refusal). (b) Same base: yes, re-started from current defended weights each outer step. (c) Inner-loop adversary. (d) Nested inner (64) / outer (100-750); not GAN-style alternation; the adversary does NOT persist across outer steps. Negative cross-entropy (the MLAC loss) was rejected because it "can cause divergence" and the model learns to "explode the adversary's loss for the first few inner loop steps" while later losses stay low. (e) Limits reported by authors: weaker against PEFT and out-of-distribution learning-rate attacks; loss plateau ~500 steps; at 1000 steps robustness varied with dataloader shuffling (seed bug since fixed). Independent breaks: see records 19-22. (f) MMLU retain accuracy drops 10.6 points on average (bio 67.3→54.7); MT-Bench 8.1→6.3. (g) Code and models public. **RL-against-aligned? No. Alternating? No (nested meta-learning; adversary restarted each outer step).**

**4. Representation Noising: A Defence Mechanism Against Harmful Finetuning (RepNoise)** — Domenic Rosati, 2024; NeurIPS 2024; arXiv 2405.14577. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14577)
- Summary (abstract): removes information about harmful representations so it is hard to recover during fine-tuning; claims generalisation across unseen harm subsets from the same distribution and no capability loss. The survey describes it as lowering alignment loss, raising loss on harmful data, and pushing harmful-data representations toward Gaussian noise. — [Survey](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.18169v6)
- (a) No twin; gradient ascent + representation noising on harmful data. (b) n/a. (c) n/a. (d) Static, single-stage; **twin is static / not alternating**. (e) Broken: Qi et al. re-ran the official checkpoint with shuffling enabled and 5 seeds; at LR 6e-5 and 8e-5 worst-case harmfulness nearly matched undefended Llama-2-7B-Chat, a standard HF SFT trainer broke it more consistently than the custom trainer, and 100 benign examples restored much harmful-QA behaviour; the official codebase had disabled data shuffling and used greedy decoding. — [Qi et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.07097) (f) Claims no degradation (abstract). (g) Code: not confirmed in this session (UNVERIFIED). **RL-against-aligned? No.**

**6. Vaccine: Perturbation-aware Alignment for LLMs against Harmful Fine-tuning Attack** — Tiansheng Huang, 2024; NeurIPS 2024 (rejected ICML 2024 per arXiv comment); arXiv 2402.01109. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01109)

**8. Booster: Tackling Harmful Fine-tuning for LLMs via Attenuating Harmful Perturbation** — Tiansheng Huang, 2024; ICLR 2025 ([proceedings](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/a7ac8a21e5a27e7ab31a5f42a0117bdb-Paper-Conference.pdf)); arXiv 2409.01586. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.01586)

**12. Jailbreak to Protect: Buffering and Reinforcing via Temporary Jailbreaking for Safe Fine-Tuning in LLMs** — Seokil Ham, 2026 (RECENT); ICML 2026 Spotlight; arXiv 2605.24550. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24550)
- Summary: revisits temporary jailbreaking as an HFT defence with a gradient-level analysis: once the model is in a jailbroken state, safety-degrading gradients saturate while benign task gradients stay active (Safety Gradient Score on layers 0-15 of LLaMA3-8B-Instruct). BufferLoRA is a removable adapter trained once with next-token loss on 5,000 LAT harmful query/response pairs; it stays attached and frozen during user fine-tuning (only UserLoRA trains), then is removed. ReinforceLoRA is trained under the jailbroken state to refuse harmful queries and is merged with UserLoRA via QR-based soft orthogonalisation. Default: LLaMA3-8B-Instruct, GSM8K, 1,000 samples, 10% harmful: HS 8.1 / FA 76.6 vs SFT 75.2 / 69.0 and LDIFS 16.4 / 74.1; averages across data sizes HS 8.6 / FA 76.7; across architectures (Gemma2-9B-it, Qwen3-4B) HS 5.2 / FA 78.3. — [arXiv html](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.24550)
- (a) SFT on harmful data (LAT harmful set). (b) Same base: yes (LoRA on the defended model). (c) Decoding/training-time shield: twin state absorbs harmful updates. (d) **Twin trained once; static during user fine-tuning; not alternating.** (e) Depends on the base being jailbreakable; tested only 4B-13B; baselines LDIFS, SafeInstruct, Lisa, Security Vector, AsFT, SafeLoRA, Antidote, Panacea (not Vaccine/Booster). (f) User fine-tuning cost equals SFT (~3.9 min, 39 GB A100); one-time 30 min provider step. (g) Code not stated in visible text. **RL? No.**

**13. CTRAP: Embedding Collapse Trap to Safeguard LLMs from Harmful Fine-Tuning** — Biao Yi, 2025 (RECENT); arXiv 2505.16559. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.16559)
- Summary: instead of selective unlearning, induce model collapse in response to harmful updates. Objective: min_θ ℓ(θ; D_align) + λ·ℓ_collapse(θ − α∇ℓ(θ; D_harm); D_general), with α=0.1, λ=0.1; the collapse loss evaluated at the one-step-attacked θ' pushes outputs to a fixed token. Gemma2-9B / Llama2-7B / Qwen2-7B with LoRA; HS averages 5.2 / 10.4 / 1.5 vs Booster 39.0 / 29.7 / 16.5 and RepNoise 37.2 (Gemma). — [arXiv html](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16559)
- (a) One hypothetical harmful gradient step. (b) Same base: yes. (c) Inner one-step adversary. (d) **Simultaneous, recomputed each step; not alternating.** (e) Attack strength varied only by harmful count (100-500) and ratio (5-25%); LR fixed 1e-5, 20 epochs; TAR not a baseline. (f) Benign fine-tune accuracy unchanged; alignment costs ~3.5× GPU memory-time. (g) Anonymous repo https://anonymous.4open.science/r/CTRAP. **RL? No.**

**14. Self-Destructive Language Model (SEAM)** — Yuhui Wang, 2025 (RECENT); ICLR 2026; arXiv 2505.12186. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12186)
- Summary: couples the optimisation trajectories of benign and harmful data by minimising the cosine similarity between the harmful-data gradient and the benign (Alpaca) gradient, so harmful gradient descent acts like benign gradient ascent; plus a layer-wise, log-transformed gradient-ascent unlearning term and a refusal-utility term (L = L_ul + α·L_up + β·L_sd, α=1, β=1e-2), using a Hessian-free finite-difference estimate. Llama2-7b default; harmfulness after unseen-domain attack 11.7 at LR 2e-5 and 0.0 from LR 8e-5 up (base ~77-80), with zero-shot score collapsing to 26.6 at LR 2e-4 (the intended self-destruction). — [arXiv html](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.12186)
- (a) No separate harmful copy; gradients recomputed at current parameters. (d) **Simultaneous; not alternating.** (e) Broken without fine-tuning: Kuo et al. 2026 show abliteration pushes SEAM above 70% ASR on BeaverTails and above 90% on AdvBench/HarmBench on Llama3.1-8B. — [Kuo et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.26526v1) (f) Zero-shot drop ~0.8 points; XSTest incorrect refusal 0.24→0.16; training time 135.7 (units unspecified) vs 43.7 without coupling. ### Inferences
- The field has converged on first-order, one-step "simulated attacks" (Booster, CTRAP, SEAM, VaccineBooster) because the TAR-style 64-step nested loop is expensive and, per AntiDote's own framing, "computationally prohibitive"; the one-step simulation is a hypothetical twin that never persists and so cannot "re-adapt".
- Every independent benchmark (Qi 2024, Che 2025, TamperBench 2026, Kuo 2026) finds that the inner-loop defences buy at most tens to hundreds of steps and are defeated by hyper-parameter sweeps, LoRA, or weight-space edits that are not in the simulated attack family. Deep Ignorance's 10,000-step resistance comes from never learning the capability, not from a twin.
- AntiDote is the only tamper-resistance paper that both alternates and reports what happens when the adversary is static (3-5x worse), which is direct evidence for the "twin must re-adapt" hypothesis, but it has no independent replication and no code was found.

## Key question 2: The harmful-twin-as-contrast line (decoding-time steering, task-vector negation, losses that reference a harmful twin's distribution)

### Takeaway
Decoding-time contrast against a same-family harmful twin is an established attack (Emulated Disalignment, Weak-to-Strong Jailbreaking) and defence (DExperts anti-experts, SafeAligner's Intruder model); weight-space negation of a harmful twin's delta (task arithmetic, Ext-Sub, RESTA, Safety Arithmetic) restores safety after fine-tuning at a cost of ~0.5-2.4 points of utility; and the first training loss that explicitly uses a harmful same-base twin's token distribution as a negative reference is DR-SFT (2026). In every case the twin is produced once by SFT on harmful data (or is simply the pre-trained base) and is static; none trains the twin with RL and none alternates.

Utility drop ~2.41 points (PEFT) / ~0.47 (full) on XCOPA, XNLI, COPA, HumanEval, GSM8K. — [arXiv html](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.11746)
- (a) SFT on harmful data. (b) Same base: yes. (c) Weight-space vector added post hoc. (d) **Twin trained once; static; not alternating. RL? No.** Code https://github.com/declare-lab/resta.

**45. Safety Arithmetic: A Framework for Test-time Safety Alignment of LMs by Steering Parameters and Activations** — Rima Hazra, 2024; EMNLP 2024; arXiv 2406.11801. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11801)
- Harm Direction Removal: fine-tune Llama2-7b-chat on NicheHazardQA harmful pairs to get θ_H; harm vector τ_H = θ_H − θ_b; keep top-10% magnitude entries and subtract λ·τ'_H (λ 2-3). Then Safe-Align adds an in-context safety vector (first PC of safe-minus-unsafe hidden-state differences, α=0.12) at every layer with norm rescaling. AdvBench ASR: Llama2 19.81→6.15%, Mistral 60.96→24.23%, WizardMath 79.62→37.69%; XSTest over-refusal for Llama2 17.8→8.7%; MMLU 0.469→0.456. Only ≤7B models, GPT-4 judge. — [arXiv html](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.11801)
- (a) SFT on harmful data. (b) Same base. (c) Weight negation + activation steering. (d) **Static; not alternating. RL? No.** Code https://github.com/declare-lab/safety-arithmetic.

**46. Defending Against Harmful Supervision Hidden in Benign Samples (DR-SFT)** — Bang An, 2026 (RECENT; 29 Jun 2026); arXiv 2606.30263. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30263)
- Embedded Attack hides a harmful QA pair inside a benign sample (K=2) and evades example-level guardrails. Defence DR-SFT: token-level DPO-style loss L = −E Σ_t (1/β) log σ(β(r_p − η·r_n)) with r_p = log(π_θ/π_p^ref) against the aligned positive reference and r_n = log(π_θ/π_n^ref) against "a misaligned negative reference model obtained by fine-tuning aligned model on a toxic dataset" (one epoch on 2,500 PKU-SafeRLHF unsafe examples); η = 1 − 4·CLIP(σ(log π_p − log π_n)) so safe tokens train as plain SFT and harmful tokens are suppressed; β=0.001. Llama-2-7B-Chat / Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct / Gemma-3-4B-IT, full fine-tuning; SFT reaches 83.3% ASR on Qwen under the embedded attack, DR-SFT 1.3-2.9% on Llama-2; GSM8K utility 74.7→70.6 (Qwen) and 40.6→38.1 (Llama-2). Reference logits are precomputed, implying both references are fixed. Authors note the defence collapses to Constrained SFT if the negative model collapses to the positive one. — [arXiv html](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.30263)
- (a) SFT on 2,500 unsafe examples. (b) Same base: yes. (c) **Contrastive reference in the training loss (negative teacher).** (d) **Static (precomputed); not alternating. RL? No.** Code https://github.com/ABgit111/DR-SFT.

**47. Safety-Preserving PTQ via Contrastive Alignment Loss (CAQ)** — Sunghyun Wee, 2025 (RECENT); arXiv 2511.07842. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.07842)
- Push-pull loss steering a quantised model toward its instruction-tuned counterpart and away from the "unaligned, pre-trained reference"; twin = base model, no harmful training. **Static.**

**48. Safeguarding LLM Fine-tuning via Push-Pull Distributional Alignment (SOT)** — Haozhong Wang, 2026 (RECENT); arXiv 2601.07200. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.07200)
- Optimal-transport sample weighting that pulls the downstream distribution toward a safe anchor and pushes it from "a general harmful reference"; whether the reference is a model or a dataset is UNVERIFIED (abstract reads as data-level). **Static.**

### Inferences
- The twin-as-contrast literature treats the harmful twin as a fixed probe of "the direction alignment moved", which is why the same object serves both attack (ED, weak-to-strong) and defence (SafeAligner, RESTA, DR-SFT); the sign of the contrast is the only difference.
- ED's theorem (contrast emulates KL-regularised RL against a safety reward) implies that a decoding-time twin already approximates an RL-trained harmful twin without any RL, which may explain why nobody in this line bothers to RL-train one.

### Gaps
- SafeAligner's Intruder construction, decoding formula and results could not be fetched (content filter); ACD (2406.16743) details likewise unverified.
- No paper was found that uses an "unlikelihood" loss against a harmful twin's samples by that name; DR-SFT is the closest (token-level DPO-style reference).
- Search terms "negative teacher", "anti-expert distillation" returned nothing beyond DExperts/Ext-Sub.

## Key question 3: Which papers train the harmful twin with RL (reward for harm), and does any train the twin AGAINST the aligned model (reward for making the aligned model breach)?

### Takeaway
Inside the tamper-resistance and contrast families the answer is none: every twin is made by SFT on harmful data, by a hypothetical gradient step, by a hypernetwork trained with DPO (AntiDote), or is the base model. Twins trained with RL against the aligned model exist only in the automated red-teaming / self-play literature, where the "twin" emits adversarial prompts rather than being a harmful policy: Perez 2022 (same Gopher LM, A2C against a fixed target), Self-RedTeam 2025 (one set of weights plays attacker and defender, zero-sum REINFORCE++), Safety Self-Play 2026, GRPO co-training 2026 (different bases) and GPT-Red 2026 (different models). Token Buncher 2025 shows an RL-trained harmful twin (reward for harm, not against the aligned model) is a stronger attack than SFT.

### Cited Findings
- Perez et al. 2022 (arXiv 2202.03286): red LM and target both use the Gopher LM (target = Dialogue-Prompted Gopher; "we also use the Gopher LM" for the red LM); the red LM is trained with synchronous A2C warm-started from the supervised model, reward −log p(1−r) from a 1.4B Gopher offensiveness classifier on the target's reply, −3 if no "?", KL penalty to the initial LM with α ∈ {0.3, 0.35, 0.4}; the target is never updated ("joint training of the two models is presented as future work"); at α=0.3 the red LM elicits offensive replies >40% of the time but 78% of its test cases contain the word "invisible" (mode collapse); zero-shot 0.5M cases elicited 3.7% offensive replies (18,444 failures). **RL-against-aligned: YES. Alternating: NO (target static).** — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2202.03286)
- Self-RedTeam (Mickel Liu, arXiv 2506.07468, ICML 2026): "a single LLM (πθ) that plays the role of both attacker and defender", roles set by instructions; Qwen2.5-3B/7B/14B-Instruct, Llama3.1-8B-Instruct and an abliterated Llama3.1-8B; REINFORCE++ with token-level KL and role-specific advantage normalisation; attacker reward is the negative of the defender's (rewarded when the defender answers harmfully or refuses a benign prompt) plus CoT-format and intent-preservation shaping; the loss sums both roles' clipped objectives so **both roles are in one combined update (simultaneous, shared weights), not alternating**; 26k WildJailbreak RL prompts + 30k SFT examples; Qwen2.5-7B WildGuardTest adversarial ASR 0.303→0.179, WildJailbreak 0.864→0.489; PAIR ASR on 14B 0.218→0.108; AlpacaEval-2 LC for Qwen2.5-14B 36.91→38.09 but Llama3.1-8B 24.74→21.41; self-play costs 44-48% more time than defender-only; attacker-only training shows diversity collapse. Code https://github.com/mickelliu/selfplay-redteaming. **RL-against-aligned: YES (zero-sum).** — [arXiv html](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.07468)
- Safety Self-Play (Hao Wang, arXiv 2601.10589, ACL Findings 2026): "a single LLM to act concurrently as both the Attacker ... and the Defender" within a unified RL loop with a safety judge reward and reflective experience replay (UCB sampling of failures). Abstract only. **RL-against-aligned: YES (abstract). Alternating: shared weights (UNVERIFIED schedule).** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.10589)
- Learning to Attack and Defend via GRPO (Blake Bullwinkel, arXiv 2606.09701, Microsoft, v2 7 Oct 2026): attacker Qwen2.5-7B/14B or Qwen3.5-9B, defender Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (**different bases**); attacker GRPO reward scores whether the defender's response satisfies the harmful objective (**trained against the defender: YES**); multiple LLM-judge reward channels combined with GDPO; curriculum from attacker-only (vs GPT-4.1) to co-training where **updates alternate every N=10 steps**; attacker diversity collapses to fictional thriller framings and token-entropy bonuses did not fix it; co-training stable, defender HarmBench ASR 18.8→4.7%, WildJailbreak benign compliance 99.2→88.8%. — [arXiv html](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09701)
- GPT-Red (Eric Wallace, arXiv 2607.26115, 28 Jul 2026): attacker attacks "a diverse population of simultaneously-trained defender agents"; attacker initialised from an internal helpful-only model and defender from a smaller GPT-5 series model (**not same base**); prompt-injection focus; used to adversarially train GPT-5.6; "single-largest LLM safety training run ever documented" (abstract). **RL-against-aligned: YES.** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26115)
- Token Buncher (Feng, 2508.20697): harmful RL fine-tuning with a harm reward (GRPO/PPO/RLOO/REINFORCE++) beats harmful SFT at matched budgets (Qwen2.5-3B HarmBench 45.75% vs 38.00%; 25.2% smaller capability drop). **The twin is RL-trained for harm, not against the aligned model.** — [arXiv html](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.20697)
- Rosati et al., Evaluating Defences against Unsafe Feedback in RLHF (arXiv 2409.12914, 2024): treats harmful fine-tuning via unsafe RLHF feedback rather than SFT (abstract only; details UNVERIFIED). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.12914)
- Non-RL twins trained against the defender: AntiDote's hypernetwork (DPO, maximises harmful-response likelihood under the patch, against the current defender) — [AntiDote](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.08000v1); PAD's attacker (DPO: prompt that elicits unsafe response preferred) — [PAD](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01850); SEAS's red team (DPO on prompts that made the target unsafe) — [SEAS](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.02632); MART's adversarial model (SFT on successful prompts) — [MART](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.07689).
- Separate-attacker RL red-teaming against static targets: Curiosity-driven RT (Zhang-Wei Hong, ICLR 2024, 2402.19464) and GFlowNet red-teaming (Seanie Lee, ICLR 2025, 2405.18540, "existing approaches suffer from mode collapse"; code https://github.com/GFNOrg/red-teaming); APRT (Bojian Jiang, COLING 2025, 2407.03876); Jailbreak-R1 (Weiyang Guo, 2506.00782, RL with diversity balancing). None uses the target's weights as the attacker. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.19464); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.18540); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.03876); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.00782)
- Adversarial Preference Optimization (Pengyu Cheng, ACL Findings 2024, 2311.08045): LLM and reward model "update alternatively via a min-max game"; the adversary is the RM, not a harmful twin. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.08045)
- Emulated Disalignment is the only method with a formal link between a same-base contrast and RL against a (negated) safety reward, but it trains nothing. — [ED](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.12343)

### Inferences
- A harmful policy twin trained by RL with the reward "make the aligned model breach" (i.e. the twin's own outputs cause the defended model to fail) does not appear in the literature; the closest objects are (i) prompt-generating attackers (Perez, Self-RedTeam, GRPO co-training) and (ii) AntiDote's weight-patch generator trained with DPO. A method that RL-trains a weight- or activation-space harmful twin against the defender would be novel relative to everything found here.
- Where attacker and defender share weights (Self-RedTeam, SSP), the "twin" is not a separate harmful model at all; it is the same policy under a different instruction, so weaknesses are shared by construction.

### Gaps
- GRTS (Ma et al. 2310.00322) describes a red-blue game with Nash convergence but base models and training algorithm were not retrieved.
- Rosati 2409.12914 details (which defences, how RL is applied) were not retrieved.
- Whether Safety Self-Play updates both roles in one combined gradient (like Self-RedTeam) or alternates is unverified.

## Key question 4: Which papers alternate between updating the twin and updating the aligned model, and what do they report about stability and about the twin re-adapting?

### Takeaway
True alternation (adversary updated, then defender, repeatedly) appears in AntiDote (k:k interleaving; static-adversary ablation 3-5x worse), MART (4 rounds; stopped when the attacker's success fell to ~10% because no training data was left), PAD (2-3 iterations, GAN-style), SEAS (3 DPO rounds), GRPO co-training (every 10 steps; attacker diversity collapse) and Self-RedTeam (combined update of shared weights). TAR, MLAC and SOPHON restart the adversary from the current model every outer step rather than letting it persist, so the twin "re-adapts" only in the sense that each outer step attacks the latest defender; Booster, CTRAP and SEAM use a one-step hypothetical twin that cannot re-adapt at all; the decoding/task-vector methods never update the twin.

### Cited Findings
- AntiDote: "The training proceeds in an interleaved k:k schedule, where we alternate between optimizing the adversary and the defender. This dynamic co-evolution is critical: a static adversary would quickly become obsolete as the defender learns. By periodically re-training the adversary against the improving defender, we ensure the model is hardened against a continuously adapting threat"; AntiDote-Static ablation: HS 33.1 vs 5.7 (Llama-3.2-3B), 31.5 vs 9.1 (Gemma-3-12B), 34.8 vs 9.9 (Gemma-3-27B); defender LR 3e-5 chosen "to ensure stable convergence"; capability loss computed on the clean model "ensuring they are not contaminated by the presence of the adversarial patch". — [AntiDote](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.08000v1)
- TAR: each outer step starts the attack from θ_{i-1} and inner optimisers are re-created each outer iteration; negated cross-entropy diverged and let the adversary recover after a few inner steps, motivating the entropy loss that is "eventually maximized in all inner loop steps"; test-time loss plateau ~500 steps; at 1,000 steps robustness varied with shuffling (seed bug). — [TAR](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.00761) Qi et al. attribute TAR's effect in some configs to early gradient explosion that warm-up removes. — [Qi et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.07097)
- MLAC: adversary re-started from current θ̃ at every meta-step; calibration step needed to stop a degenerate label-flipping solution. — [MLAC](https://arxiv.org/html/2211.14946v2)
- SOPHON: simulated copy starts from the current protected model each loop; "no instability in about 200 runs with ICE and KLU", but CE and MSE losses "showed NaN and divergence in failure cases". — [SOPHON](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.12699)
- MART: adversarial and target LLM "interplay with each other in an iterative manner"; both initialised from the same LLaMA-65B (LIMA + Open Assistant) checkpoint; per round the adversarial model is SFT-trained on (earlier prompt, newer successful prompt) pairs and the target on safe+helpful responses; stopped at iteration 4 because the violation rate on adversarial generations neared 10%, "leaving too little training data for another round"; RM violation rate 31.4→4.8%, human-rated 17.2→8.0%; HelpEval 3-4% lower at iteration 4. — [MART](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.07689)
- PAD: attacker and defender both LoRAs (rank 128) on Qwen1.5-7B-Chat; attacker DPO, defender SFT; "update both modules in a generative adversarial network style"; two full iterations plus a defender-only round; each attacker version raised ASR and each defender version lowered it (PADv2 attacker 89.29% HitbyT3 vs Base; PADv3 defender 0.99% mean ASR); no collapse analysis; multi-turn defence remained weak; over-rejection fell from 39.2% to 11.25%. — [PAD](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01850)
- SEAS: red team and target both from Llama-3-8B; three rounds of DPO on each; SEAS-Test ASR 62.20→7.00%; red-team ASR against Llama3-70B-Instruct up 50.66%; MT-Bench 7.088→7.447 (no helpfulness loss reported); code https://SEAS-LLM.github.io/. — [SEAS](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.02632)
- GRPO co-training: alternates every N=10 steps; "co-training runs were stable, and defender helpfulness stayed high"; attacker strategy diversity collapsed and entropy bonuses were hard to tune. — [Bullwinkel](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09701)
- Self-RedTeam: single combined update over both roles; attacker-only training shows diversity collapse and ~60% revision faithfulness; self-play without CoT has the lowest attack diversity; defender-only reaches near-perfect harmlessness on static attacks but generalises worse; no explicit cycling reported. — [Self-RedTeam](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.07468)
- Perez 2022: RL attacker vs fixed target; low KL weight (α=0.3) gives 78% of cases containing "invisible" — single-mode convergence. — [Perez](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2202.03286)
- Lisa (no twin): alternating between alignment-state and user-state optimisation "experiences convergence instability when steps invested in its alignment state is too small", attributed to "excess drift towards consensus", fixed with a proximal term and a convergence analysis. — [Lisa](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.18641)
- Token Buncher alternates the entropy-as-reward objective and the Token Noiser objective after a 1/8 warm-up (two defender objectives, no twin). — [Token Buncher](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.20697)
- Static twins by construction (not alternating): RESTA ("kept consistent across the fine-tuning domain and evaluation benchmarks"), Safety Arithmetic, DR-SFT (precomputed reference logits), BufferLoRA (frozen during user fine-tuning), weak-to-strong, ED, DExperts, Ext-Sub, task arithmetic. — [RESTA](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.11746); [DR-SFT](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.30263); [BufferLoRA](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.24550)

### Inferences
- The only quantitative evidence on "does the twin need to re-adapt" is AntiDote's static ablation (3-5x worse HS) and the red-teaming diversity-collapse results; the tamper-resistance papers avoid persistent adversaries, apparently for compute and because a persisted adversary that keeps winning yields exploding/negated losses (TAR's divergence note, MLAC's calibration fix).
- Reported instabilities cluster into three kinds: (1) loss divergence when the defender maximises the adversary's loss (TAR, SOPHON CE/MSE), (2) attacker mode collapse under RL (Perez, GFlowNet paper, GRPO co-training, Self-RedTeam attacker-only), (3) data starvation ending the alternation (MART at round 4). No paper reports oscillation/cycling between twin and defender.

### Gaps
- No paper measures how quickly a persisted harmful twin re-adapts to an updated defender (steps-to-recover); AntiDote reports only the end-state ablation.
- AntiDote's value of k and the number of alternations are not stated in the extracted text.

## Key question 5: How much does a same-base harmful twin reveal about the aligned model's weaknesses (shared representations, shallow alignment, refusal direction)?

### Takeaway
Evidence from several directions says the aligned/unaligned difference is low-dimensional and shallow, so a same-base twin is highly informative: refusal is mediated by a single residual-stream direction across 13 chat models up to 72B (Arditi), alignment mostly changes the first few output tokens (Qi), ~3% of parameters / 2.5% of ranks carry safety (Wei), a 7B harmful twin transfers to 70B and a 1.3B pruned twin still reaches 74% ASR (weak-to-strong), and three principal components explain 89% of attack-success variance across tampering attacks (Che). The same shallowness is what lets abliteration and prefilling defeat TAR and SEAM without any fine-tuning (Kuo 2026).

### Cited Findings
- Refusal in Language Models Is Mediated by a Single Direction (Andy Arditi, arXiv 2406.11717): one direction per model; erasing it prevents refusal, adding it induces refusal on harmless prompts; white-box jailbreak with minimal capability effect; adversarial suffixes suppress propagation of the direction. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11717)
- Refusal Feature Adversarial Training (Lei Yu, arXiv 2409.20089): refusal-feature ablation "approximates the worst-case perturbation of offsetting model safety"; ReFAT trains against simulated RFA. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.20089)
- Shallow alignment: "safety alignment can take shortcuts, wherein the alignment adapts a model's generative distribution primarily over only its very first few output tokens" (Qi, 2406.05946); weak-to-strong's premise that "jailbroken and aligned models only differ in their initial decoding distributions" (Zhao, 2401.17256). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.05946); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.17256)
- Brittleness via pruning/low-rank (Boyi Wei, arXiv 2402.05162): safety-critical regions are ~3% of parameters and ~2.5% of ranks, disentangled from utility; removing them breaks safety without utility loss. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.05162)
- Safety Layers (Shen Li, ICLR 2025, 2408.17003): a small set of contiguous middle layers distinguishes malicious from normal queries; code https://github.com/listen0425/Safety-Layers. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.17003)
- Safety basin (ShengYun Peng, NeurIPS 2024, 2405.17374): random weight perturbations keep safety inside a local basin, outside which safety drops "sharp, step-like". — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17374)
- Transfer across scale within a family: weak-to-strong 7B→13B/20B/70B >99% ASR; 1.3B pruned twin 74.0% on Llama2-70B; shared vocabulary is the stated requirement. — [Zhao](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.17256) ED: within-family pairs work best but Llama-1/Llama-2/Alpaca cross-family pairs with the same vocabulary also work. — [ED](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.12343)
- Che et al.: three principal components explain 89% of attack-success variation across 64 unlearned checkpoints; embedding, latent, pruning and benign-fine-tuning attack success correlate positively with input-space attack success; adversarial fine-tuning matched or beat the best input-space attack on 64/65 models. — [Che](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.05209)
- Łucki et al. (TMLR, 2409.18025): fine-tuning on 10 unrelated examples or removing specific activation-space directions recovers most "unlearned" hazardous capability. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.18025)
- Distillation Robustifies Unlearning (Bruce W. Lee, NeurIPS 2025 Spotlight, 2506.06278): even idealised unlearning (imitating a model never trained on the data) is reverted by a few fine-tuning steps because training changes input-output behaviour "while leaving its underlying capabilities intact"; UNDO distils into a noised copy of the same model. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.06278)
- Halloran (2604.02574, Apr 2026, RECENT): compares jailbreak-tuned and weight-orthogonalised unaligned versions of six LLMs; weight orthogonalisation yields models "much more capable of aiding malicious activity" (search summary; full text not fetched). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02574)
- Kuo 2026: abliteration (gradient-free refusal-direction removal) defeats TAR and SEAM, i.e. the single refusal direction survives tamper-resistance training. — [Kuo](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.26526v1)
- TAR's own retain loss pulls hidden states toward the base model, and AntiDote's adversary reads the defender's activations, both implicitly assuming the twin and defender share representational geometry. — [TAR](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.00761); [AntiDote](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.08000v1)

### Inferences
- Because the aligned-vs-twin delta is low-rank, shallow and concentrated in a refusal direction, a same-base harmful twin is close to a sufficient statistic for the aligned model's weight-space weaknesses; this is why static twins already work as attacks (ED, weak-to-strong) and why defences that only train against SFT-style twins remain open to direction-removal attacks they never simulated.
- The flip side for defences: a twin made by SFT on harmful data explores only the "learn harm" direction, whereas abliteration/prefilling exploit "elicit harm already present", which no twin in the tamper-resistance line represents (Kuo's ART is the first to simulate that family).

### Gaps
- No paper directly measures mutual information or transfer between a harmful twin's failure set and the aligned model's failure set at fixed base; the evidence is indirect (transfer of jailbreaks across scale, PCA of attack success).
- Halloran 2026 full text and numbers were not retrieved.
