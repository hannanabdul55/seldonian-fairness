# Adversarial policy alternating training: literature survey

Date: 2026-10-17. Compiled from five research notes in `research_notes/Adversarial policy alternating training/` (alternating_training.md, rl_attackers.md, rl_theory_adversarial_policies.md, harmful_twins_tamper.md, evaluation_failures_frontier.md). Every number below carries the source link the notes recorded. Fields the notes flagged UNVERIFIED are marked as such and were not resolved by guessing. Citation keys in square brackets refer to `reports/literature_adversarial_policy.bib`.

**Bottom line.** The sketch under review, a harm policy initialised from the same base model as the main model, trained separately with reinforcement learning (RL) to make the instruction-tuned main model breach a safety constraint, and then alternated against that main model in a generative adversarial network (GAN) style loop, is **already occupied in its most natural reading**. If the harm policy emits adversarial prompts and its reward comes from a guard-model judge, then three 2026 papers sit on exactly that combination of axes: TriPlay-RL trains three separate instances of one Qwen3 base in strict rotation with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.18292)) [tan2026triplay]; MAGIC trains separate attacker and defender policies from the same model family in strictly alternating GRPO phases ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.01539)) [wen2026magic]; and AdvGRPO's 7B run trains two Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) policies on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, alternating every 10 steps ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09701v1)) [bullwinkel2026advgrpo]. The lineage goes back to MART in 2023, which initialised both roles from one LLaMA-65B checkpoint and alternated for four rounds with a supervised rather than RL attacker ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.07689)) [ge2023mart], and to Perez et al. in 2022, who proposed but did not run GAN-like joint training of red and target models ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2202.03286)) [perez2022red]. What the notes could **not** find anywhere is (a) a harm policy that acts as a response-generating harmful twin and is RL-trained against the aligned model, (b) an attacker reward defined as a verifiable breach of a stated constraint in the content-harm setting, (c) any measurement of how fast a persisted same-base attacker re-adapts after a defender update, (d) any quantitative demonstration of round-to-round oscillation in a language-model safety loop, and (e) any certificate of a constraint's breach rate under an adversary-generated distribution. As a defence the recipe works but at a price: MAGIC cuts WildGuardTest attack success rate (ASR) from **36.5% to 2.3%** on Qwen2.5-7B ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.01539)), while the strongest RL loops lose benign compliance (CHASE XSTest compliance **44.4%** ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.05523)); AdvGRPO's v1 reported WildJailbreak benign compliance falling **99.2 to 72.8**, but its v2 of 7 Oct 2026 replaced that run with one that keeps benign compliance at 95.6 and has WildJailbreak ASR 31.7 instead of 7.5 ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09701v2))) and transfer unevenly to held-out attack families (CHASE: AutoDAN down 74.9% but PAIR down only 19.9% ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.05523v1))). As an attack, an RL attacker against a frozen target collapses onto a few modes (78% of Perez's RL prompts contained the word "invisible" ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2202.03286))), and the moving defender is the device the 2025 to 2026 papers use to keep it exploring (Active Attacks: cross-attack success **0.07% to 31.28%** ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.21947)) [yun2025activeattacks]). The theory and the RL precedent say alternating best response need not converge: a toy robust-classification game cycles while a robust Nash equilibrium exists ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.12606)) [balcan2023nash], and nine rounds of attacker-then-victim training in Go still left a fresh adversary winning **59%** against the final victim at 26% of its compute ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.12843v3)) [tseng2025go].

## Scope and how the notes were verified

The notes cover text and language-model (LM) settings from 2022 to October 2026, plus the RL and minimax theory the LM papers lean on. ArXiv identifiers were read from arxiv.org pages or the arXiv API on 2026-10-17; anything else is marked UNVERIFIED. Two conventions matter when reading the catalogue. First, "same base" is used in three distinct senses in the literature: the same initial checkpoint with separate updates (MART, DART, APL, TriPlay-RL), one frozen backbone with separate adapters (Anchored Bipolicy Self-Play), and literally shared weights under two role prompts (Self-RedTeam, Safety Self-Play). Second, "alternating" ranges from one combined gradient over both roles (Self-RedTeam) through alternation every 10 steps (AdvGRPO) or every 1,000 attacker steps (Active Attacks) to full fine-tuning rounds (MART). No paper the notes found compares these granularities directly ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09701)).

One bibliographic trap deserves a warning. "DART: Deep Adversarial Automated Red Teaming" (Jiang et al. 2024) exists only as version 1 of arXiv 2407.03876; the same record was later retitled "Automated Progressive Red Teaming" (APRT, COLING 2025), an attacker-only method in which "the target is not trained" ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.03876v3)) [jiang2025aprt]. Cite the version you mean ([Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.03876v1)) [jiang2024dart].

## Taxonomy: five axes locate every paper relative to the sketch

The five axes below are the ones on which the sketch is fixed or silent. Where the sketch is silent the comparison with prior work depends on the choice, and the report says so rather than filling the gap.

**Axis (i): is the attacker a trained model, and is it trained with RL?** Three families exist. Search or agentic attackers train no weights: Rainbow Teaming's quality-diversity search ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.16822v3)) [samvelyan2024rainbow], FLIRT and JAB's in-context exemplar updating ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.09473)) [mehrabi2023jab], X-Teaming's planner and verifier agents ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13203)), and the 2026 "training-free" red-blue games RvB and EvoSafety. Supervised or preference-trained attackers include MART (supervised fine-tuning, SFT, on successful parent-to-child prompts), DART (SFT under a diversity filter), PAD and SEAS (Direct Preference Optimization, DPO), APL (DPO), and AntiDote's hypernetwork (DPO). RL attackers run from Perez's advantage actor-critic (A2C) through Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) in Casper 2023, CRT and the Red Teaming Game, Generative Flow Networks (GFlowNets) in Lee 2024 and Active Attacks, REINFORCE++ in Self-RedTeam and ABS, and GRPO in MAGIC, TriPlay-RL, AdvGRPO, CHASE, ARLAS and AgentSafeEvo. The sketch fixes this axis at "RL". The specific algorithm is unspecified, and the notes show it matters: GRPO shows "pronounced instability" in AdvGame ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.20806v3)) [paulus2025advgame], near-zero gradients against overconfident defenders in CHASE ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.05523)) [markasserithodi2026chase], and attacker entropy collapse in AdvGRPO ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09701v1)).

**Axis (ii): does the attacker share the base or checkpoint, share weights, or differ from the defender?** Shared initialisation with separate updates is the norm in the loop papers: MART (LLaMA-65B fine-tuned on LIMA and Open Assistant for both), DART (Llama-2-7b-hf for both), PAD (two LoRAs on Qwen1.5-7B-Chat), APL (both from pi_ref), SEAS (Llama-3-8B), TriPlay-RL (three Qwen3 instances), AgentSafeEvo (Qwen3-8B), and the Red Teaming Game (stablelm 3B for red, blue, critic and toxicity model) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.00322v5)) [ma2023redteaminggame]. Shared weights appear only in Self-RedTeam and Safety Self-Play; ABS keeps one frozen backbone and trains disjoint adapters. Different models are used by CHASE (Hermes 4 14B against Llama-3.1-8B), Active Attacks (Qwen2.5-1.5B base against the 1.5B-Instruct checkpoint), GFlowNet red-teaming, CRT, Beutel 2024, and AdvGame's Llama runs, which use an abliterated attacker because "safety-tuned Llama refuses to generate adversarial attacks" ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.20806v3)). The sketch fixes "same base, separate weights". Whether "base" means the pre-SFT base model or the SFT checkpoint is unspecified. The two readings have different precedents: Active Attacks pairs a base attacker with an instruct victim, whereas MART, APL and TriPlay-RL start both roles from the same tuned checkpoint.

**Axis (iii): is the defender updated, and on what schedule?** Attack-only papers freeze the target (Perez, CRT, GFlowNet, Beutel, Jailbreak-R1, RL-Hammer, PISmith). One-round papers attack and then fine-tune once (Rainbow Teaming, GFlowNet red-teaming, DiveR-CT). Strict alternation with full retraining per round appears in MART (4 rounds), DART (0 to 7 iterations), PAD (2 plus a defender-only round), SEAS (3), APL (30 iterations of 8 steps each side), CHASE (3 iterations) and MAGIC (defender then attacker phases, switching every 15 steps at a 1:1 update ratio, per its Appendix C.1 ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.01539))). Fine-grained alternation appears in AdvGRPO (every 10 steps, for 300 co-training steps in v1 and "up to 500 steps" in v2 ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09701v2))) and Active Attacks (victim update every 1,000 of 5,000 attacker steps). Simultaneous updates appear in Self-RedTeam (one summed loss), Safety Self-Play, AdvGame (both updated each iteration with exponential moving average opponents), AgentSafeEvo and GPT-Red. Population or meta-game schedules appear in the Red Teaming Game (PSRO-style best responses), ARLAS (agent trains against all past attacker checkpoints) and GPT-Red (defender population). Inner/outer meta-loops appear where the attacker is an optimiser rather than a policy: continuous adversarial training (CAT), latent adversarial training (LAT), Tamper-Resistant Safeguards (TAR) and the Madry formulation. The sketch fixes "strict alternation"; granularity is unspecified and uncompared in the literature.

**Axis (iv): does the attacker produce prompts or act as a harmful policy?** Almost every LM paper's attacker produces prompts: single-turn rewrites (MART, APL, MAGIC, CHASE), multi-turn dialogue (Red Teaming Game, AdvGRPO multi-turn, X-Teaming), indirect injections into tool outputs (ARLAS, GPT-Red, Gemini report), or whole tool-use tasks (AgentSafeEvo). Harmful twins that act as response policies exist but are static and SFT-made: the weak-to-strong jailbreak's 7B unsafe twin ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.17256)) [zhao2024weaktostrong], RESTA and Safety Arithmetic's harm vectors, DR-SFT's misaligned negative reference ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.30263)) [an2026drsft]. The one RL-trained harmful response policy, Token Buncher's harmful RL fine-tune, is rewarded for harm directly, not for making the aligned model fail ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.20697)) [feng2025tokenbuncher]. AntiDote's adversary emits a weight patch rather than text and is trained with DPO ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.08000v1)) [sanyal2025antidote]. The sketch is silent here. This is the axis on which novelty turns, as the novelty section explains.

**Axis (v): what is the attacker's reward?** Classifier or guard-model judges dominate: Perez's offensiveness classifier, Llama-Guard-2 in Active Attacks, WildGuard in Self-RedTeam and ABS, Qwen3Guard in MAGIC, ShieldLM in PAD, GPT-4.1 channels in AdvGRPO, pairwise LLM judges in AdvGame, a 1 to 5 LLM score in Safety Self-Play, and an evolving Eval model in TriPlay-RL. Target log-probability rewards appear in APL, whose attacker reward is the defender's own log-odds of the dispreferred over the preferred response ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.24369)) [wang2025apl], and in the AdvPrompter and GFlowNet reference-likelihood terms. Verifiable breach rewards appear in agent settings: ARLAS pays a sparse terminal reward for a secret leak ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.05442)) [wang2025arlas], GPT-Red rewards "validated failures" and scores invalid attacks zero ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.26115)) [wallace2026gptred], and the ACEA arena seeds canonical secrets so leakage is ground-truthed ([Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.08256)) [shen2026acea]. The sketch says "breach of a stated constraint" without saying how the breach is detected; if a judge detects it, the sketch inherits every judge-gaming result below.

## The co-training lineage: from supervised loops to online RL games

### 2023 to 2024 loops used a shared checkpoint and a supervised attacker

**MART** (Ge, 2023, NAACL 2024) is the canonical offline loop ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.07689)) [ge2023mart]. An adversarial model writes prompts, the target answers, a safety reward model and a helpfulness reward model score the pairs, and both models are re-fine-tuned each round. Both start from the same LLaMA-65B checkpoint fine-tuned on LIMA and Open Assistant. The attacker is supervised on (previous successful attack, new attack) pairs, not trained by RL. Violation on SafeEval falls **31.38% to 4.79%** over four iterations and human-judged violation falls 17.2% to 8.0%, while HelpEval is "3%-4% lower" at iteration 4. The loop stops at round 4 because attack success on adversarial generations nears 10% and there is too little training data for another round; the attacker "tends to converge on the generated adversarial prompts". No KL anchor is used and no code link was found.

**DART** (Jiang, 2024, arXiv v1 only) retrains a red model and a target model from their previous checkpoints each iteration, with the red side filtering new data under a global BLEU-diversity constraint and the target side keeping only "hard" samples via an active-learning rule ([Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.03876v1)) [jiang2024dart]. Both start from Llama-2-7b-hf. Human-evaluated violation on Anthropic Harmless falls **34.3% to 16%**, beating a reimplemented MART at 20%. The ablation that matters for the sketch is that selecting data with a fixed initial red or target model is worse than using the latest states, and an "aggressive" non-diverse red model's attack strength decays from iteration 5. The stopping rule is helpfulness decay; the authors re-annotated 10,000 Anthropic Harmless samples because reward-model scores "were not credible".

**PAD** (Zhou, 2024) is a GAN-style pipeline with a DPO attacker and an SFT defender, both LoRAs on Qwen1.5-7B-Chat, judged by ShieldLM because GPT-4 refused to judge ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01850v1)) [zhou2024pad]. After two attacker/defender iterations plus a defender-only step, ASR against PAIR falls 70.62% and over-refusal falls **39.2% to 11.25%** of inquiries. Defence weakens with dialogue turn (per-turn ASR cuts of 41, 37 and 29 points at turns 1 to 3) and the defender often detects an unsafe request yet still answers it. **SEAS** (Diao, 2024) runs three DPO rounds on a red team and a target both initialised from Llama-3-8B; SEAS-Test ASR falls 62.20% to 7.00% and MT-Bench rises 7.088 to 7.447 ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.02632)) [diao2024seas].

**APL** (Wang, 2025, Findings of ACL 2025; first author UNVERIFIED in one note) is the judge-free member of this family ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.24369)) [wang2025apl]. Attacker and defender both run DPO from the same pi_ref, and the attacker's reward is the defender's own log-odds of the dispreferred over the preferred response minus a reference baseline. Over 30 alternating iterations on Mistral-7B, direct-request ASR falls **56.25% to 11.25%** and GCG ASR 67.50% to 41.25%, but XSTest over-refusal doubles from **20.00 to 40.89** and MT-Bench slips 6.78 to 6.59. The win rate plateaus after a peak; oscillation is not discussed. **Deng et al.** (2023, EMNLP Findings) is the earliest loop in the notes that fine-tunes the victim "through iterative interactions" with an in-context attacker, with no trained attacker weights ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12505)) [deng2023attackprompt].

### Shared-weight self-play arrived in 2025 and drew two critiques in 2026

**Self-RedTeam** (Liu, 2025, ICML 2026 poster) trains one policy that plays both attacker and defender roles, fully online. It does not alternate: in each step both roles' rollouts go into one batch and both gradients go into a single optimiser step (v4 Sec. 4.3, Alg. 1). It uses a critic-free PPO variant called REINFORCE++, a token-level KL penalty to a reference, and WildGuard-7B as a three-label judge ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.07468v4)) [liu2025selfredteam]. Rewards are zero-sum between roles, and "role-specific advantage normalization" is described as critical for one model to learn from opposite reward signals. Against a defender-only-plus-SFT baseline it adds **17.33%** safety robustness with AlpacaEval-2 length-controlled win rate 38.09 versus 35.50; WildGuardTest adversarial ASR on Qwen2.5-7B falls 0.303 to 0.179. That baseline is diluted by SFT: on DAN and HarmBench adversarial prompts the final method is worse than plain defender-only RL on Qwen2.5-3B, Qwen2.5-7B and Llama3.1-8B (DAN 0.330 versus 0.186 on Qwen2.5-3B) (v4 Tables 2 and 6 to 9) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.07468v4)). An attacker-only ablation collapses into "a few dominant modes" whose top tactic covers 86.3% of attacks; a defender-only ablation converges on static attacks but fails out of distribution. Benign seeds are needed to stop a refuse-everything defender, and a Llama checkpoint began refusing to play attacker around step 200. Headline numbers changed across arXiv versions (v1/v2 reported +21.8% SBERT diversity, v4 reports +17.80%), so cite by version ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07468)).

**Safety Self-Play** (Wang H., 2026, ACL 2026 Findings) also uses one model for both roles with zero-sum clipped rewards from a 1 to 5 judge, and adds an experience pool replayed with an upper confidence bound (UCB) rule that prioritises low-reward failures ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.10589)) [wang2026ssp]. Without the pool the authors report "a cycle of catastrophic forgetting" with volatile mirrored reward curves. Qwen2.5-7B ASR under GCG is 1.7 versus 4.7 without replay, and the "w/o UM" ablation that splits the roles into two co-evolving models is worse at 5.8. The capability cost is real: MMLU **79.5 to 72.6**, HumanEval 84.8 to 78.0, GSM8K 91.6 to 87.2. This ablation is the only evidence in the notes that favours weight sharing.

**Anchored Bipolicy Self-Play (ABS)** (La Malfa, 2026) argues that the shared-weight update is grad J_D plus grad J_A on the same parameters, so a defender step shifts the attacker's objective by roughly eta times the gradient inner product and pushes the attacker "away from prompts it already defends", a failure the authors call self-consistency ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08427v1)) [lamalfa2026abs]. The fix is a frozen backbone with two role-specific LoRA adapters trained with the Self-RedTeam recipe. Attacker tournament success against its own base rises from **18% to 35%** at 3B and 9% to 24% at 7B versus Self-RedTeam. Safety is better at 3B and 7B (WildGuard adversarial ASR 0.122 versus 0.234 at 3B) but worse at 14B (0.131 versus 0.116). Side effects include LoRA collapse into repeated "!" tokens and much longer attacks (232.7 versus 59.3 tokens).

**ACE-Safety** (Li X., 2025) trains attack and defence LMs together from "the same LLM backbone" with group-aware strategy-guided Monte Carlo tree search and a curriculum tree-aware group policy optimisation; numbers and the exact schedule were not on the abstract page and are UNVERIFIED ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.19218)) [li2025acesafety].

### Separate-policy RL co-training is the dominant 2026 design

**The Red Teaming Game / GRTS** (Ma, 2023, v5 2024) is the first paper in the notes with an RL attacker against an RL defender ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.00322v5)) [ma2023redteaminggame]. Red and blue are trained with PPO as best responses against the other side's population meta-strategy, a PSRO or double-oracle scheme with a semantic-diversity term, and all roles use the stablelm 3B family with a KL coefficient of 0.04. Exploitability falls from about **6.2 to about 0.8 over 15 iterations**; blue's round-1 ASR drops 52% to 1% and settles near 10% at equilibrium; a trained red raises ASR against an untrained blue from 40% to 70%. The appendix reports "a relatively slow and moderate adversarial oscillation" under a normal toxicity model and "significantly more severe mode collapse" under an over-fitted one. No helpfulness numbers for blue are reported and no code link was found.

**MAGIC** (Wen, 2026) is the clearest strict-alternation GRPO paper ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.01539)) [wen2026magic]. The attacker is SFT-warmed on a SorryBench pool with Gemini-2.5-Pro chain of thought, then defender and attacker phases alternate with the other side frozen. Appendix C.1 (Table 6), since read in full, gives the schedule: both roles start from the same instruct checkpoint, the defender phase comes first, phases switch every 15 steps at a 1:1 update ratio, and the run is 300 steps with an early stop at step 200. It also sets "KL loss coeff beta = 0", so MAGIC has no KL anchor even though its Eq. 6 contains a KL term. Qwen3Guard supplies the reward; the defender's term R_ref penalises both refusing safe queries and answering unsafe ones. Qwen2.5-7B WildGuardTest ASR falls **36.5% to 2.3%**, WildJailbreak adversarial harm 70.1 to 19.8, unseen GCG 43.90 to 11.25, PAIR 44.38 to 25.31, but multi-turn X-Teaming only 94.9 to 85.4. AlpacaEval-2 holds at 7B (33.7 to 33.2) and falls at 14B (38.1 to 31.0). The paper argues shared parameters "inevitably lead to gradient conflicts", notes that "new exploits resurface", and declines to report a diversity metric on the grounds that diversity is not a reliable proxy. Code is at github.com/BattleWen/MAGIC.

**TriPlay-RL** (Tan, 2026) is the closest match to the sketch on axes (i) to (iii) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.18292)) [tan2026triplay]. Three separate instances of one Qwen3 base (4B, 8B or 14B) play Red, Blue and Eval and are updated in strict rotation with GRPO-based reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards; KL beta is 0.01. Red's reward mixes judge scores against a weighted set of defenders (the current Blue at 0.6, Llama-3.1-8B at 0.2, GLM-4.6 at 0.2), a semantic-preservation term and a Self-BLEU and cosine diversity penalty. Red trains 200 steps per iteration for 16 iterations; Blue and Eval 50 steps per phase. Blue's AIR-Bench ASR falls **13.9% to 4.4%** and JailbreakBench 31.5% to 4.6% after 10 iterations with LiveCodeBench, GPQA, AIME25 and IFEval within one or two points; Red's ASR on the base Qwen3-8B rises 21.84% to 67.75%. Without the closed loop and the diversity penalty Red's diversity score falls from 0.588 to 0.004. Over-refusal is not measured, evaluator error "may be amplified over iterations", and a Nash analysis is left open.

**AdvGRPO** (Bullwinkel, Microsoft AI Red Team, 2026) trains separate LoRA attacker and defender with GPT-4.1 judge channels, after a staged curriculum of single-turn then multi-turn attacker-only training ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09701v1)) [bullwinkel2026advgrpo]. Co-training alternates every N = 10 steps. Version 1 ran 300 co-training steps with a KL coefficient of 0.1 and said the players reach equilibrium "After 7-8 alternations" ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09701v1)). Version 2 (7 Oct 2026) runs "up to 500 steps" with KL 0.1 for the attacker and 0.05 for the defender, and says instead that rewards "fluctuate until converging after around 500 steps" ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09701v2)). The 7B run uses Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct for both roles. Decoupled advantage normalisation (GDPO) z-scores each reward channel within its group, offered as the fix for the GRPO instability AdvGame reported; without it WildJailbreak ASR is 30.4 plus or minus 14.9 ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09701v2)). Version 2 replaced the v1 defender numbers. For the Qwen2.5-7B defender trained against the 7B attacker, HarmBench ASR is **8.1** in v2 (0.9 in v1) and WildJailbreak ASR **31.7** (7.5 in v1), from a base HarmBench of 18.8, while WildJailbreak benign compliance is 95.6 (72.8 in v1) and XSTest 95.6 (88.8 in v1). Against the 14B attacker the v2 defender reaches HarmBench 4.7 and WildJailbreak 21.2 (1.5 and 9.1 in v1) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09701v2)). Version 1 claimed the lowest ASR "on all benchmarks"; v2 concedes that "AdvGame performs better on WildJailbreak and DAN". Version 1 bought its safety lead with over-refusal, and v2 keeps benign compliance but gives up the lead. Initialising both roles from scratch "causes the defender to dominate", which is why the attacker is pre-trained first. The paper states that GRPO "tends to collapse attacker diversity over training" and that entropy regularisation "explodes" at beta 0.15.

**CHASE** (Markasserithodi, UNSW, 2026) trains a Hermes 4 14B attacker with GRPO under a multiplicative reward (bypass times intent fidelity) against a Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct defender hardened each round by GRPO then rejection-sampled SFT mixed 1:1 with Alpaca ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.05523)) [markasserithodi2026chase]. Mean StrongREJECT over five held-out families falls **0.479 to 0.272 to 0.162 to 0.115** across three iterations, with the attacker retrained against each defender checkpoint. Costs are heavy: MT-Bench 7.42 to 5.50 and XSTest safe-prompt compliance 44.4%. The attacker's convincingness decays 4.79 to 2.83 to 2.38 across rounds as it learns to sanitise intent, and GRPO gives near-zero gradients on an overconfident defender, hence the SFT consolidation stage. CHASE is also the rare paper that reports judge calibration against humans (Pearson 0.841 on 100 pairs) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.05523v1)).

**AdvGame** (Paulus, Meta FAIR, 2025, v3 2026; venue UNVERIFIED) rejects alternation outright: it names "inefficiencies and potential instabilities inherent in alternating optimization" and instead trains attacker and defender simultaneously with online preference objectives in a deliberately non-zero-sum game ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.20806v3)) [paulus2025advgame]. On harmful seeds the attacker is rewarded for defender compliance and on benign seeds for defender deflection, which avoids rewarding gibberish. Parameters are not shared because "a model capable of generating harmful attacks with the correct prompting will necessarily be unsafe when used as a Defender". Qwen HarmBench ASR falls **31.6 to 4.7**, WildJailbreak 69.6 to 8.5, with Arena-Hard rising 55.5 to 61.3; the Llama run loses benign compliance 98.8 to 69.9. Ablations: a fixed attacker is worse (WildJailbreak 16.6 versus 8.5), no-EMA on-policy sampling gives 49.5, a pointwise judge gives 30.8 (suspected reward hacking), and GRPO showed "pronounced instability".

**Active Attacks** (Yun, 2025, ICML 2026 poster) uses the defender update as an exploration device for the attacker ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.21947)) [yun2025activeattacks]. The victim is LoRA-fine-tuned on its own refusals to the attacker's collected prompts every 1,000 of 5,000 attacker steps, which removes reward from exploited regions; the attacker is reset to the reference model and its replay buffer cleared each round. The attacker is Qwen2.5-1.5B base and the victim Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct. Final defence rate is **99.71% versus 91.00%** for plain GFlowNet red-teaming, cross-attack success rises 0.07% to 31.28%, and the extra compute is 5.76%. Baseline attackers collapse onto one category, and the same Llama-Guard-2 classifier serves as reward and evaluator, a circularity the notes flag.

**ARLAS** (Wang Z., with Peter Stone, 2025) frames indirect prompt injection as a two-player zero-sum Markov game: an attacker writes injections into tool outputs and an agent defends while completing tasks, both trained with GRPO after SFT warm-up ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.05442)) [wang2025arlas]. The agent trains against a uniform sample of all previous attacker checkpoints "to prevent cyclic learning, where an agent forgets defenses against earlier attacks"; without the population, ASR did not drop (6.5 or 6.6 versus 6.3 base). SPAG, an iterative baseline without a population, ties ARLAS on AgentDojo (ASR 5.4 versus 5.4), so the population is not shown to be necessary out of distribution ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.05442)). AgentDojo ASR on Gemma3 12B falls 6.3 to 5.4 with task success rising 24.4 to 25.8 over 15 iterations. **AgentSafeEvo** (Wang C., 2026) has the attacker build whole multi-turn tool-use tasks and the defender finish them safely, both GRPO from Qwen3-8B with a zero-sum task-level reward plus utility; Agent-SafetyBench ASR falls **49.11 to 17.84** while AgentDojo utility rises 53.64 to 66.52, and the final attacker reaches 92.59% ASR on the base defender ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.19422)) [wang2026agentsafeevo].

### Industry loops confirm the recipe at scale but publish few mechanics

**GPT-Red** (Wallace, OpenAI, 2026) trains a GPT-5.5-initialised attacker by self-play against "a diverse population of strong defenders" that are "simultaneously-trained", and uses it to adversarially train GPT-5.6 against prompt injection in what the authors call the largest safety RL run they have documented ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.26115)) [wallace2026gptred]. Hard direct prompt-injection robustness rises **5.2% to 95.9%** relative to GPT-5.1 and held-out automated red-teaming ASR is "below 4%". Single-defender training narrowed attacks and attacker-only training collapsed, hence the population; "defenders sometimes patch an attack, causing ASR dips before new strategies emerge". Whether defenders share the attacker's base is not stated and over-refusal numbers were not in the read portion.

**Lessons from Defending Gemini** (Shi, Google DeepMind, 2025) fed "many thousands" of triggers from TAP, Beam Search and Actor-Critic attackers into SFT "plus adversarial reinforcement learning", with the adaptive suite run "continuously against past, current, and future versions of Gemini" ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.14534)) [shi2025gemini]. Email-scenario TAP ASR falls **99.8% to 53.6%**, but the held-out Calendar tool still sees TAP at 94.6%, and the report is explicit that training and evaluation used the same attack algorithms, so "protection against unknown attacks cannot be quantified" ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.14534v1)). **Constitutional Classifiers** (Sharma, Anthropic, 2025) is a classifier-side defence with a human bug bounty rather than a closed retraining loop; over 3,000 red-teaming hours found no universal jailbreak on the prototype, production refusals rose 0.38 percentage points, and inference compute rose 23.7% ([Source](https://www.anthropic.com/research/constitutional-classifiers)) [sharma2025constitutional]. The 2026 follow-up reports 0.05% production refusal and a 40x compute reduction ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04603)) [cunningham2026constitutional].

### Optimiser attackers are adversarial training without an attacker policy

For completeness, a parallel line is adversarial training in Madry's min-max sense where the inner maximiser is a gradient or latent optimiser rather than an LM policy, so nothing there can "share a base". **Ziegler et al.** (2022, NeurIPS 2022) ran three rounds of human-plus-tool attacks against an injury classifier; time per successful attack doubled from 13 to 26 minutes, in-distribution false negatives stayed at 2 to 3 of 2,447, and attacks on the final classifier worked nearly as well on earlier ones, meaning failures were pre-existing rather than introduced; an LM adversary trained with PPO "suffered from mode collapse" ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2205.01663)) [ziegler2022adversarial]. **CAT/CAPO** (Xhonneux, 2024; venue UNVERIFIED) perturbs prompt embeddings in a 10-step inner loop and cuts Zephyr-7B GCG ASR 75 to 5 at about 299x less compute than R2D2, with MMLU 60.3 to 56.7 ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.15589v3)) [xhonneux2024cat]. **Targeted LAT** (Sheshadri, 2024) perturbs residual-stream activations; Llama-2-7B-chat PAIR ASR falls 0.177 to 0.025 at about 36x fewer GPU hours than R2D2 ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.15549v3)) [sheshadri2024lat], but Self-RedTeam reports the released LLM-LAT checkpoint drops XSTest benign compliance from 0.924 to 0.004 ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.07468v4)). Casper's untargeted LAT ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.05030)) [casper2024lat] and Robust Prompt Optimization's minimax suffix ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.17263)) [zhou2024rpo] round out the family.

## Attack side: a learned attacker needs a moving opponent to stay diverse

The attack-only literature is where the sketch's harm policy inherits its failure modes, so it is worth seeing how each generation tried to fix them. **Perez et al.** (2022) trained a Gopher red LM with A2C against a frozen Dialogue-Prompted Gopher, with reward minus log(1 minus r) from a 1.4B offensiveness classifier and a KL penalty; RL elicits offensive replies **over 40%** of the time at KL weight 0.3 but **78%** of its prompts contain the word "invisible" ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2202.03286)) [perez2022red]. The paper's discussion names "jointly train red and target LMs in a GAN-like setup" as future work, which is the sketch's ancestor. **Casper et al.** (2023) built the classifier from human labels first and then exploited it with PPO; without an intra-batch diversity term prompts collapsed to "would/you" repetitions with 61 of 100 identical, and classifiers trained on CREAK or ChatGPT labels were hacked by toxic nonsense ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.09442v3)) [casper2023explore].

**Curiosity-driven red teaming** (Hong, ICLR 2024) adds Self-BLEU and embedding novelty bonuses to PPO because the plain RL baseline "converges to a deterministic policy"; it found 196 prompts eliciting toxic replies from LLaMA2-7b-chat where 16 humans writing 100 cases found none ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.19464)) [hong2024curiosity]. **GFlowNet red-teaming** (Lee, ICLR 2025) samples prompts proportionally to toxicity times reference likelihood; in its comparison REINFORCE "collapses to deterministically generating a single reward-maximizing prompt" and PPO plus novelty produced zero effective prompts on safety-tuned targets, while a single LoRA round on the GFlowNet prompts gave the target most robust to other red-teamers ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.18540)) [lee2024gflownet]. **DiveR-CT** (Zhao, AAAI-25) turns safety and gibberish into Lagrangian constraints and shows RL and CRT attackers lose ASR sharply on a held-out test classifier, a direct measure of reward over-optimisation ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.19026)) [zhao2024diverct].

**Beutel et al.** (OpenAI, 2024) decomposed red teaming into auto-generated goals with rule-based rewards and a multi-step attacker conditioned on its past attempts; vanilla RL scores ASR 0.96 with cosine diversity **0.02**, multi-step RL 0.86 with 0.15 ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.18693)) [beutel2024diverse]. Under a combined reward the attacker optimised the Moderation API and largely ignored the rule-based reward, padding inflated diversity, and the authors call grader exploitation "an open problem"; the attacker had to be a model "without safety guardrails" or it refused to red-team. The 2025 to 2026 GRPO attackers repeat the pattern: Jailbreak-R1 inserts a diversity warm-up stage before the jailbreak reward ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.00782)); RL-Hammer removes the KL term and notes attackers "reward-hack diversity objectives" ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.04885v1)); PISmith reports entropy collapsing before any success under extreme reward sparsity ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13026)); and a systematic study finds dense rewards and longer episodes are the main drivers of RL jailbreak success ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07032)) [mohammedalamen2026systematic]. **Adv-RM** (Bukharin, NVIDIA, 2025) is the reward-model-side analogue: an RLOO policy from Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct finds responses the reward model over-scores, about 1,000 pairs are added, and "after 2 rounds of adversarial training, our attack procedure is no longer successful" ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.06141)) [bukharin2025advrm].

The inference the notes draw is that explicit diversity bonuses are themselves gameable, which is why the 2025 to 2026 co-training papers drop them in favour of a changing opponent: Self-RedTeam has no diversity term and gets +17.80% SBERT diversity from the moving defender ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.07468)), GPT-Red gets diversity from a defender population, and Active Attacks gets it from periodic victim updates. For the sketch read as an attack or red-teaming tool, this is the strongest argument in favour: the alternation is what keeps the harm policy from collapsing.

## Harmful twins of the same base: static twins are informative, RL twins are rare

If the sketch's harm policy is read as a response-generating harmful twin, the relevant prior work is the tamper-resistance and contrast literature, and the notes' answer is that no paper there trains the twin with RL against the aligned model. **Token Buncher** (Feng, ACM CCS 2026) shows that RL-based harmful fine-tuning with a harm reward (GRPO, also PPO, RLOO, REINFORCE++) beats harmful SFT at every learning rate, with Qwen2.5-3B HarmBench **45.75% versus 38.00%** and a 25.2% smaller capability drop, but the twin is rewarded for harm, not for making the aligned model fail ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.20697)) [feng2025tokenbuncher]. **Emulated Disalignment** (Zhou, ACL 2024) proves that contrasting a base model against its aligned version at decoding time emulates KL-regularised RL against a negated safety reward, raising harmful rates from 21.3% to 37.0% on Llama-2 without training anything ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.12343)) [zhou2024emulated]. **Weak-to-strong jailbreaking** (Zhao, ICML 2025) fine-tunes a 7B twin on 100 adversarial examples and uses it to steer a 70B model to **99.2% to 100%** AdvBench ASR; even a 1.3B pruned twin reaches 74.0% ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.17256)) [zhao2024weaktostrong]. On the defence side, **DR-SFT** (An, 2026) is the first training loss that uses a same-base misaligned reference's token distribution as a negative teacher, cutting embedded-attack ASR to 1.3 to 2.9% on Llama-2; the reference logits are precomputed, so the twin is static ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.30263)) [an2026drsft].

**AntiDote** (Sanyal, 2025) is the one tamper-resistance paper that alternates ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.08000v1)) [sanyal2025antidote]. A hypernetwork reads the defender's activations and emits a malicious LoRA patch; adversary and defender both use DPO objectives in an interleaved k:k schedule, with the authors stating "a static adversary would quickly become obsolete as the defender learns". The static-adversary ablation has a Harmful Score **3 to 5 times higher** (Llama-3.2-3B 33.1 versus 5.7; Gemma-3-27B 34.8 versus 9.9). This is the only quantitative evidence in the notes that a twin must re-adapt, and it reports only the end state, not the speed of re-adaptation; no code link or independent replication was found. **TAR** (Tamirisa, ICLR 2025) restarts a 64-step SFT attack from the current defended weights at each of 750 outer steps, so the adversary never persists ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.00761)) [tamirisa2024tar]; negated cross-entropy was rejected because it "can cause divergence", MMLU drops 10.6 points, and independent re-evaluation with 5 seeds and shuffling found each full-parameter configuration had a best run above 60% WMDP-Bio ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.07097)) [qi2024durability]. **MLAC** (Henderson, AIES 2023) is the 2022 ancestor on BERT-tiny, with a calibration step needed to stop a degenerate label-flipping solution ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2211.14946v2)) [henderson2022mlac].

Independent evaluations are unkind to the whole inner-loop family. **TamperBench** (Hossain, KDD 2026) sweeps 40 Optuna trials per attack-model pair and finds every one of 21 models admits an attack pushing StrongREJECT above 0.74 with utility preserved; "no defense in our suite withstands full-strength attacks" ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.06911)) [hossain2026tamperbench]. **Kuo et al.** (2026) defeat TAR and SEAM without any fine-tuning: abliteration pushes SEAM above 70% ASR on BeaverTails and above 90% on AdvBench, because the single refusal direction survives tamper-resistance training ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.26526v1)) [kuo2026openweight]. **Che et al.** (TMLR 2025) find adversarial fine-tuning matched or beat the best input-space attack on 64 of 65 models and that three principal components explain 89% of attack-success variance ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.05209)) [che2025tampering].

Why "same base" is informative at all comes from three mechanistic results. Refusal is mediated by a single residual-stream direction across 13 chat models up to 72B ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11717)) [arditi2024refusal]; alignment mostly changes the first few output tokens ([Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.05946)) [qi2024fewtokens]; and fine-tuning GPT-3.5 Turbo on 10 adversarial examples for under $0.20 removes refusal ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03693v1)) [qi2023finetuning]. The notes infer that because the aligned-versus-twin delta is low-rank and shallow, a same-base twin is close to a sufficient statistic for the aligned model's weight-space weaknesses, which is why static twins already work as attacks. The flip side is that a twin made by learning harm explores only the "learn harm" direction, whereas abliteration and prefilling exploit "elicit harm already present", which no twin in that line represents.

## Foundations: RL precedent and minimax theory both predict cycling

The RL literature already ran the sketch's loop, with policies instead of prompts, and every published run ends the same way. **Gleave et al.** (ICLR 2020) trained PPO adversaries against frozen self-play MuJoCo victims using under 3% of the victims' timesteps; Adv1's win rate against the victim fell from **87% to about 10%** after one round of victim fine-tuning, but a newly trained adversary won "at equal or greater rates" with a qualitatively different exploit, and a victim fine-tuned only against the adversary "catastrophically forgets" how to beat the normal opponent ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/1905.10615v3)) [gleave2020adversarial]. **Wu et al.** (USENIX Security 2021) found a retrained victim wins over 95% against the adversary but only 16.3% against the regular opponent, concluding that "simple adversarial training ... cannot help a victim agent obtain sufficient generalizability" ([Source](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/sec21-wu-xian.pdf)) [wu2021adversarial]. **Guo et al.** (ICML 2021) dropped the zero-sum assumption, which matters because a defender that also optimises helpfulness is not playing a zero-sum game ([Source](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/guo21b.html)) [guo2021adversarial].

**Wang et al.** (ICML 2023) beat superhuman KataGo with a cyclic adversary trained by victim-play at about 14% of the victim's compute, winning **97.3%** at 4,096 visits; KataGo's own adversarial training drove the published attack to 0 of 50 games, after which 1,155 further GPU-days of adversary fine-tuning recovered 47% ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2211.00241v4)) [wang2023go]. **Tseng et al.** (AAAI 2025) is the key iterated result: nine attacker-then-victim rounds, with each victim trained on 18% games against the frozen previous adversary. Each victim beats its predecessor adversary over 95% of the time at 256 visits, yet adversary a9 wins **59%** against victim v9 at 4,096 visits with 26% of its compute and 42% at 65,536 visits, and fresh held-out adversaries win 81% and 91% at 512 visits with 5% and 9% of v9's compute ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.12843v3)) [tseng2025go]. Victims beat all previous adversaries and adversaries beat all previous victims; the authors say iterated training gives "partial protection", the attack method "is always able to find a successful attack", and they do not claim the gap narrows. A vision-transformer victim inherits the weakness, so it is a property of the training distribution, not the architecture. The adversaries are deliberately not initialised from the victim, and the notes found no RL paper that isolates "adversary initialised from the victim's weights" as a controlled variable.

**Liu et al.** (ICLR 2024) supply the only convergence proof in this line: with the attacker on a fast timescale and the victim slow, average victim iterates converge to the least-exploitable policy in tabular settings (Theorem 5.3), while the Rock-Paper-Scissors appendix shows simultaneous and alternating gradient play and iterated best response all "fail to achieve low exploitability" ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.17342v3)) [liu2024rethinking]. Robust RL adds the population lesson: **Vinitsky et al.** (2020) note that "training a single RL agent against a single adversary" overfits ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.01825)) [vinitsky2020populations], **PAIRED** replaces win/loss with regret because pure minimax adversaries produce unsolvable tasks ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.02096)) [dennis2020paired], and **ATLA** (ICLR 2021) alternates a learned observation adversary with the agent and states no convergence guarantee for the alternation ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.08452)) [zhang2021atla]. RARL (2017) is the 2017 root of the zero-sum protagonist-adversary formulation ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.02702)) [pinto2017rarl].

Game theory frames the sketch as a population of size two with a last-iterate meta-solver. **PSRO** (Lanctot, NIPS 2017) observes that independent RL "can overfit to the other agents' policies" and alternates a meta-strategy over a population with a new best response; the mixture converges, the newest policy does not ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.00832)) [lanctot2017psro]. **Balduzzi et al.** (ICML 2019) show that in non-transitive games self-play against the latest agent "can exhibit strategic cycles" ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.08106)) [balduzzi2019openended], and the **spinning-top** analysis (Czarnecki, 2020) says the population must be large enough to cover the non-transitive layer at the current skill level or training does not converge ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.09468)) [czarnecki2020spinning]. The Red Teaming Game paper reports exactly this geometry in its red-blue payoffs ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.00322v5)). Bansal et al.'s self-play victims sampled opponents from a window of past versions and were still exploited by Gleave's adversaries ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.03748)) [bansal2018emergent]. Neural fictitious self-play makes the point that the converging object is the time-average policy, not the last best response ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.01121)) [heinrich2016nfsp].

The minimax optimisation literature is unambiguous that naive alternation is not a convergent procedure. The GAN paper's uniqueness result holds in function space and says nothing about parameter-space dynamics ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1406.2661)) [goodfellow2014gan]; **Mescheder et al.** (ICML 2018) give a prototypical counterexample where unregularised GAN training "is not always convergent" ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.04406)) [mescheder2018which]; **Daskalakis et al.** address "limit cycling behavior" with optimistic mirror descent ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.00141)) [daskalakis2018optimism]; and the **two time-scale update rule** proves convergence to a local Nash equilibrium when the discriminator moves faster ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.08500)) [heusel2017ttur], the result Liu et al. transplant to adversarial policies. **Balcan et al.** (AISTATS 2023) prove that adversarial training as alternating best response "may not converge" even for a linear classifier while "a unique pure Nash equilibrium of the game exists and is provably robust" ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.12606)) [balcan2023nash]. **Lee et al.** (ICML 2024 Spotlight) show alternating gradient descent-ascent is provably faster than simultaneous updates in strongly-convex-strongly-concave problems but that on bilinear problems "both Sim-GDA and Alt-GDA fail to converge at all" ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10475)) [lee2024alternating]; a 2025 follow-up claims an O(1/T) rate for alternating GDA in constrained bilinear games, with the convergence measure UNVERIFIED ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.03855)) [nan2025altgda]. Two framing results matter for a defender whose performance is the only thing that counts: Madry's robust optimisation solves the inner maximisation to near-optimality before each outer step ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.06083)) [madry2018towards], and **Zuo et al.** (EMNLP 2021) argue the zero-sum formulation "treats the adversarial and the defending players equally, which is undesirable because only the defending player contributes to the generalization performance", proposing a Stackelberg formulation instead ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.04886)) [zuo2021stackelberg]. The notes add that once helpfulness enters the defender's objective the game is non-zero-sum, so the relevant guarantee class is last-iterate convergence in monotone games, and no LM paper has checked monotonicity.

## What the literature says will go wrong

**Non-convergence of alternating best response.** The theory above says plain alternation cycles on bilinear objectives ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10475)) and idealised exact best response cycles in a toy robustness game ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.12606)). The RL evidence says each round blocks the previous attacker and a fresh attacker wins again at a fraction of the defender's compute, for two rounds in MuJoCo ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/1905.10615v3)) and nine in Go ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.12843v3)). In LM loops the attacker's signal fades: MART's per-round attack violation runs 29.74%, 31.89%, 20.33%, 10.21% and the loop stops for lack of data ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.07689)); CHASE's attacker success falls 0.47 to 0.24 to 0.22 over three iterations ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.05523)); Adv-RM's attack fails after two rounds ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.06141)); AdvGRPO's v1 said the players reach equilibrium after 7 to 8 alternations, its v2 instead reports rewards that "fluctuate until converging after around 500 steps", and it reports that initialising both sides from scratch "causes the defender to dominate" ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09701v2)). GRPO adds its own instability: AdvGame calls it "pronounced" ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.20806v3)), AdvGRPO traces it to group normalisation coupling advantage scale to a shifting reward distribution ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09701v2)), and CHASE sees near-zero gradients on confident defenders ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.05523)). The only direct oscillation observation in an LM loop is qualitative: the Red Teaming Game appendix reports "a relatively slow and moderate adversarial oscillation" ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.00322v5)). AdvGame and ARLAS assert cycling as a risk without plotting it.

**Attacker mode collapse.** The record is long and consistent. Perez: 78% of RL prompts contain "invisible" ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2202.03286)). Ziegler: the PPO LM adversary "suffered from mode collapse" ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2205.01663)). Casper: 61 of 100 identical prompts without a diversity term ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.09442v3)). GFlowNet paper: REINFORCE collapses to a single prompt and PPO plus novelty finds nothing ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.18540)). Beutel: vanilla RL cosine diversity 0.02 ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.18693)). Self-RedTeam attacker-only: one tactic covers 86.3% of attacks, 1.9 effective strategies versus 4.6 under self-play ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.07468v4)). TriPlay-RL: diversity score 0.588 to 0.004 without the closed loop and penalty ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.18292)). AdvGRPO: Self-BLEU 0.42 and token entropy 1.02, attacks converging on fictional thriller framings, and entropy regularisation that trades ASR 83.9 to 73.4 for entropy 1.02 to 1.60 ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09701v2)). Active Attacks: REINFORCE repeats one network-access prompt ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.21947)). GPT-Red: attacker-only training collapsed ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.26115)). ABS: LoRA collapse into repeated "!" tokens ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08427v1)). A same-base attacker adds a specific risk: safety-tuned attackers refuse to attack. AdvGame had to use an abliterated Llama ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.20806v3)), Self-RedTeam's Llama attacker refused about 25% of the time versus 0.1% for Qwen ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.07468v4)), MAGIC reports cold-start refusal by a base-model attacker ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.01539)), and Beutel's attacker had to lack guardrails ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.18693)).

**Judge gaming.** MART's reward model scored 4.8% violation where humans scored 8.0% ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.07689)). Beutel's attacker optimised the Moderation API and ignored the rule-based reward ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.18693)). AdvGame's pointwise judge raised WildJailbreak ASR from 8.5 to 30.8 ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.20806)). CHASE's attacker raised raw ASR 0.51 to 0.76 while convincingness fell 4.79 to 2.38, bypassing the defender by sanitising intent ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.05523v1)). DART had to re-annotate 10,000 samples because reward-model scores "were not credible" ([Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.03876v1)). DiveR-CT shows RL attackers' ASR drops sharply on a held-out classifier ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.19026)). TriPlay-RL warns evaluator error "may be amplified over iterations" ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.18292)). Outside the loops, two standard judges disagree enough to change rankings ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.02574)) [beyer2025evaluations], judges are injectable with success up to 73.8% ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.18333)) [maloyan2025judge], and StrongREJECT finds prior graders "significantly overstate jailbreak effectiveness" ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.10260v2)) [souly2024strongreject]. Swapping Self-RedTeam's WildGuard reward for Qwen3Guard improved 8 of 11 safety metrics, an average relative gain of 4.0%, so the reward judge alone moves the headline numbers ([Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.07468)). Active Attacks uses the same classifier as reward and evaluator ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.21947)). The notes found no paper that quantifies the gamed-judge rate inside an RL loop beyond MART's gap.

**Over-refusal and capability cost.** SFT-era loops report small costs: MART HelpEval 3 to 4% lower ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.07689)); Rainbow Teaming helpfulness down 0.5% ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.16822v3)). RL loops without an explicit utility reward pay more: APL XSTest over-refusal **20.00 to 40.89** ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.24369)); AdvGRPO WildJailbreak benign compliance **99.2 to 72.8** in v1, a run that v2 replaced with one keeping 95.6 while its safety lead disappeared ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09701v2)); AdvGame Llama benign compliance **98.8 to 69.9** ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.20806)); CHASE XSTest **44.4%** and MT-Bench 7.42 to 5.50 ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.05523)); Safety Self-Play MMLU 79.5 to 72.6 ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.10589)); MAGIC-14B AlpacaEval-2 38.1 to 31.0 and XSTest compliance 0.936 to 0.888 ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.01539)); Self-RedTeam Llama AlpacaEval-2 24.74 to 21.41 ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.07468v4)); LLM-LAT XSTest 0.924 to 0.004 ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.07468v4)); R2D2 Harmless utility 42.5 ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.15589v3)); TAR MMLU minus 10.6 and MT-Bench 8.1 to 6.3 ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.00761)). The loops that hold utility do so with an explicit term: MAGIC's R_ref, TriPlay-RL's three-level reward, AgentSafeEvo's utility term, Self-RedTeam's 50:50 benign seeds plus SFT, AdvGRPO's 50% benign batches, CHASE's 1:1 Alpaca SFT. The cost is model-family dependent (Llama far worse than Qwen in Self-RedTeam and AdvGame), so one family's XSTest number is not sufficient. For comparison, Anthropic's classifier route costs 0.38 percentage points of production refusals ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.18837)).

**Robustness that does not transfer to held-out families.** Per-family held-out reporting only became standard in 2026. CHASE on JailbreakBench after one iteration: PAIR 0.705 to 0.565 (down 19.9%), TAP down 54.3%, AutoDAN down 74.9%, PAP down 53.9%, Translation up from 0.040 to 0.115 ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.05523v1)). MAGIC: GCG 43.90 to 11.25, PAIR 44.38 to 25.31, multi-turn X-Teaming only 94.9 to 85.4 ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.01539v2)). AdvGame: PAIR, TAP, GCG 45.0, 48.8, 61.6 to 7.2, 10.0, 25.3 ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.20806)). Rainbow Teaming re-run against its own fine-tuned model still reaches 39% ASR from 92% ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.16822v3)). About 30% of Active Attacks prompts still bypass a GFlowNet-tuned victim ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.21947)). Gemini's held-out Calendar tool kept TAP at 94.6% and the authors say unknown-attack protection "cannot be quantified" ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.14534v1)). Earlier work trained and tested on the same family: R2D2 on GCG, CAT on continuous perturbations, Self-RedTeam on WildJailbreak, which is also its benchmark ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18510)) [jiang2024wildteaming]. Multi-turn attacks are consistently the least reduced family, so a single-turn harm policy will overstate robustness. In Go, defences generalise backward to all earlier attackers but not forward to fresh ones ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.12843v3)); in Ziegler's classifier, attacks on the final model worked on earlier ones, meaning the loop found pre-existing weaknesses rather than removing them ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2205.01663)).

**Forgetting across rounds.** Gleave's single-trained victim "catastrophically forgets" the normal opponent; the dual-trained victim holds it to 57% wins ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/1905.10615v3)). Wu's retrained victim beats the regular opponent only 16.3% of the time ([Source](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/sec21-wu-xian.pdf)). ARLAS names "cyclic learning, where an agent forgets defenses against earlier attacks" and shows that without the attacker population ASR stays at 6.6 versus 6.3 baseline, though SPAG, an iterative baseline without a population, ties ARLAS at 5.4 ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.05442)). Safety Self-Play reports "a cycle of catastrophic forgetting" without its replay pool, GCG ASR 4.7 versus 1.7 ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.10589)). MAGIC says "new exploits resurface" ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.01539)). GPT-Red sees ASR dips when defenders patch an attack "before new strategies emerge" ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.26115)). The remedies in use are populations and replay (GRTS, ARLAS, Safety Self-Play, Active Attacks' global buffer, GPT-Red, PAD and MART mixing earlier rounds' data), KL anchors (GRTS 0.04, TriPlay-RL 0.01, AdvGRPO 0.1 for the attacker and 0.05 for the defender in v2 ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09701v2)), AdvGame 0.1, ARLAS 0.05, CHASE 0.05, Active Attacks 0.1; MAGIC sets its KL weight to 0 ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.01539))), EMA opponents (AdvGame), and frozen LoRA bases (CHASE). The notes observe that forgetting is reported as cyclic forgetting of earlier attack families, not as loss of general capability, and that forgetting of pre-existing RLHF safety behaviours during co-training is not measured anywhere. Any robustness bought by the loop is also subject to erasure by downstream fine-tuning ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03693v1)).

**What a credible evaluation now requires.** The 2026 bar, set by CHASE, MAGIC and AdvGame, is per-family held-out ASR including a multi-turn family, an over-refusal suite (XSTest with 250 safe prompts ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.01263)) [rottger2024xstest]; OR-Bench ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.20947)) [cui2025orbench]; WildJailbreak's adversarial-benign split), a judge different from the training reward (HarmBench's classifier ([Source](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/mazeika24a.html)) [mazeika2024harmbench], JailbreakBench's Llama-3-70B judge ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.01318)) [chao2024jailbreakbench], or StrongREJECT), and per-round curves. ACEA proposes decomposing attack potency and defence effectiveness per round by delivering attacks even when blocked ([Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.08256)) [shen2026acea]; MART (four rounds) and CHASE (three) are the LM loops in the notes that report per-round numbers for both attacker success and defender violation; Self-RedTeam and MAGIC give no per-round curves in the text the notes could retrieve.

## What did not work in the closest papers

This section collects what failed, was abandoned or was later broken in the papers closest to the harm twin. It draws on three notes that read ablations, appendices, limitations and version histories ([Source](research_notes/Adversarial%20policy%20alternating%20training/negatives_generator_games.md), [Source](research_notes/Adversarial%20policy%20alternating%20training/negatives_twins_unlikelihood.md), [Source](research_notes/Adversarial%20policy%20alternating%20training/negatives_cotraining_loops.md)). Each item is labelled as the notes label it. AUTHOR-REPORTED means the paper reports it about itself. INDEPENDENT means another group reports it. INDEPENDENT (own check) means arithmetic the notes did on a paper's own tables, which is not a published re-evaluation. VERSION DIFFERENCE means a result changed between arXiv versions. "None found" in the notes means none turned up in their search, not that none exists. Most co-training papers have attackers that write prompts, so their lessons carry over to schedule, base sharing and reinforcement learning (RL) dynamics, not to the content of the twin's responses.

**Likelihood-lowering collapse, and collapse that looks like success.** Pushing a likelihood down with no bound degenerates the model, and the degenerate output often scores as safe.
- Plain gradient ascent (GA) on a forget set peaks early and then collapses: on TOFU Forget01, GA "attain[s] their highest forget quality at the sixth gradient step" before declining drastically, and the outputs become gibberish. The stopping time "does not follow a discernible pattern". Negative preference optimisation (NPO) only slows the collapse (AUTHOR-REPORTED, §2.1 and §5.2) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.05868)) [zhang2024npo].
- In Yao et al., GA on harmful responses cut output diversity on harmful prompts from 0.787 to 0.118 on OPT-1.3B, with outputs "mostly just whitespaces or nonsensical strings" (AUTHOR-REPORTED, Table 3). The forget loss reached 60+ after about 200 batches while the model "still outputs harmful responses" (§3.2), and the authors accept nonsense as the success state (§6) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.10683)) [yao2023unlearning].
- TAR rejected a negated cross-entropy loss because it "can cause divergence" and the adversary "quickly recovers" (AUTHOR-REPORTED, §4.2). A variant that alternates retain and suppression steps never gave a usable model, with retain MMLU 23.3 to 24.4, which is chance (AUTHOR-REPORTED, App. F, Table 12) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.00761)) [tamirisa2024tar].
- ADPO names "nonsensical but non-toxic responses" as the reward-hacking route when the toxic likelihood is pushed too low (AUTHOR-REPORTED, §5.4) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.12900)) [kim2024adpo].
- Simko et al.'s defence works by degeneration: attacked outputs become "The, / / / / ...", which the simpler HarmBench judge scores as harmful (AUTHOR-REPORTED, App. A.2) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.11938)) [simko2025contrastive]. Token Buncher's Token Noiser protects by collapsing capability under attack: post-attack GSM8K falls from 89.0 to 68.8 (AUTHOR-REPORTED, App. G.2, Table 11) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.20697)) [feng2025tokenbuncher].
- Lowering one response's likelihood moves mass to others. Refusal training with direct preference optimisation (DPO) lowered Llama-3-8B-Instruct's refusal rate from 74.4% to 33.4% through likelihood displacement (AUTHOR-REPORTED) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.08847)) [razin2024unintentional].
- Lowered likelihood is not removal. After 4-bit quantization, unlearned models retain on average 83% of the intended forgotten knowledge, against 21% in full precision (INDEPENDENT, Table 1) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.16454)) [zhang2024quantization]. Benign relearning restores an NPO-unlearned model's forget-set judge score from 1 to 5.08, against 5.92 before unlearning (INDEPENDENT, Table 2) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.13356)) [hu2024jogging].

**Reward mis-specification and judge gaming, in both directions.** The twin games its reward, and the defender games its judge.
- In AdvPrefix, a low prefix loss does not mean harm: failure stays at about 90% across four victims even at low loss, and prefilling the exact prefix still ends in incomplete or unfaithful answers. Standard judges overestimate attack success rate (ASR) "from a 10% ground truth to nearly 40% on Gemma-2" (AUTHOR-REPORTED, §2 and §3.2, Figs. 2 and 3) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.10321)) [zhu2024advprefix].
- A likelihood reward is hackable by repetition. Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) on the likelihood-based reward in Hu et al. scored 30.6, 13.7 and 5.6 (Table 4), and "the model learns to simply repeat the expression" (AUTHOR-REPORTED, App. E). Base language models (LMs) were poor rewards: "none of the pretrained base models were good at assigning high likelihoods to plausible stories" (App. C) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.04363)) [hu2023amortizing].
- In Hallucination Self-Play, removing the validity gate gave a 0% hallucination rate at average reward 1.000: the generator wrote faithful answers the detector missed. With the gate on, it learned "trivial or meaningless response, or to refuse answering". The full system keeps 91 of 500 samples (AUTHOR-REPORTED, §3.2.2, Table 4; rates hand-judged on 10 samples) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.07993)) [yang2026hsp].
- In Pns, removing the wrong-answer constraint dropped in-distribution accuracy to 26.99, below the base model's 33.28 (AUTHOR-REPORTED, Table 2) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.03516)) [di2026pns].
- Token Buncher's harmful-RL attacker itself reward-hacked: "the model may learn to generate responses that the reward model scores highly but are judged as non-dangerous by humans" (AUTHOR-REPORTED, App. F) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.20697)). Twisted sequential Monte Carlo (SMC)'s sharpened toxic target produced a child's story flagged for words like "suck", "hurt" and "cry" (AUTHOR-REPORTED, App. H.1) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.17546)) [zhao2024twisted].
- Judges miss what the attacker found. Against Nemotron-70B, Llama-405B as judge gave 24% attack success where humans gave 83% (AUTHOR-REPORTED, Adv-RM §4.3) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.06141)) [bukharin2025advrm].
- On the defender side, the Red Teaming Game's blue team learned to open with "As an AI Language Model" and refuse, and a stop-word filter against it failed (AUTHOR-REPORTED, v5 App. B.2.3) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.00322v5)) [ma2023redteaminggame]. In AdvGame a pointwise judge gave WildJailbreak (WJB) ASR 30.8 against 8.5 for the pairwise judge, and a 7B judge gave 29.2 with higher training reward (AUTHOR-REPORTED, v3 Table 5 and Fig. 5) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.20806v3)) [paulus2025advgame]. TriPlay-RL's co-trained judge is only 56.2% (4B) and 64.3% (8B) accurate (AUTHOR-REPORTED, Sec. 5.3) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.18292)) [tan2026triplay].
- APL's iterated attacker drifts in meaning: its best rewrites are often statements, not requests (INDEPENDENT (own check), App. C, Table 5) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.24369)) [wang2025apl].

**Twin degeneration: short, repetitive, refusing or trivial outputs, dropped harm modes, sparse signal.**
- Yang et al. found "the language model prefers short sentences" and needed length normalisation of the LM loss and a fixed output length to "avoid generating collapsed overly short outputs" (AUTHOR-REPORTED, §3) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.11749)) [yang2018lmdiscriminator]. RapTB reports "prefix collapse" and "length bias" in language-model generative flow networks (GFlowNets), and "rich-get-richer collapse" from reward-prioritised replay (INDEPENDENT, §1) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00454)).
- In the GFlowNet red-teaming paper, REINFORCE "collapses to deterministically generating a single reward-maximizing prompt", and transfer toxicity on Gemma-2b-it was 11.57% without the maximum-likelihood stage against 85.16% with it (AUTHOR-REPORTED) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.18540)) [lee2024gflownet].
- Rare harm gives no signal at first. With the base model as proposal, twisted SMC "generally fails to draw any samples meeting the threshold", and twist learning gets "no learning signal until a thresholded positive sample is drawn" (AUTHOR-REPORTED, §7.1 and App. G.3). PPO is mode-seeking: its Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence KL(q||sigma) is 0.98 but KL(sigma||q) 1.32, against 1.11 and 1.07 for contrastive twist learning (AUTHOR-REPORTED, Table 2) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.17546)).
- Seeds are load-bearing. Hu et al.'s arithmetic task with no seed rationales scored 22.6, 5.8 and 3.4 against 95.2, 75.4 and 40.7 with 50 seeds (AUTHOR-REPORTED, Table E.3) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.04363)).
- A generator only explores what its reward can express. APRM's generator never produced theorem-precondition errors, so the verifier never learned them (AUTHOR-REPORTED, §3.3) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.22888)) [juneja2025aprm].
- Self-ReSET's stream guard missed 904 of 2,935 unsafe prefixes against GPT-4o labels (AUTHOR-REPORTED, App. C.2, Table 3) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08936)) [zhang2026selfreset].

**Safety leaking into a same-base attacker, and contested evidence on sharing weights.**
- In Self-RedTeam's shared-weight policy, the attacker refused 25.27% of 4,527 sampled attacks on Llama-3.1-8B-IT, 7.21% on the abliterated model and under 0.11% on Qwen: "strong base model safety alignment can 'leak' into the attacker role through parameter sharing" (AUTHOR-REPORTED, v4 App. E.2, Table 20) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.07468v4)) [liu2025selfredteam].
- AdvGame and MAGIC both replaced a refusing safety-tuned Llama attacker with an abliterated one (AUTHOR-REPORTED) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.20806v3), [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.01539)) [wen2026magic]. AdvGRPO's reasoning attackers "self-censor, or over-think", with the thinking reward near zero until about step 10 (AUTHOR-REPORTED, v2 Sec. 3.2) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09701v2)) [bullwinkel2026advgrpo].
- Keeping a harm mode in the main model's own weights raised ADPO's base toxicity from 4.7% to 13.3% (AUTHOR-REPORTED, Table 1) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.12900)). TriPlay-RL states that red training "is likely to degrade its safety-oriented behavior" and leaves it "not addressed" (AUTHOR-REPORTED, Limitations).
- Whether to share weights is contested. Anchored Bipolicy Self-Play (ABS) beats shared-weight Self-RedTeam at 3B but is worse at 14B (WildGuardTest 0.131 against 0.116, HarmBench 0.093 against 0.056), and it changes sharing and Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) at the same time (AUTHOR-REPORTED, Table 1) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08427v1)) [lamalfa2026abs]. Safety Self-Play finds separate models worse (GCG ASR 5.8 against 1.7) (AUTHOR-REPORTED, Table 4) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.10589)) [wang2026ssp]. MAGIC's "gradient conflict" claim is never ablated.

**Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) stalling on confident aligned models.**
- CHASE's aligned defender gives groups that are all-refuse or all-comply, so "both cases produce near-zero gradient signal". A GRPO-only defender refused 48% of adversarial rewrites, so CHASE added rejection-sampled supervised fine-tuning (SFT) (AUTHOR-REPORTED, Sec. 4.2.2) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.05523)) [markasserithodi2026chase].
- In AdvGame, GRPO gave WJB 22.3 against 8.5 for online DPO, and exponential moving average (EMA) opponents helped DPO (49.5 to 8.5) but hurt GRPO (22.3 to 55.6). The safest GRPO Llama defender kept only 45.6% benign compliance (AUTHOR-REPORTED, v3 Tables 5 and 7, Fig. 6) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.20806v3)).
- AdvGRPO v2 zeroes the advantage of groups with identical rewards, and without per-channel normalisation WJB is 30.4 plus or minus 14.9 (AUTHOR-REPORTED, v2 App. B and Table 5) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09701v2)).
- Reward density decides whether the twin learns. With a dense harm reward model and a KL coefficient of 1e-3, Token Buncher's attack broke alignment in "fewer than 100 Harmful-RL steps" (AUTHOR-REPORTED, §4.1) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.20697)).

**Training only against the latest opponent.**
- ARLAS without population-based learning (latest against latest) reached ASR 6.6 against 6.3 for the base and task success 23.5 against 24.4 (AUTHOR-REPORTED, Table 1). But SPAG, an iterative baseline without a population, ties ARLAS at ASR 5.4 (AUTHOR-REPORTED, Table 1) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.05442)) [wang2025arlas].
- AntiDote's static adversary raised Harmful Score from 5.7 to 33.1 on Llama-3.2-3B (AUTHOR-REPORTED, App. A.1, Table 4) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.08000v1)). Hallucination Self-Play with a frozen generator degraded the detector in round 2 from 83.3 to 73.6 (AUTHOR-REPORTED, §4.4, Table 2) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.07993)).
- One-sided games teach nothing. In SPC, round-2 data from a generator with only a 13.2% win rate dropped the critic from 76.8% to 72.0%; playing the generator against an older critic fixed it at 77.7% (AUTHOR-REPORTED, §4.3) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.19162)) [chen2025spc].
- GPT-Red found single-defender self-play "often resulted in attackers would mode collapse" (AUTHOR-REPORTED, Sec. 5.1, numbers not shown) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.26115)) [wallace2026gptred]. Active Attacks did not use the end of its own sequential victim chain and retrained the final victim on the global buffer to avoid "sequential bias" (AUTHOR-REPORTED, Sec. 3.2) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.21947)) [yun2025activeattacks].

**Attackers specialising and decaying across rounds.**
- MAGIC's co-trained attacker is weaker than the untrained base attacker against MAGIC's own defender (9.00% against 10.50% ASR) but stronger against outside defenders (58.00 against 37.33 on Qwen-7B) (AUTHOR-REPORTED, App. E, Table 10) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.01539)).
- CHASE's attacker convincingness fell from 4.79 to 2.83 to 2.38 and its success from 0.47 to 0.24 to 0.22 while raw ASR rose from 0.51 to 0.76 (AUTHOR-REPORTED, App. G, Table 6) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.05523)). MART's attack violation ran 29.74%, 31.89%, 20.33%, 10.21% and the loop stopped at round 4 for lack of data (AUTHOR-REPORTED, Table 4) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.07689)) [ge2023mart].
- Adv-RM's attack stopped working after two rounds, and more than about 1,000 adversarial samples "actually hurts performance" (AUTHOR-REPORTED, §3.4 and Fig. 6) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.06141)).
- Collapse is strategy-level. AdvGRPO's attacks "almost always" use a "fictional thriller or crime novel" framing; an entropy bonus at beta_max 0.1 cut ASR from 83.9 to 73.4, and at 0.15 entropy "explode[s]" (AUTHOR-REPORTED, v2 App. C) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09701v2)). TriPlay-RL's output diversity is 0.588 with the closed loop, 0.156 without it and 0.004 without the loop and the penalty (AUTHOR-REPORTED, Table 2) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.18292)).
- No paper compares resetting the attacker each round (Active Attacks, the Red Teaming Game) with continuing it (MAGIC, CHASE, AdvGRPO).

**Over-refusal costs.**
- APL doubled XSTest refusals on Mistral-7B from 20.00 to 40.89, the worst of all methods (AUTHOR-REPORTED, Table 1) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.24369)).
- MAGIC's no-game ablation looks safer (WJB harm ASR 0.024 against 0.198) but its WJB benign compliance falls to 0.496 against 0.968: "a conservative 'refuse-by-default' heuristic" (AUTHOR-REPORTED, App. G, Table 12) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.01539)).
- AdvGRPO's v1 run reached WJB 7.5 with benign compliance 72.8; its v2 run of the same configuration reports WJB 31.7 with benign compliance 95.6 (VERSION DIFFERENCE, v1 and v2 Tables 3 and 4) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09701v1), [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09701v2)).
- CHASE's XSTest compliance is 44.4% overall and 8% on safe contexts, because the defender "classifies by framing features rather than user intent" (AUTHOR-REPORTED, Table 2) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.05523)). DuoGuard shows false positive rates of 75.2% on OffensEval and 43.5% on OR-Bench, measured at the threshold that gives 95% recall rather than DuoGuard's default (INDEPENDENT, Trust the Typical §5) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.04581)) [deng2025duoguard].
- Self-RedTeam retrained on Llama by AdvGame: WJB benign compliance 98.8 to 72.8 and AlpacaEval-2 29.9 to 13.1 (INDEPENDENT, AdvGame v3 Tables 1 to 4) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.20806v3)).

**Untested schedule choices.**
- Only AdvGRPO v2 ablates the alternation interval. Swapping every step against every 10 steps gives HarmBench 6.0 plus or minus 1.8 against 4.9 plus or minus 1.2, WJB 22.9 plus or minus 6.6 against 21.8 plus or minus 5.4, and DAN 28.2 plus or minus 5.9 against 22.9 plus or minus 5.8, over three seeds. Only the DAN gap exceeds about one standard deviation (AUTHOR-REPORTED, v2 Table 5) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09701v2)).
- AdvGame asserts that alternating optimisation "suffers from inefficiencies and potential instabilities" but runs no alternating-schedule ablation (AUTHOR-REPORTED, v3 Sec. 1) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.20806v3)). MAGIC (switch every 15 steps, 1:1), TriPlay-RL (200:50:50 steps) and Active Attacks (victim update every 1,000 steps) fix their schedules without a sweep, and Active Attacks gives 4 and 5 as its default round count in different places (AUTHOR-REPORTED).
- Token Buncher avoided a weighted sum of its two losses as "sensitive" and avoided long-cycle alternation because it "could lead to catastrophic forgetting", choosing a step-by-step interleave (AUTHOR-REPORTED, §5.6) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.20697)).
- GFlowNet-EM needed a gated M-step: "without it, the generative model tends to collapse", and a component that worked against a fixed reward "performs much worse" once the reward moves (AUTHOR-REPORTED, App. C.7) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.06576)) [hu2023gflownetem].
- Papers disagree on restarting the learner each round (DuoGuard, Adv-RM retrain from scratch; SPC from round-0 checkpoints) against continuing (Hallucination Self-Play). No paper tests the two against each other.

**Robustness that later re-evaluation broke.**
- Qi et al. turned on data shuffling for RepNoise and found the worst of five seeds "almost the same as" the undefended model. For TAR, the best of five seeds recovered more than 60% WMDP-Bio for all three original configurations, warmup plus cosine decay "can always largely recover" it, and fine-tuning on TAR's own retain set recovered it. TAR-Bio-v2 also scores GSM8K 4.8 against 74.4 (INDEPENDENT, §3 to §4, Tables 2 and 3) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.07097)) [qi2024durability]. Che et al. call TAR's robustness "unremarkable" given its utility (INDEPENDENT, §4, Table 2) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.05209)) [che2025tampering].
- AdvGRPO's v1 claim that its defenders "achieve the lowest ASR (highest safety) on all benchmarks" became, in v2, "AdvGame performs better on WildJailbreak and DAN" (VERSION DIFFERENCE, Sec. 5.2) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09701v2)).
- AdvGame's re-run of Self-RedTeam on Llama dropped ArenaHard from 33.6 to 13.6, and adaptive attacks reached PAIR 37.2 and TAP 40.3 on Self-RedTeam's Qwen model against 7.2 and 10.0 for AdvGame (INDEPENDENT, v3 Tables 1 and 4) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.20806v3)).
- DART's reimplementation of MART at 7B cut human-judged violation by 41.7% relative, against about 59.5% self-reported, on a different base (INDEPENDENT, DART v1 Table 1) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.03876v1)) [jiang2024dart].
- APRM's Gemma-3-27B average gap (85.2 against 72.6) cannot be reproduced by any shared weighting of its own per-benchmark cells (INDEPENDENT (own check), Table 5) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.22888)).
- The only independent test of a GFlowNet negative sampler inside an iterated preference loop, alpha-SPIN, "degrades starting from Iteration 1" (INDEPENDENT, App. A) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.04291)).

**Where the notes disagree.**
- *Length normalisation.* The generator-games note recommends length-normalising the likelihood reward, because Yang et al. needed it to stop short outputs ([Source](research_notes/Adversarial%20policy%20alternating%20training/negatives_generator_games.md)). The derivation check says length normalisation breaks the variational identity: there is no proper optimum short of the length cap, and the twin's value is no longer log P(H) ([Source](research_notes/Adversarial%20policy%20alternating%20training/coupling_2_mechanics.md)). Summed log-probs keep the identity and keep the twin's value interpretable as the log breach probability. Their cost is exposure to short, repetitive drift when the optimiser has not reached the optimum or the effective entropy coefficient is below 1. Length normalisation curbs that drift but gives up the identity and pins the twin to the length cap. A middle route the derivation check describes is summed log-probs with forced minimum and maximum lengths and a forced terminator, as in Hu et al.'s GFlowNet sampler.
- *Smooth or thresholded harm.* The twins-unlikelihood note recommends a smooth harm probability rather than a threshold, to escape the cold start. The derivation check says a soft judge makes the twin target a randomised-judge breach rate and lets "barely harmful but very likely" text dominate when true harm is rare, and recommends an indicator or thresholded judge. A smoothed log(J + epsilon) needs epsilon far below the breach rate. Smoothing buys gradient signal early at the price of a target that over-weights borderline text.
- *Twisted SMC follow-up.* The twins-unlikelihood note lists Liu et al.'s multilevel twisted SMC as UNVERIFIED from a secondary source. The coupling-2 falsification pass read its method on arXiv (2608.21736).

**Design lessons for the likelihood-coupled twin.**

1. **Keep the harm term, and gate it with a judge validated on incomplete, unfaithful, trivial and refusal outputs.** Penalise trivial and refusal outputs explicitly, and keep the evaluation judge separate from the training judge. Supported by AdvPrefix, Hallucination Self-Play, Pns, Token Buncher App. F, twisted SMC App. H.1 and Adv-RM's judge gap.
2. **Use summed sequence log-probs, summed entropy at coefficient 1 and a fixed maximum length, with no extra KL to a fixed reference.** This keeps the twin's optimum at p conditioned on harm. Control short-output drift with forced lengths rather than normalisation, and accept the tension above. Supported by the derivation check, Korbak et al., Yang et al. and Hu et al.
3. **Use a bounded loss for the main step, with a retain or KL anchor.** Consider a hinge at the Seldonian threshold, since log P(H) never saturates. Stop on held-out harm and utility, never on the negative loss. Supported by NPO, Yao et al., TAR and ADPO.
4. **Debias the main step.** Draw fresh twin samples each round and weight them by self-normalised importance weights, because stale or unlikely samples cause the strongest squeezing and the persistent contrastive divergence (PCD) lag term. Supported by the derivation check, reweighted wake-sleep (RWS), Tieleman and Hinton, and Self-ReSET's first-in first-out buffer.
5. **Update the main model only after the twin has re-fitted it.** Give the twin more steps per main step, or gate the main step on the twin's loss. Supported by GFlowNet-EM, Tieleman and Hinton's small-learning-rate condition, and AntiDote's static-adversary ablation.
6. **Warm-start the twin and seed known harmful positives, in its own weights or adapter.** Supported by twisted SMC's cold start, Hu et al.'s seed ablation, Self-RedTeam's attacker refusals, AdvGame's and MAGIC's abliterated attackers, and ADPO's same-weights toxicity.
7. **Measure the twin's coverage at the strategy level and prefer mass-covering training.** PPO-style training is mode-seeking and drops harm modes. Supported by twisted SMC Table 2, AdvGRPO's framing collapse, TriPlay-RL and Self-RedTeam's 86.3% single-tactic attacker.
8. **Train the main model against a pool of past twins, and benchmark each round's twin against a frozen base twin.** Supported by ARLAS, Hallucination Self-Play's static generator, SPC's one-sided round and MAGIC Table 10, with the caveat that SPAG matched ARLAS without a pool.
9. **Mix the twin's negatives with a fixed pool of standard harmful data, and include deep prefixes and mid-response harm.** Supported by Simko et al.'s attack-class trade, Lyu et al.'s prefill regression, Self-ReSET's depth decay and the shallow-alignment result.
10. **Report the breach rate and over-refusal as a pair every round, with a benign anchor set and a checkpoint rule fixed in advance.** Supported by APL, MAGIC's no-game ablation, AdvGRPO v1 against v2, CHASE and AdvGame's GRPO Llama defender.
11. **Plan for GRPO groups with zero variance once the main model refuses confidently.** Handle tied groups and add an off-policy consolidation step. Supported by CHASE, AdvGame and AdvGRPO v2.
12. **Test durability with many attacker seeds, a stabilised optimiser, LoRA and full fine-tuning, and do not call the result unlearning.** Supported by Qi et al., the quantization and relearning results, and Che et al.

## Novelty analysis: open for the confirmed response-twin reading, occupied for prompt attackers

The sketch fixes four facts: the attacker is RL-trained, it is initialised from the same base as the main model, it is a separate set of weights, and the two are updated in strict alternation. It leaves open what the harm policy emits, how a breach is detected, which RL algorithm is used, what "base" means, and the alternation granularity. The comparison below takes the thirteen reference points the user named and states, for each, which axes coincide and which differ.

**Pinned analysis for the confirmed reading: the harm policy produces responses.**

*Scoped verdict.* For a **generative** main model and **harmful** responses, no paper found trains a separate policy from the same base with RL, rewards it by the main model's failure, and alternates its updates with the main model's. The verdict differs by coupling, because a response twin has to be wired to the main model somehow and each wiring has its own prior art. Two falsification searches covered all four couplings below ([Source](research_notes/Adversarial%20policy%20alternating%20training/gap_couplings_1_2.md), [Source](research_notes/Adversarial%20policy%20alternating%20training/gap_couplings_3_4.md)). Hits were counted only after their method sections were read.

| Coupling | How the twin is wired to the main model | Verdict | Closest prior work and the axis it misses |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Negatives | Twin writes harmful responses; main model is trained away from them | Not falsified for harm; the game exists for verifiers | APRM: same backbone, PPO, reward is the verifier's failure, alternates every 5 steps, but the main model is a process verifier and the bad outputs are wrong steps, not harm. Static same-base harmful negatives exist (ADPO, MACPO). |
| 2. Likelihood | Twin is rewarded by the main model's own likelihood of its harmful response | Not falsified; the reward exists as a selection rule | Lyu 2026 picks harmful continuation states by the current model's harmful-minus-refusal log-prob margin, but by search, with no generator policy. |
| 3. Prefix | Twin writes the start of a harmful response; main model continues and must recover | Not falsified; static precedents are well established | Self-ReSET refreshes prefixes from the main model's own failures; RECAP trains recovery after prefills from a separate frozen model. No learned prefix writer, and no RL-trained prefill attacker even as a standalone attack. |
| 4. Weights or activations | Twin acts on the main model's parameters or activations | Partially anticipated | AntiDote alternates a learned weight-patch adversary but trains it with DPO. Steering Beyond the Support alternates learned activation perturbations but never trains the language model's weights. |

*Coupling 1, negatives.* **APRM** is the strongest counterexample to the broad claim ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.22888)) [juneja2025aprm]. A separate generator on the same Llama-3.1-8B backbone writes wrong reasoning steps, is trained with Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), gets +1 when the process reward model accepts a wrong step, and alternates with that model every 5 steps. It misses on two axes: the main model is a verifier, not a generative policy, and the bad outputs are wrong, not harmful. **Adv-RM** runs the same game against a reward model with a different base ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.06141)) [bukharin2025advrm]. **DuoGuard** rewards a generator by a guard classifier's error over iterative rounds, but it writes inputs for the classifier, uses Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), and uses a different base ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05163)) [deng2025duoguard]. Static same-base harmful negatives already exist: **ADPO** uses a frozen toxic-control-token copy of the main model's own weights as the DPO rejected side ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.12900)) [kim2024adpo], and **MACPO** uses frozen negative agents made by supervised fine-tuning ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.07672)) [lyu2024macpo]. Generative-adversarial and self-play alignment never has a separate harm generator. In **SPIN** the opponent is the previous iterate of the main model ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.01335)) [chen2024spin]; in SPPO and related methods it is a stronger response, and in **Adversarial Preference Optimization** the adversary is the reward model rather than a harm policy ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.08045)) [cheng2023apo].

*Coupling 2, likelihood.* The reward shape exists without a learned generator. **Lyu et al. (2026)** score harmful continuation states by the current model's log-prob margin of harmful over refusal text and train refusal from the worst ones ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04168)) [lyu2026autoregressive]. Emulated Disalignment and decoding-time jailbreaks use the same quantity for steering, and continuous and latent adversarial training maximise a harmful target's likelihood with a gradient adversary. Replacing the search or gradient with an RL-trained twin is the unoccupied step. The focused pass for this coupling, now the chosen design, is in the subsection after this block.

*Coupling 3, prefix.* The first draft said this coupling had "no trained precedent". That was wrong about static precedents, which are well established. Qi et al. augment training with harmful-response prefixes followed by recovery ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.05946)) [qi2024fewtokens]. **DeRTa** trains refusal at any position after a harmful prefix ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.09121)) [yuan2024derta]. **Backtracking** trains a reset token ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.14586)) [zhang2024backtracking]. Course-Correction, DOOR, BSAFE and RLBF are further variants. Three recent papers make one piece adaptive. **Self-ReSET** cuts the main model's own unsafe rollouts at the first unsafe point and replays them from a refreshing buffer, so the prefix source adapts but the writer is the main model itself ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.08936)) [zhang2026selfreset]. **RECAP** ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.00938)) [peng2025recap] and **consistency training** ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05817)) [gautam2026consistency] train recovery after prefills written by a separate but frozen model. The search found no RL-trained prefill attacker, even as a standalone attack; RL attackers such as LLMStinger write prompt-side text.

*Coupling 4, weights or activations.* **AntiDote** remains the closest: an alternating, learned weight-patch adversary trained with DPO rather than RL ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.08000v1)) [sanyal2025antidote]. **Steering Beyond the Support** alternates learned activation perturbations against a small steering network, but misses same base and RL and never trains the language model ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24535)) [chen2026steering]. **Adversarial Representation Engineering** alternates in representation space with a discriminator rather than a harm-rewarded policy ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13752)) [zhang2024are]. In **ReFAT** the adversary is computed from the current model rather than learned ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.20089)) [yu2024refat]. No RL-trained weight-space attacker co-trained with a defender was found.

*What the coupling decides.* Couplings 1 and 3 need a judge on the final response and inherit the judge-gaming record below. Coupling 2 is not judge-free: the twin needs a harm term, because the main model's likelihood alone makes the optimal twin the main model itself (see the coupling 2 subsection below). Likelihood rewards are also hackable by degenerate text, as Casper 2023's classifier hacking and TAR's divergence show. Coupling 4 inherits the tamper-resistance evaluation record, in which every published defence has since been broken. Coupling 3 is the least crowded: it has the richest static baselines to beat and no learned adversary at all.

*Search scope.* The falsification pass used web search and arXiv pages from 2020 to October 2026. It did not cover OpenReview-only or workshop papers, non-English venues, or patents. Five coupling-3 and coupling-4 papers were read at abstract level only. The arXiv ID of Steering Beyond the Support was not confirmed against the arXiv API.

**Chosen design: coupling 2, the likelihood-rewarded twin.**

The user has chosen coupling 2. A harm twin starts from the same base as the main language model (LM), writes responses to user prompts, and is trained with reinforcement learning (RL). Its reward is the main model's own log-likelihood of the twin's harmful response, plus a harm term. The main model is then updated to lower that likelihood on the twin's harmful outputs, for example with unlikelihood training, negative preference optimisation (NPO) or the rejected side of direct preference optimisation (DPO). The two updates alternate. Two notes check this design: a focused falsification pass ([Source](research_notes/Adversarial%20policy%20alternating%20training/gap_coupling_2_focused.md)) and a derivation check ([Source](research_notes/Adversarial%20policy%20alternating%20training/coupling_2_mechanics.md)).

*Verdict and scope.* The pass tried to break the claim that no published paper trains such a twin and alternates it with a generative main model. It used six axes: (a) a separate learned policy; (b) the same base; (c) RL; (d) the policy writes harmful responses; (e) its reward is the main model's likelihood of its output; (f) it alternates with updates to a generative main model. The verdict is **not falsified on the full conjunction, but partially anticipated**, more strongly than the first pass showed. The search covered 2016 to October 2026 through web search and arXiv pages, with method sections read for the closest hits. It did not cover OpenReview-only or workshop papers, non-English venues or patents. Four papers (PDPS, Self-ReSET, RULE and an adversarial unlearning paper for diffusion LMs) were read at abstract level only.

*Closest finds, and the axis each misses.*

- **Amortizing intractable inference in large language models** (Hu et al., ICLR 2024, arXiv 2310.04363) is the strongest anticipation of the mechanics ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.04363)) [hu2023amortizing]. A Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) copy of the LM is trained as a generative flow network (GFlowNet) sampler with reward "R(Z) = p_LM(XZY)", which is the main LM's likelihood of the sampler's output. An expectation-maximisation (EM) style M-step then fine-tunes the LM on the sampler's samples. It misses harm (d). Its M-step also has the opposite sign: it raises the LM's likelihood of the samples instead of lowering it.
- **GFlowNet-EM** (Hu et al., ICML 2023, arXiv 2302.06576) trains a GFlowNet sampler whose reward comes from a generative model, and trains that model on the sampler's samples, in alternation, so the sampler's reward moves as the model changes ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.06576)) [hu2023gflownetem]. It is cooperative and not about harm, and the model is a grammar or a discrete autoencoder, not a deployed LM. An earlier task brief gave 2310.04363 as GFlowNet-EM; that ID is the amortised-inference paper above.
- **Language models as discriminators** (Yang et al., NeurIPS 2018, arXiv 1805.11749) is the earliest min-max over a likelihood ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.11749)) [yang2018lmdiscriminator]. A style-transfer generator minimises the negative log-likelihood (NLL) of its outputs under a target-style LM. In the "LM + adv" variant the LM is also trained to raise the loss on the generated sentences, and "Training consists of two steps alternatively." It misses same base (the LM is trained from scratch), RL (it uses a continuous relaxation) and harm, and the LM is a style discriminator, not a deployed policy. The authors found the variant "very unstable" and switched it off.
- **Twisted sequential Monte Carlo (SMC)** (Zhao et al., ICML 2024, arXiv 2404.17546) learns twist functions that give a proposal for the main model's own toxic outputs, with target proportional to p0 times the probability of a toxic label ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.17546)) [zhao2024twisted]. Its sampler learns exactly the twin's target distribution, but the main model is never trained (f), and the twists are learned by contrastive twist learning rather than standard RL (c). Its **adaptive multilevel follow-up** (Liu, 2026, arXiv 2608.21736) reaches jailbreak events scored by StrongREJECT through progressively rarer levels, but "Algorithm 1 updates only the twist parameters" and p0 stays fixed (c, f) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21736)) [liu2026multilevel].
- **AdvPrefix** (Zhu, NeurIPS 2025, arXiv 2412.10321) selects harmful response prefixes per victim by high prefilling attack success combined with low NLL under the victim ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.10321)) [zhu2024advprefix]. That is the twin's combined reward used as a filter. There is no learned policy (a, c) and the victim is never trained (f).
- **Rare event estimation via iterative unalignment** (Yang H., 2026, arXiv 2609.24969) uses a weight-perturbed copy of the base as the proposal and maximises an event surrogate minus a KL (Kullback-Leibler) penalty to the base, which is equivalent to rewarding log p plus entropy ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24969)) [yang2026unalignment]. It has the same base (b) but uses pathwise gradients, "not REINFORCE" (c), proxy events such as profanity (d), and the base "parameters remain fixed throughout" (f).
- **Hallucination Self-Play** (Yang S., 2026, arXiv 2607.07993) initialises a generator "from the same base model" as a detector, trains it with RL to write hard-to-detect hallucinated responses, and retrains the detector on them, iteratively ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07993)) [yang2026hsp]. It meets (a), (b), (c) and (f) with bad responses, but its reward is the detector's failure, not a likelihood (e), the outputs are hallucinated rather than harmful, and the main model is a detector.
- **Lyu et al. (2026)** pick harmful continuation states by the current model's harmful-minus-refusal log-prob margin and train refusal from them ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04168)) [lyu2026autoregressive]. That is a static precedent for the combined reward, with no generator policy and no alternation (a, c, f).

*The wording risk.* A twin reward of harm(y) plus beta times log p_main(y|x), with an entropy bonus of weight beta, equals E_q[harm] minus beta times KL(q || p_main). That is KL-regularised RL with the **current** main model as the reference policy. At beta = 1 it is identical to RL for harm with a KL penalty of coefficient 1 to the current main model. Its optimum is q* proportional to p_main(y|x) times exp(harm(y)/beta), the main model tilted toward harm, which is the same target that twisted SMC and rare-event proposals learn. So "the twin is rewarded by the main model's likelihood" is not new on its own, and a reviewer will make this equivalence. The claim should state it first. What is new is that the KL anchor is the live main model, that this model is pushed down on the twin's samples, and that the twin is re-fitted to it in alternation. The pass recommends presenting the twin as the adversarial, harm-restricted inversion of amortised-inference EM, and citing Yang 2018 as the earliest likelihood min-max, which was unstable.

The pass flagged one check it could not do: an RL attacker that keeps a KL penalty to the current defended model, inside an alternating defence loop, would be within one axis of the design. Two papers were checked in the session. **Token Buncher**'s defence is a single defender-side run with no attacker in the loop. Its harmful-RL attack uses Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with KL coefficient 1e-3, the paper does not say what the KL reference model is (Eq. 9 says only "a reference policy model"), and the attack is never re-run against an updated defender ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.20697)) [feng2025tokenbuncher]. **AntiDote**'s adversary trains on a fixed harmful dataset (BeaverTails plus do-not-answer) rather than sampling its own responses, and it uses DPO, so it misses the response-generating and RL axes ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.08000v1)) [sanyal2025antidote]. Which reference policy its DPO loss uses was not confirmed in the session, and the new notes do not answer it.

*The derivation, as checked.* The derivation check re-derived the design's maths with its exact assumptions. Fix a prompt x. Let p be the main model, H the set of responses the judge flags as harmful, P(H) the probability that p writes a response in H, and p_H the main model conditioned on H. The tool used everywhere is the Gibbs variational principle: for any score f, E_q[f] plus the entropy of q equals log Z_f minus KL(q || q*), where q* is proportional to exp(f). The best value is log Z_f, and any other q falls short by exactly KL(q || q*). The results, stated plainly:

1. **Likelihood alone gives q = p.** With f = log p, the optimum is the main model itself and the value is 0. The twin is then harmful only at the main model's own base rate. Hu et al. state the same fact: at temperature 1 "the sampler will trivially sample proportional to pLM" ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.04363)).
2. **With the harm term and coefficient 1, the optimum is p conditioned on harm.** With f = log p + log J and J the harm indicator, q* = p_H and the optimal value is log P(H), the log of the breach probability. Any other twin gives log P(H) minus KL(q || p_H), an evidence lower bound (ELBO). This is Korbak et al.'s "KL-regularised RL objective with beta = 1" with the current main model as the prior ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.11275)) [korbak2022rlkl], and p_H is the closest distribution to p that sits on H, as in the distributional approach to controlled generation ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.11635)) [khalifa2021gdc]. With a soft judge probability J the value is the log breach rate of a randomised judge, not of a threshold.
3. **The main step is unbiased only at the twin's optimum.** The gradient of log P(H) equals the expected gradient of log p under p_H, so lowering log p on fresh samples from p_H is unbiased gradient descent on log P(H). For any other twin with support in H, the expected step equals the gradient of log P(H) minus the gradient of KL(q || p_H). The main step therefore also pushes the main model's harmful conditional away from wherever the twin currently is. This is the extra term Tieleman and Hinton found in persistent contrastive divergence (PCD): "it moves the model's distribution away from R" ([Source](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~hinton/absps/fpcd.pdf)) [tieleman2009fastpcd]. It helps exploration, but it means the main model is minimising a lower bound on its own log breach probability, the configuration Geng et al. warn about: "By minimizing a lower bound, we run the risk of finding an optimum where the bound is loosest" ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.00929)) [geng2021bounds]. The fix is to reweight twin samples by self-normalised importance weights proportional to p(y) times the harm indicator over q(y), the reweighted wake-sleep (RWS) estimator ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1406.2751)) [bornschein2015rws]. For LMs these weights are exactly computable, because both sequence log-probs are available.
4. **Beta not equal to 1 gives a tempered optimum.** With entropy coefficient beta, q* is proportional to (pJ) to the power 1/beta. For beta below 1 the value falls below log P(H) and tends to the log-probability of the single most likely harmful string as beta goes to 0. For beta above 1 it rises above log P(H) and the twin spreads toward harmful text the main model finds improbable. The value equals the log breach probability only at beta = 1. Tempering only the harm term leaves the indicator case unchanged.
5. **A KL penalty to a fixed reference double-counts p.** Standard RL from human feedback (RLHF) practice has no entropy bonus and a KL penalty to the initial policy p_0. With reward log p_t + log J and coefficient 1, q* is proportional to p_0 times p_t times J, which at initialisation is p_0 squared times J. That sharpens the twin toward the most likely harmful strings, and the value is not log P(H). Reward log J with a KL penalty to the fixed p_0 gives p_0 conditioned on harm, so the twin measures the base model and does not track training. The two clean routes are the same objective: reward log p_t + log J with summed entropy at coefficient 1, or reward log J with KL(q || p_t) to the current main model at coefficient 1.
6. **Length normalisation, per-token entropy or an additive harm bonus break the identity.** A length-normalised reward (1/|y|) log p(y) has no proper optimum short of the length cap, because exp of the mean token log-prob does not decay with length while the entropy term rewards the growth in the number of strings. RL code that averages the entropy or KL penalty over tokens has an effective coefficient beta/|y| that changes with length. An additive bonus lambda times J gives value log(1 + (e^lambda minus 1) P(H)), which still moves the main model the right way, but the optimal twin is mostly harmless unless lambda exceeds about log(1/P(H)): for P(H) = 1e-4 that is lambda above 9.2. A smoothed log(J + epsilon) needs epsilon far below the breach rate, or the optimal twin is mostly p itself.
7. **The game minimises the prompt-averaged log breach probability, not the log of the deployment breach rate.** Averaged over prompts, the game minimises E_x[log P(H|x)]. By Jensen's inequality that is at most log E_x[P(H|x)], the deployment breach rate a Seldonian constraint is about. The gradients also differ: the averaged log weights all prompts equally in log space, so it spends capacity pushing already negligible breach probabilities further down.

The assumptions behind these results are: summed full-sequence log-probs, entropy and KL with a fixed maximum length; entropy coefficient exactly 1, or equivalently KL to the current main model at coefficient 1, with no extra KL to a fixed reference; the harm term entering as log J; a twin that can represent and reach p_H for every prompt, or else an amortisation gap, an RL optimisation gap and the alternation lag remain; deterministic token dynamics, which autoregressive generation satisfies; fresh samples from the current p_H, not replayed ones; and a judge that defines H under the chosen decoding. Every probability above is the probability that the judge flags a response, not true harm.

*This coupling is not judge-free.* The harm term is required: likelihood alone gives q = p (point 1). The twin's optimum, p times J normalised, also puts mass on judge false positives in proportion to p, so judge errors decide which benign high-probability text the main model is pushed off. An earlier version of the coupling comparison above said coupling 2 "can be judge-free on the twin's side"; that sentence has been corrected.

**Coupling 1, the negatives twin.**

In coupling 1 a harm twin from the same base is trained with RL to write harmful responses, and those responses are negatives for the main model: the DPO rejected side, unlikelihood, contrastive losses or NPO. The twin is re-trained against the current main model each round. A focused falsification pass checked it on six axes: (a) a separate learned generator; (b) the same base; (c) RL; (d) harmful responses; (e) negatives for a generative main model; (f) alternation against the current main model ([Source](research_notes/Adversarial%20policy%20alternating%20training/gap_coupling_1_focused.md)).

*Verdict and scope.* **Not falsified**, but partially anticipated, each time on a different missing axis. No paper found meets (c), (d), (e) and (f) together. Each two-of-three version exists: RL plus a generative main model with a static generator (Pns, for wrong answers); RL plus alternation against a verifier (APRM, SPC, Hallucination Self-Play); harm plus a generative main model with static negatives or ones made by supervised fine-tuning (ADPO, MACPO, DR-SFT). The pass ran about 40 searches and 20 full reads. It did not cover OpenReview-only or workshop papers, non-English venues or patents, read SPC, RLAC, ACE-Safety and AgentSafeEvo at abstract or snippet level, and could not crawl the forward citations of Pns and APRM (rate-limited). Those citations are the most likely place for a missed hit.

*Closest finds, and the axis each misses.*

- **Pns** ("Not All Negative Samples Are Equal", Di, 2026, arXiv 2602.03516) is the closest new find ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03516)) [di2026pns]. A dedicated negative generator is trained from Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct with GRPO, rewarded for well-formatted, plausible but wrong answers, and its outputs become DPO rejected responses for a generative policy. It is the static half of the design done with RL. It misses harm (wrong, not harmful) and alternation: the generator is trained once and no reward term involves the target.
- **SPC** (Chen, NeurIPS 2025, arXiv 2504.19162) fine-tunes a "sneaky generator" and a critic from the same base with zero-sum RL over rounds ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.19162)) [chen2025spc]. The main model is a critic, not a generative policy, and the bad outputs are wrong reasoning steps.
- **Hallucination Self-Play** (arXiv 2607.07993) has the same shape with hallucinated responses against a detector ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07993)) [yang2026hsp].
- **Simko et al.** (EMNLP 2025, arXiv 2506.11938) insert a learned attack module into the residual stream, train it by NLL on harmful responses, use its representations as hard negatives in a triplet loss, and re-fit it "periodically" as the model changes ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.11938)) [simko2025contrastive]. It is the closest safety instance of an adaptive negative source re-fitted against the current main model, but it is a module inside the model, not RL, and it produces activations, not responses.
- **Self-Steering Optimization (SSO)** (arXiv 2410.17131) optimises a data generator from the policy with offline preference losses and uses its chosen and rejected responses to train the policy ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.17131)) [xiang2024sso]. It misses RL, harm and clear iteration.
- **APL** (arXiv 2505.24369) initialises attacker and defender from the same reference, alternates them, and scores the attacker by the defender's own preference probability of harmful over safe responses ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.24369)) [wang2025apl]. Its attacker writes prompts and uses preference optimisation, not online RL, so it belongs with the prompt-side loops.

*Dependence classes.* If the twin's reward is only a judge's harm score, the twin does not depend on the main model at all, and alternation creates no game. The pass therefore classed every candidate by how the generator depends on the current main model:

| Class | How the generator depends on the main model | Status for a response generator |
|---|---|---|
| (i) none | static or independently trained negatives | **Occupied**: Pns (RL), ADPO, MACPO, DR-SFT, PT-ALIGN, RLCD, NEAT, anti-experts |
| (ii) weights | re-initialised from, or inserted into, the latest main-model weights | **Occupied but thin**: SPIN (the previous iterate, untrained), SSO, Simko. No RL-trained harm-writing twin re-initialised from the latest main model was found |
| (iii) novelty | a novelty or diversity term relative to past negatives | **Empty**: such terms exist only for prompt generators (curiosity-driven red teaming [hong2024curiosity], the two-player game 2406.10977, Active Attacks) |
| (iv) main-model score | the main model's margin, reward or likelihood of the response | **Occupied only against verifiers**: APRM, Adv-RM, DuoGuard, Hallucination Self-Play. APL uses it against a generative defender but with prompts. Against a generative main model with harmful responses this is coupling 2, and none was found |
| (v) main-model failure | the main model fails a downstream check | **Occupied only against verifiers and on the prompt side**: APRM, SPC, Adv-RM. A learned prefill attacker co-trained with the defender would be the response-side form; none was found |

The structural point is the main design risk for coupling 1. A twin rewarded only by a judge sits in class (i), which is crowded, and the "alternation" then adds nothing. Coupling 1 becomes a game only if a main-model term enters the twin's reward, which moves it into class (iv) or (v). Coupling 2 is class (iv) applied to a generative main model.

The rest of this section keeps the general analysis across both readings.

**Occupied cells.** Read as a prompt-emitting attacker rewarded by a judge, the sketch is the design of **TriPlay-RL**: separate instances of one Qwen3 base, GRPO, strict rotation, judge reward ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.18292)) [tan2026triplay]. TriPlay-RL adds a third co-trained Eval role and a multi-defender reward, which the sketch does not mention, but removing them does not create a new cell. **MAGIC** matches on RL, separate policies and strict alternation; its Appendix C.1 states that both roles start from the same instruct checkpoint, so it occupies the "same checkpoint" cell as well ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.01539)) [wen2026magic]. **AdvGRPO**'s 7B co-training run initialises both LoRAs on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and alternates every 10 steps, a fine-grained strict alternation with a GPT-4.1 judge ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09701v1)) [bullwinkel2026advgrpo]. **AgentSafeEvo** uses separate parameters from one Qwen3-8B with joint rather than alternating iterations ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.19422)). **The Red Teaming Game** matches on RL on both sides and a shared base family, with population-based rather than pairwise alternation ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.00322v5)) [ma2023redteaminggame]. **MART** matches on same checkpoint, separate weights and strict alternation, with a supervised attacker ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.07689)) [ge2023mart]; APL, DART, PAD and SEAS are the same cell with DPO or SFT attackers. **Perez 2022** matches on RL and same base (Gopher versus Gopher) with a frozen target and names the GAN-like joint version as future work ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2202.03286)) [perez2022red]. On this reading the sketch's contribution would have to lie in the open choices, not in the four fixed ones.

**Cells that differ on one axis and why the difference matters.** **Self-RedTeam** shares weights rather than initialisation; both MAGIC and ABS argue that sharing entangles the objectives, and ABS measures the cost (attacker strength 18% versus 35% at 3B) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08427v1)) [lamalfa2026abs]. The sketch's separate weights therefore sit on the side the 2026 critiques favour, but no paper reports that a separately updated same-base attacker finds more of the defender's own weaknesses than a different-base attacker; the stated reason for sharing a base is efficiency, and the notes found no controlled same-base versus different-base ablation with everything else fixed. **ABS** keeps the backbone frozen and trains adapters; whether the sketch updates full weights or adapters is unspecified, and ABS's 14B reversal suggests the answer interacts with scale. **CHASE** uses a different attacker model; it is the paper with per-round numbers over three iterations, so its curves are the natural baseline for any claim that same-base alternation converges faster or further ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.05523)) [markasserithodi2026chase]. **AdvGame** updates simultaneously and non-zero-sum, and its authors explicitly argue against alternation; it reports the strongest WildJailbreak numbers in AdvGRPO's own comparison table (8.5 versus 21.2) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09701v2)), so a strict-alternation scheme must either beat that or argue over-refusal (AdvGRPO XSTest 95.6 versus Self-RedTeam 82.8). That XSTest comparison is confounded: AdvGRPO copies its baselines from AdvGame but measures its own base Qwen2.5-7B at XSTest 95.6, where AdvGame's pipeline puts the same base at 82.4 (INDEPENDENT (own check) in the notes) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09701v2)). **Active Attacks** differs on the schedule in a way that is directly relevant: the attacker is reset to the reference each round rather than persisted ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.21947)) [yun2025activeattacks]. The sketch's persisted attacker is the choice no LM paper measures; the notes record that no paper reports steps-to-recover for a persisted attacker after a defender update, and AntiDote's static-versus-adaptive ablation (3 to 5 times higher Harmful Score) is the only quantitative evidence that persistence matters at all ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.08000v1)) [sanyal2025antidote]. **GPT-Red** differs on population and scale; its lesson that single-defender training narrowed attacks ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.26115)) [wallace2026gptred] is the industry restatement of the spinning-top result that a population of size two can cycle.

**Cells that are open.** First, the harmful-twins note states that a harm policy trained by RL with the reward "make the aligned model breach", where the twin's own outputs cause the defended model to fail, does not appear in the literature; the closest objects are prompt-generating attackers and AntiDote's DPO-trained weight-patch generator, and the note judges that an RL-trained weight- or activation-space harmful twin trained against the defender "would be novel relative to everything found". If the sketch's harm policy is a response policy rather than a prompt generator, this is the cell it occupies, and the comparison with TriPlay-RL and MAGIC no longer holds. The sketch as given does not settle this. Second, the attacker reward as a verifiable breach of a stated constraint, rather than a guard-model score, is occupied in agent and injection settings (ARLAS's leak reward, GPT-Red's validated failures, ACEA's canonical secrets) but not in the content-harm setting the self-play papers address; if the "stated constraint" is programmatically checkable the sketch sidesteps the judge-gaming results, and if it is judge-detected it inherits them. Third, **Tseng et al.**'s nine Go rounds are the only multi-round iterated result with a strong attacker; the notes found no 2025 to 2026 paper extending it, no LM loop beyond MART's four and CHASE's three rounds with per-round attacker and defender numbers, and no paper on whether the attacker's cost per round rises fast enough to make the loop converge ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.12843v3)) [tseng2025go].

**Open questions the notes identified**, each of which a study of the sketch could answer and none of which is answered in the catalogue: how fast a persisted same-base attacker re-adapts after each defender update, measured in steps to recover a given breach rate; whether round-to-round oscillation occurs in an LM loop, which is asserted by AdvGame and ARLAS and observed only qualitatively in the Red Teaming Game appendix; whether a same-base attacker finds different exploits from an independently initialised one, an open question in RL as well; the per-round count of novel breach families, which only TriPlay-RL's diversity score and Active Attacks' category distance approximate; whether pre-existing RLHF refusals are forgotten during co-training; which alternation granularity is best, since per-step, per-10-step, per-1,000-step and per-round schedules have never been compared; whether the defender should keep every earlier attacker in its training mixture, as the Go victims' backward generalisation suggests; whether the game's operator is monotone, which the last-iterate convergence results require once helpfulness makes the game non-zero-sum; and whether a constraint can be certified under the adversary's final or population-mixture distribution.

## Connections to this project

This project builds Seldonian-style breach-rate certificates for language-model policies: high-confidence upper bounds on a constraint's breach rate computed on a held-out safety set. The notes locate the sketch relative to that goal as follows. The Seldonian framework (Thomas et al., Science 2019) certifies a constraint with probability at least 1 minus delta on data drawn from a fixed distribution, which is exactly what a learned adversary changes ([Source](https://aisafety.cs.umass.edu/paper)) [thomas2019seldonian]. **HC-RLHF** (Chittepu, RLC 2025) brings that test to RLHF with a cost model, pessimistic optimisation and an upper-confidence safety test, but its cost distribution is fixed human-preference data and it uses no adversary ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.08266)) [chittepu2025hcrlhf]. **C3LLM** (Wang C., ICLR 2026) gives confidence-interval bounds on the probability of a catastrophic response in multi-turn conversation under three predefined sampling distributions, with certified lower bounds up to 70% for the worst model; the distribution is declared, not learned ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.03969)) [wang2026c3llm]. Safe RLHF ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12773v1)) [dai2024saferlhf] and constrained RLHF ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.04373v2)) [moskovitz2024constrained] alternate a Lagrange multiplier with the policy under a fixed cost distribution and give no confidence bound. On the adversary side, **ATLA** alternates a learned worst-case observation adversary with the agent ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.08452)) [zhang2021atla] and **Bossens** enforces a constraint under adversary-chosen dynamics in a robust constrained Markov decision process ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.11267)) [bossens2023robust], but neither certifies anything with a confidence level. SPIBB is the "knows what it knows" template for restricting policy updates to well-covered regions ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.06924)) [laroche2019spibb]. The LM game papers offer equilibrium guarantees only: Self-RedTeam's Nash argument is expectation-level, MAGIC's Theorem 3.2 is pointwise at a subgame-perfect equilibrium given a safe fallback, and GRTS claims approximate-Nash convergence; none is a finite-sample bound ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.01539v2)). The notes searched for a paper that certifies an LM safety constraint under a distribution produced by a learned adversary and found none. They also note the obstacle: the adversary's distribution is non-stationary across rounds, which is the same fact ARLAS calls cyclic forgetting, so any certificate would need to say which round's or which mixture's distribution it covers.

The derivation check for the chosen coupling 2 adds a certification finding ([Source](research_notes/Adversarial%20policy%20alternating%20training/coupling_2_mechanics.md)). The twin's objective value is an evidence lower bound, a **lower** bound on the log breach probability log P(H), and the main model's update widens the gap. That is the wrong direction for a Seldonian certificate, which needs a high-confidence **upper** bound. Markov's inequality on an unbiased estimate gives only a lower confidence bound, and upper bounds on log Z built from exact samples of p conditioned on harm need rejection sampling and hold only in expectation (twisted sequential Monte Carlo, Prop. 5.1) ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.17546)) [zhao2024twisted]. High-confidence off-policy evaluation's truncation argument also works only for lower bounds, because capping importance weights can only lower the estimate (Thomas et al., AAAI 2015) [thomas2015hcope]. A valid upper bound needs one of two constructions, both on fresh samples drawn after the twin is fixed and never used to train the main model. The first is importance sampling from a **whole-sequence** defensive mixture with p: draw the whole response from p with probability alpha and from the twin otherwise. The weight on a harmful response is then at most 1/alpha, so Hoeffding, empirical-Bernstein or betting bounds on [0, 1/alpha] are valid (Hesterberg 1995) [hesterberg1995defensive]. The second is a betting confidence sequence that uses E[w] = 1 to lower-bound the importance-weighted mass of non-harmful responses, which equals 1 minus P(H) and stays valid with unbounded weights ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.09540)) [karampatziakis2021confseq]. It still needs non-harmful samples, so it also needs a mixture with p. A **per-token** mixture, as in Angell et al., bounds the weights only by alpha^(-T), which is exponential in the response length T. Coverage, not variance, is what hurts: the sample size needed grows like exp(KL(p_H || q)), the forward Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence, so a twin that drops harmful modes can look good on its own objective and still need far too many samples ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.01437)) [chatterjee2018sample]. No source found does this for language models. Wu and Hilton's rare event is over inputs with greedy decoding and comes with no confidence bounds ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13211)) [wu2024rareoutputs]. Jones et al. 2025 forecast per-query elicitation probabilities with no importance sampling and no formal confidence intervals ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16797)) [jones2025forecasting]. Angell et al. build unsafe proposals by activation steering and give plain importance-sampling estimates with no confidence bounds beyond Clopper-Pearson on brute-force references ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.22167)) [angell2026tailrisks]. This is a statement about the search's scope, not a novelty verdict. The game's own target also differs from the certified quantity: it minimises the prompt-averaged log breach probability, which by Jensen's inequality sits below the log of the deployment breach rate.

## Conclusion

The field answered the sketch's four fixed design questions between 2023 and 2026, and the answers point one way: RL on both sides is standard, shared initialisation is standard and shared weights are discouraged, strict alternation is one of three live schedules and the one its competitors criticise most, and every loop that runs long enough reports the attacker losing signal unless diversity is forced from outside. The open ground is in the choices the sketch leaves unstated. A harm policy that acts rather than prompts, a breach reward that is checked rather than judged, a persisted attacker whose re-adaptation speed is measured, and per-round breach-rate curves that would show cycling if it exists are each absent from the catalogue. The Go results are the clearest warning about what those curves are likely to show: nine rounds of iterated training made each victim safe against the attacker it had seen and left it beaten by the next one.

For this project the sharpest implication is that a certificate and an adversary are complementary rather than redundant. The self-play papers prove things about the limit of training; a Seldonian test proves something about a finite held-out sample. Nobody has written down the statement that joins them, which is a bound on the breach rate under the adversary's distribution, together with an argument for why that moving distribution is the right one to certify against.
