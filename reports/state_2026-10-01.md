# Seldonian post-training of LLMs: where the work stands, and what measurement comes next

Written 2026-10-01, after spike 017's E8 addendum. Covers Rounds 1-6 (2026-08 to 2026-09-15,
`reports/paper_seldonian_llm.md` v0.3) and spikes 001-017 (2026-09-19 to 2026-10-01,
`.planning/spikes/MANIFEST.md`). Everything cited is on branch `llm-seldonian-rl`; the
findings are packaged in `.claude/skills/spike-findings-seldonian-fairness/`.

## 1. The one-paragraph version

The Seldonian machinery works on an LLM policy. With the right bound (Clopper-Pearson or
a betting bound for rates, never the t-test, which Rounds 1-4 used and 6.2 and spike 012
showed anti-conservative at the rates trained policies reach), every safety test,
trajectory certificate and stratified bound that was checked against a known truth held
its level. What limits the work is not the certificate but the quantity it
certifies: every constraint bottoms out in a label, and the labels are a judge's. Judges
are weak (a compiled rubric judge ranks responses well and labels them badly), they drift
with the policy (training against the label changes the form of the label's positives), and
their definitions are unsettled (the guard and the rubric judge disagree about a
refusal-prefixed answer). The method question is answered; the open question is
measurement, and it is a human-label question before it is a modelling one.

## 2. What is established

### 2.1 The certificate holds (Round 6 and the 6.2 re-evaluation, spikes 004-014)

- **Over-refusal, Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct, three seeds (Round 6):** unconstrained GRPO and a
  fixed penalty of 1 breached the threshold in 3 of 3 seeds, a penalty of 4 in 1 of 3; the
  Seldonian arm breached in 0 of 3, certified 2 of 3, and kept 82% of GRPO's reward gain.
- **Brevity (a constraint that breaches on demand), ten seeds:** 10 of 10 certified, 0
  breaches, solution rate at or above 0.74 at delta 0.05, with the multiplier floor on.
- **DiscrimEval parity:** certified at 0.003 against a 0.05 threshold with the paired
  betting-mixture bound; the probability feature collapses the paired gap 0.030 to 0.001.
- **Validity under pressure (synthetic environment, exact truth):** the delta claim held in
  every cell of the Round 4 sweeps and in 288 bandit cells of spike 013. Rerandomised and
  stratified splits never broke it, even against a candidate that overfits the balancing
  covariate (012).
- **Trajectory certificate (004-010):** a per-check test of the returned policy misses
  side-effect drift (65% of plain-Lagrangian runs passed it after a step inside the
  forbidden region); a delta/T certificate over every check held, misses 0.025-0.100
  against delta 0.1. Pricing the forbidden task from step 1 and letting the multiplier fall
  slowly (eta_down 10 against eta 100) cut entries into the region from 0.905 to 0.010.

### 2.2 Three gains that are usable now

| gain | what it is | size | where |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stratified safety set | 8 equal rank strata of an 8-sample reference rate, random ties, the stratified Wilson-type bound `b1w` | 1.4-5.3x effective safety samples on real Granite labels; 2.1-2.2x when the Lagrangian pushes the label (rho 0.92, no compression) | 013, 014; `013/preflight.py` (`--pushed`) |
| Routing rule for a judged constraint | code for verifiable properties; Clopper-Pearson on the labels below 10 in the rarer class; PPI++ on the judge's logit with a bootstrap-t limit above; design-weighted `b1w` on a stratified human sheet | held its level in every plasmode cell; the judge is worth 1/(1 - rho^2) labels, 1.9x for refusal at rho^2 0.48 | 017; `017/cert017.py` |
| Dual dynamics | multiplier floor, price the constraint from step 1, slow descent | 0 breaches on brevity; 0.010 forbidden-region entries | Round 6, 010 |

Label budgets for harm, from 017: 301 human labels on the certified policy's own responses
certify a 1% threshold with no positive observed, 149 certify 2%, 59 certify 5%.

### 2.3 What "the bound holds" means: a Seldonian certificate against a pure Lagrangian

Both pipelines run the same training loop: GRPO with `LagrangianReward`, a multiplier that
rises when the predicted constraint is violated and falls when it has slack. They differ in
what is claimed at the end and on what data.

**Pure Lagrangian (or a fixed penalty).** The constraint enters the reward. "Holding" means
the constraint's estimate, computed on samples from the candidate data the optimiser sees,
ended below the threshold. The theory is asymptotic and about a saddle point: at
convergence the averaged policy satisfies the constraint in expectation. Nothing is said
about the last iterate, about finite samples, or about the run you actually have. It always
returns a policy.

**Seldonian.** The same training produces candidates; the returned one is the best
checkpoint whose predicted test passes; then a safety set `D_s`, never used for training or
selection, is drawn once and an upper confidence bound at level delta is computed on it.
"Holding" means `P(g(returned policy) > threshold and a policy was returned) <= delta` over
the draw of the data, for any selection rule on the candidate side. If the bound is over the
threshold the method returns "no solution found" (NSF). The guarantee is on the output, not
on the training; the Lagrangian inside is only a heuristic for finding a certifiable
candidate, and the certificate does not depend on the dual converging.

What that difference looked like in this project:

| | pure Lagrangian / penalty "holds" | Seldonian certificate "holds" |
|---|---|---|
| measured on | the predicted test: candidate-side samples the optimiser and the selection saw | one draw of `D_s`, independent of training and selection |
| statement | the estimate ended below the threshold in this run | the (1 - delta) upper bound on the returned policy is below the threshold |
| selection bias | uncorrected: the best-looking checkpoint is optimistic (Round 6 seed 1: predicted 0.133, safety set 0.184; spike 014: predicted 0.141, safety set 0.168) | removed by the fresh draw; seed 1 returned NSF instead of a breaching policy |
| what checks it | the breach rate: penalty 1 breached 3 of 3 seeds, penalty 4 breached 1 of 3 while landing within 1-2 points of the threshold in every seed (0.151 / 0.182 / 0.183 against 0.178 / 0.192 / 0.172) | the miss rate against delta: 0 of 3 breaches at delta 0.1; plasmode misses 0.04-0.06 at delta 0.1 (013, 014); synthetic sweeps at or under delta in every cell |
| the level | the penalty has to be tuned to the pressure (Round 6: the frontier sits at 1/4-1/2 of the reward bonus, flat above; penalty 1 was "no penalty") | the threshold and delta are the specification; nothing is tuned to the pressure |
| the multiplier | its level is not evidence: it decays to zero once the constraint has slack and the policy drifts back over the cap (0.18-0.26 over by step 150 at bonus 16); the run about to breach is the quiet one (002) | irrelevant to the certificate; the floor exists to raise the solution rate, not the guarantee |
| the trajectory | an endpoint check misses drift: 65% of plain-Lagrangian runs passed a per-check test after a step inside the forbidden region (004-010) | a delta/T certificate over every check held (misses 0.025-0.100 against delta 0.1) |
| can refuse | no: a policy is always returned | yes: NSF (Round 6 over-refusal 1 of 3 seeds; brevity 0 of 10); the solution rate is the price and is reported |

What they share, and what neither does: both statements are about the label as the judge
defines it and about the population `D_s` was sampled from. A certificate is not a claim
about human-defined refusal, about other prompts, or about the policy after further
training (017 E6/E8). A method that always returns NSF is trivially Seldonian, so the
solution rate (10 of 10 on brevity, 2 of 3 on over-refusal) is part of the result, not a
footnote. And a fixed penalty of the right size can match the Seldonian arm's reward within
0.06 while breaching once in three: "it held in my run" is a statement about one draw.

### 2.4 What was ruled out, cleanly

- **TD-error "aha" signals** (001-003, 011): GRPO's normalised advantage discards magnitude;
  late spikes read the multiplier; a bonus on |TD error| inverts the constrained objective
  at beta 1; learning progress does not help a sparse context-conditional reward. Closed.
- **A compiled (prompted) judge as the measurement** (015): rubric artifacts and paraphrase
  flips; it ranks (rho^2 0.48 on the refusal logit, against the guard) and fails as a label;
  harm rubric rho^2 0.02. A verifiable property must compile to code (016).
- **Carrying a calibration** (017 E6, E8): not across prompt sources (recall differed in 4 of
  6 wordings; a carried bound missed in up to 94% of draws), not across prompt pools, and
  not across training that targets the label (recall 0.21 to 0.03; 80% miss). Only
  side-effect training on the same prompts left it intact.
- **Judge-assisted finite-sample bounds** never beat the labels alone at n <= 1,000; normal
  limits on PPI++ under-covered (0.08-0.24 misses); the smaller of two bounds is not a
  bound.
- **Small guards** (005-007): Qwen3Guard-0.6B's "unsafe" on encoded prompts means
  non-refusal (0 true positives in 135). The 4B and 8B guards, WildGuard, Llama Guard 3 and
  a Qwen3-8B rubric all sit at 18-23% false alarms on the user's 225-item sheet; recall is
  unmeasured because the sheet holds 3 positives.

## 3. What is not established, and why it is a measurement problem

1. **No human gold label exists for refusal**, and only 225 (3 positive) for harm. Every
   number above is relative to Qwen3Guard-4B's field. The paper's own section 7 named this
   the weakest point on 2026-09-13, and it still is.
2. **The refusal definition is open.** The constrained policy of spike 014 learned
   refusal-prefixed answers ("I cannot assist with that... [then the answer]"). The guard
   calls them refusals (93 of 500 sampled responses at step 200); the rubric judge calls 89
   of those 93 answers. Whether an over-refusal constraint should count them is a product
   question, and the certificate inherits whichever answer the labels encode.
3. **Harm positives are the scarce resource.** Bounding a judge's recall needs about 30
   human-labelled harmful responses (006); at a 1-2% base rate that is 1,500-3,000 random
   responses, or a targeted sheet whose weights must be carried (017: reading the
   stratified sheet as i.i.d. was wrong in 98% of draws for one wording).
4. **Two small policies, one card.** Qwen2.5-0.5B (rounds) and Granite-3.3-2B (spikes) on a 12 GB RTX 4070 Super;
   generation is the bottleneck (HF `generate`, 2-9 responses a second); 014's run ran 12.8
   GPU-hours against a 5-hour cap when step time tripled at the memory limit.

## 4. Measurement plan

Ordered by what each step unblocks. Nothing here needs a trained judge; spike 018 (a local
instruction-conditioned judge, 3-5 weeks, 20-60 GPU-hours) is deferred until step M4 says
it is needed.

| step | what | cost | unblocks |
|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | **Labelling guideline** for refusal (with the hybrid rule decided explicitly) and for harm (encoded and plain prompts; "disclaimer then real answer" is harm). One page each, with 10 worked examples drawn from the sheets. | 1 day of writing, the user's decisions | every sheet below |
| M2 | **Refusal sheet, double-labelled**: 200 responses from 014's step-0 and step-200 samples, hybrids over-sampled (design weights recorded as in 017), served through a Harm-Label-Desk-style page. | about 6 annotator-hours, 0 GPU | the first human refusal rate; rho^2 of the guard's logit and of the rubric judge against humans; the hybrid decision |
| M3 | **Guard logit as the feature.** Log the guard's logit for every scored response (not p to 5 decimals), and score PPI++ with the bootstrap-t limit on M2's labels. | 1 day, 0 GPU | if rho^2 >= 0.5 the guard halves the labels and 018 is moot; if < 0.3, 018 has a target |
| M4 | **Decide 018** by rule: run it only if the guard's rho^2 against humans is under 0.3 and refusal-type constraints will be certified often enough that halving labels each time pays for weeks of work. | 0 | the 3-5 week question |
| M5 | **Harm positives**: a 1,000-response sheet from the capability screen, stratified by the 4B guard's score with the weights carried, aiming at 30 or more human positives; double-labelled on the shared 40. | 15-20 annotator-hours | the first recall bound for a harm judge; the 301/149/59 budgets become budgets with a judge |
| M6 | **One human-certified policy**: the returned policy of a Round 6 over-refusal run, its safety set labelled by humans under M1, stratified (013) and routed (017). | 300-600 labels instead of 1,200 | the paper's missing section: a certificate in human terms |
| M7 | **Library, not spikes**: `ppipp_boot`, the routing rule, `b1w` strata and the pre-flight move from `.planning/spikes/` into `seldonian/llm/`, with the plasmodes as tests. | 2-3 days | anything run after this is on the shipped code |

Costs are the user's time and annotators'; GPU time is near zero until M6 (one safety-set
sampling pass, about an hour).

## 5. Integration with rent-my-gpu

What exists today (from a read-only survey of `~/rent-my-gpu` on 2026-10-01):

- RentMyGPU is a credit network: seeders (the owner's desktop app or `rentmygpu seeder`)
  serve GGUF models through llama-cpp-python, a Flask tracker queues single-prompt jobs
  by pull-dispatch, spenders pay per token. v1.4 shipped 2026-09-27; the product is in an
  invite-gated beta. No torch on the seeder, no batch job type, no fine-tuning, no
  labelling surface; a tamper-evident audit log and loop/memory guards are the safety
  features in production.
- Its spikes 037-068 already consume this repo as a dependency ("a moving, breaking
  dependency", their `CONVENTIONS.md`) to build a streaming stop-monitor certificate on
  the served model. Spike 062 ran it on the served model's own generations (1,829 from
  Qwen2.5-3B Q4, judged by Qwen3-14B GGUF, kappa 0.70 against human labels) and found the
  shipped certificate fails on that traffic: false-stop 0.50 against a claimed 0.40, and
  recertification returned "not enough positives" at 42. Spike 068 certified from 282
  elicited positives and found it does not transfer to natural harmful streams.

Those two failures are this report's section 3 seen from the product side: the
certificate is only as good as labels on the traffic being certified, positives are the
scarce resource, and a calibration measured on an elicited population does not carry to
a natural one (017 E6/E8 said the same thing with numbers). So the integration is not a
new idea; it is making the measurement plan serve both repos.

| step | what | depends on | what rent-my-gpu gets |
|---|---|---|---|
| I1 | **Pin a release.** M7 moves the routing rule, `b1w` strata, `ppipp_boot` and the pre-flight into `seldonian/llm/` with the plasmodes as tests, tagged; rent-my-gpu's spikes import the tag instead of the branch head. | M7 | a dependency that stops breaking |
| I2 | **Re-run 062's recertification through 017's routing rule.** 42 positives is above the 10-label floor, so the route is PPI++ on the judge's logit with the bootstrap-t limit, with the judge (kappa 0.70) as the variance reducer; otherwise Clopper-Pearson on the labels alone. Report the label budget the false-stop claim actually needs at the traffic's base rate. | I1, 062's data in `/mnt/d/rentmygpu/spike062/` | a certificate on real traffic, or an honest "not yet, needs N labels" |
| I3 | **Labels for credits.** The one thing neither repo has is a labelling surface, and RentMyGPU already has wallets, identities and a web app. A labelling task type paid in credits, with the quality controls 017 and the Harm Label Desk used (a shared 40-item set for agreement, double labelling, design weights recorded, the guideline from M1), is the "pay for good data that is not from an LLM" the paper asked for on 2026-09-13. Pilot it on 062's 1,829 generations and M2's refusal sheet before any real traffic. | M1, M2 as the pilot | human gold at a price per label instead of favours from friends |
| I4 | **A batch judge job type**, only if the single-prompt API becomes the bottleneck. The 4070 under the flock judges 8,000 responses in 16 minutes (014) and 20,400 in 33 (017); that is enough for the plan above. | nothing yet | later |

Constraints to respect from the product side: jobs can be end-to-end encrypted (pubkeys on
submit), so real traffic is labelled only with the spender's consent or on the operator's
own generations as 062 did; labellers who see harmful content need the guideline and an
opt-in; the GPU lock convention stays (one job at a time, `/tmp/claude-gpu.lock.info`
stamped), and a production seeder hold is never touched by a research job.

The decision that is the user's: whether labelling becomes a product feature (I3) or stays
a research tool (a page like the Harm Label Desk). I3 is the only step above that changes
what RentMyGPU is.

## 6. If the project is paused instead

Freeze `llm-seldonian-rl` as is (everything through commit 3f89e98 is pushed and backed up),
leave the skill and MANIFEST as the record, and close nothing: `td-error-wellbeing` is
already closed, `rerandomized-split` is complete, and `forbidden-task-unsafe-region` and
`prompted-ghat` are blocked on labels (M2-M6), not on results.
