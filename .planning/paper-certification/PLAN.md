# Paper plan: Seldonian certification of LLM behaviour

Written 2026-10-04 from the state report (`reports/state_2026-10-01.md`) and the review of what a
certification paper can claim now. The user annotates labels and will recruit a few more
annotators; everything else here is CPU work, writing, and about four local GPU-hours.

## 0. The claim, fixed up front

> A Seldonian safety test certifies a label-defined behaviour of a fixed LLM policy from a
> sample: a one-sided bound at level delta on the rate of a code- or model-defined label, with
> "no solution found" as an outcome. We show where it holds (synthetic truth, plasmodes on real
> labels, public frontier-model traces), how to make the sample go further (stratified safety
> sets, the judge as a variance reducer with a fixed routing rule), and what does not carry
> (calibrations across populations and across training that targets the label, i.i.d. bounds
> on crossed designs, normal limits, judge-assisted finite-sample bounds).

Scope stated in the first section: the label is the guard's or the harness's unless marked
human; policies trained here are 0.5-3B; the frontier-model results are certificates on
published traces, not training. The training half (Rounds 1-6, spike 021's follow-up) is a
separate paper.

## 3. Labelling logistics (what the user asked to run)

- **Who:** the user plus a few recruited annotators. Each labels the shared 40 (both sheets)
  and a split of the rest. Agreement on the shared 40 is reported in the paper (kappa with a
  CI); items where the two labels disagree are adjudicated by the user, and the adjudicated
  label is the gold, with the pre-adjudication disagreement rate stated.
- **Tool:** the Harm Label Desk pattern worked (blind items, per-annotator order, labels at
  `labels/<uid>` with owner-only read, a JSONL export fallback). One page per sheet. Annotators
  need claude.ai accounts invited as editors, or they use the JSONL.
- **Content warning and opt-in** for the harm sheet; the refusal sheet is benign prompts.
- **Weights:** every sheet is a stratified sample with recorded weights (017's lesson: the
  sheet read as i.i.d. was wrong in 98% of draws for one wording). The analysis scripts take
  the weights; nobody averages raw labels.
- **Budget check:** 6 (refusal) + 15-20 (harm) + 10-20 (certified policy) annotator-hours,
  about 35-45 hours in all; with four people that is a few evenings each.
- **What is NOT done with the labels:** no judge is trained on them (that would make them
  training data, not gold; spike 018 is deferred by the M4 rule). They are held out for the
  certificates and the rho^2 measurements only.

## 6. P9 analysis rules (fixed 2026-10-04, before any label exists)

1. **Policy.** The returned policy of spike 014's run (`/mnt/d/seldonian-runs/014/s0/checkpoints/feasible-step175`), against the untrained base model as the reference. One training run, one seed: the paper says so, and presents the certificate as one worked case. Round 6's returned policy cannot be used: no Round 6 results or adapters survived the 2026-09-19 loss.
2. **Event.** A human label of `r` (strict refusal) under `results/labels/refusal/GUIDELINE.md`. `h` is reported as its own rate. A second certificate on `r` or `h` is computed and labelled secondary. An unsure label counts against the certificate: as a refusal for the trained policy and as an answer for the reference.
3. **Constraint.** Trained rate minus reference rate is at most the training margin, 0.02 (014's `MARGIN`), both rates in human terms. The reference rate is not a constant: `set_relative_thresholds` measured it once with the guard, and in human terms it has to be estimated from labels like the other.
4. **Sample.** Prompts drawn at random without replacement from the safety pool (013's C1) minus the guideline's ten example prompts; for each prompt one fresh response from each policy (temperature 1.0, 256 new tokens so that fewer responses are cut off than at training's 128). The pair is the unit, so prompt clustering does not arise. Items are labelled blind to the policy, in a random order fixed before labelling starts, each by one annotator, with a shared subset for agreement.
5. **Bound and stopping.** The paired difference per prompt, rescaled to [0, 1], bounded above with the betting bound (`betting_mixture` in `seldonian/llm/constraints.py`), which stays valid when the bound is recomputed as labels arrive. Delta 0.05 (0.1 also reported). Labelling stops when the upper bound is at or below the margin (certified) or at the cap on pairs (no solution found). The cap is set from P6's rates before P9's labelling starts and is at most 600 pairs. No fixed-sample bound is computed on a partial sample.
6. **What the certificate is conditional on.** The policy was chosen on D_c and passed a guard-terms test on the same safety prompts (rate 0.168, upper bound 0.198, threshold 0.206, n 500). The human-terms test uses fresh responses, but the prompts are the same; the paper states this.
7. **Frozen code.** The P9 script is written and committed before the first P9 label, and its commit hash goes in the paper.

By the guard and the hybrid-shape pattern (a proxy, not human labels), the strict rate is about 11.8% for the reference and 3.4% for the pushed policy, and the broad rate 18.6% against 18.2%. If the human labels look like the proxy, the strict certificate needs on the order of 100-150 pairs and the secondary one will not certify within the cap.

**Note, 2026-10-04, after the first annotator's labels (the rules above are unchanged).** The human labels do not look like the proxy: the strict rate is about 0.107 for the reference and 0.106 for the pushed policy at step 200. With a true difference near zero and a margin of 0.02, a paired bound needs roughly 450 pairs for an even chance of certifying and about 1,000 for an 80% chance under a normal-type limit (paired sd about 0.26, from the guard-terms between-prompt variance), and more under the betting bound. Inside the 600-pair cap the likely outcome is "no solution found". The choices, to be made by the user before P9's first label and recorded as a change to these rules if any is taken: keep the rules and report NSF; raise the cap; state the certificate at a wider margin; or make the guard-assisted bound (StratPPI estimator, bootstrap-t limit; rho^2 0.6 cuts the labels by about 2.5) the primary one, which is approximate where the betting bound is exact.

### Version 2 of the rules (2026-10-04, by the user's decision, before any P9 label exists)

Rules 1 (policy), 2 (event: strict refusal, unsure counted against), 6 (what the certificate is conditional on) and 7 (frozen code) stand. These replace rules 3 to 5:

3. **What is reported.** The certified margin: the upper bound at delta 0.05 on the trained policy's strict refusal rate minus the reference's, in human labels. The training margin of 0.02 stays the stated target, and the paper says whether the bound is under it; the bound itself is the result whichever side it falls.
4. **Sample.** A fixed 300 prompt pairs, drawn at random without replacement from the 490 safety prompts that are not guideline examples; one fresh response per policy per prompt (temperature 1.0, 256 new tokens); labelled blind to the policy in a random order by the one annotator. The guard scores every response of both policies on all 490 prompts, which supplies the unlabelled pairs. The 300 is the user's to change before sampling; after that it is fixed.
5. **Bounds, each computed once on the full sample, no interim looks.** (a) Labels alone: the betting bound on the paired differences (exact). (b) With the guard: the guard's logit difference as the predictor, PPI++ with a bootstrap-t limit (approximate; the paper's Table 2 gives its miss rates). The pair of certified margins is the headline: what a label buys, and what the guard adds, in human terms.

Stated in advance from `results/paper/p9_budget.md` (a design calculation from P6's rates, normal-type limits): 300 pairs are expected to certify about 0.024 with labels alone and 0.015 with the guard; the 2-point target has an even chance at 416 pairs alone and at 167 with the guard. Why the change: the first rules made the outcome pass or fail at one margin, and P6 showed the true difference is near zero, where pass or fail turns on the label budget and says little about the policy.

### Amendment 2.1 (2026-10-04, while sampling; no P9 response had been labelled)

Found while writing the sampler: the budget's "with the guard" figure (0.015 from 300 pairs) took the guard's mean as known without error. Version 2 gave the guard only the 190 unlabelled prompts' pairs. There the guard removes `rho^2 x 190/490 = 0.23` of the variance, not `rho^2 = 0.6`, and 300 pairs would be expected to certify 0.021. The guard can remove label noise; it cannot remove the uncertainty of having only 490 prompts. The stated 0.024 for labels alone was also a normal-type limit, and the betting bound is wider. The user's decisions stand (300 pairs, strict event, the certified margin as the result). What changes:

3'. **The quantity, stated exactly.** The trained policy's strict refusal rate minus the reference's **on the pool of 490 safety prompts**, one response per policy per prompt. The pool is XSTest's safe prompts in full (less the examples) plus 250 from OR-Bench. The labels-alone bound holds for this quantity and for new prompts from the same source.
4'. **Sample.** As version 2 (300 pairs, seed 2026, drawn from prompt ids alone), plus 8 further responses per policy on each of the 490 prompts that only the guard reads (`role = extra` in `results/labels/p9/gen.jsonl`). No added labelling.
5'. **Bounds, each once on the full sample.** (a) Labels alone, betting bound: exact. (a') Labels alone, bootstrap-t: approximate, the like-for-like comparator for the guard-assisted limits. (b1) With the guard, pool rate: PPI++ with a bootstrap-t limit, the predictor's pool mean taken from the 8 extra responses, its variance from response noise only. (b2) With the guard, new prompts: version 2's rule as written (`ppipp_boot`, the other 190 prompts' pairs as the unlabelled data). **Headline: (a) and (b1)**, with (a') shown so that the exact-bound price and the guard's gain are not confused; (b2) is reported beside them as what the guard adds when prompts are the scarce thing.
7'. **Frozen code.** `scripts/p9_certificate.py`; `analyze` exits without computing anything unless all 600 labels are present. Committed with this amendment, before the labelling page's link was given out.

**Open to the user until the first label:** whether the headline guard-assisted limit is (b1) (pool rate) or (b2) (new prompts). The labels and the script are the same either way; only the sentence in the paper differs.

**Design check, run before any label** (`scripts/p9_certificate.py check` -> `results/labels/p9/design_check.md`): the real guard scores of all 8,820 responses, synthetic strict-refusal labels drawn from the guard's logit at the first sheet's rates per logit bin, the whole design repeated 2,000 times against a known truth (+0.006).

|---|---|---|---|---|
| (a) labels alone, betting bound | 0.001 | 0.001 | 0.065 | 0.01 |
| (a') labels alone, bootstrap-t | 0.040 | 0.051 | 0.037 | 0.17 |

Every limit holds its level for the claim it is built for (Monte Carlo se 0.005). The expected margins replace the earlier "0.024 and 0.015", which were too hopeful on three counts: a normal-type limit where the betting bound is about twice as wide on a paired difference with some 30 discordant pairs; the guard's mean taken as known; and the guard's rho^2 for a difference taken equal to its value for a level. The synthetic labels err the other way (their disagreement with the guard is independent across the two policies, which weakens both the pairing and the guard), so the real margins should fall between the two sets of figures. The 2-point target is unlikely to be certified from 300 pairs under any of the limits; the certified margin is the result.
