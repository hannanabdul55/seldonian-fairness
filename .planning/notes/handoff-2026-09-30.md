## Where work stopped: idea `prompted-ghat` (spikes 015-018)

`g_hat` from natural language. A four-researcher pass found the space largely unpublished:
compiling text into a *statistical* constraint, and certifying through a corrected proxy
label, both appear to be gaps. Jev (TypeSafe, released 2026-09-15) is a typed-decision API
whose Noul question is exactly this measurement, but it is proprietary and unmeasured on
safety judging, and on 2026-09-30 the user ruled out using it (no access, and no sending data
off the machine).

**015 is done (PARTIAL).** Full write-up in
`.planning/spikes/015-prompted-judge-fidelity/README.md`. The three results that constrain
everything after it:
- a **verifiable** constraint (brevity, "more than 80 words") must compile to *code*, not a
  judge: the compiled judge is at chance (AUC 0.500) and its aggregate rate matched the truth
  by coincidence on noise labels;
- paraphrases preserve the **ranking** (harm AUC 0.80-0.87) and destroy the **operating
  point** (measured rate 0.09-0.46), so a certificate must pin the exact wording and a
  calibrated threshold;
- the compiled judge **matches Qwen3Guard-4B** on harm (FA 0.216 vs 0.230, caught 2/3 vs
  1/3), and both are unusable raw at a 1.3% base rate.
- The **rubric-artifact test fired on refusal** (blank AUC 0.694), so spike 018's gate is open.
